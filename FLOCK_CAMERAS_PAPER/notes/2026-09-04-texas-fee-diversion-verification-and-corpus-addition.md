## 1. Texas fee-diversion story — verification pass

**Bottom line: upgraded from WebSearch-snippet confidence (09-03 note) to primary-source-anchored
confidence.** Every load-bearing figure below is now confirmed either by direct-fetch of the
investigative reporting itself or by direct-fetch of an official Texas state government document
(not just news coverage summarizing one).

### The mechanism, with sourcing tier for each claim

- **The fee and its stated purpose.** Senate Bill 224, 88th Texas Legislature (2023) — informally
  the "Deputy Darren Almendarez Act," named for a Harris County deputy shot and killed in March
  2022 while interrupting a catalytic-converter theft. Among other provisions (new Penal Code
  catalytic-converter-theft offense, possession presumptions, penalty enhancements), SB 224 raised
  the Motor Vehicle Crime Prevention Authority (MVCPA) fee on Texas auto-insurance policies from
  **$4 to $5 per vehicle per year**, effective for fees due on or after **May 29, 2023**.
  **Sourcing: direct-fetch, Texas Department of Insurance Commissioner's Bulletin B-0006-23**
  (https://www.tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/2023/B-0006-23.html) — an official state regulatory
  document, not a news summary. Quote: "SB 224 increases the fee from $4 to $5 per motor vehicle
  each year... effective for fees due on or after the effective date of the bill (May 29, 2023)."
- **No legislative discussion of ALPR/Flock in the bill itself.** **Sourcing: direct-fetch, the
  Texas Legislature's own official bill analysis PDF for SB 224**
  (https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/analysis/pdf/SB00224F.pdf) — confirms the bill is entirely
  about the MVCPA fee increase and catalytic-converter theft penalties; **no mention of automated
  license plate recognition, Flock, or license plate readers anywhere in the bill text or
  analysis.** This directly corroborates (rather than merely repeats) the Texas Tribune's
  characterization that the fee passed "unanimously in the House and Senate" with "no discussion
  of using the money for Flock cameras and similar devices."
- **The diversion itself: at least $30 million into 3,200+ Flock cameras statewide.**
  **Sourcing: direct-fetch, Texas Tribune** (the outlet that broke the story via investigation) —
  https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/28/texas-greg-abbott-flock-cameras-order-state-money/ — and
  independently corroborated by **direct-fetch, EFF Deeplinks** —
  https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/texas-and-florida-step-back-alprs. Tribune's own figures:
  "at least $30 million" funneled by the MVCPA into "at least 3,200 Flock cameras installed since
  2023," including a **$15.9 million, three-year TX DPS interagency contract** installing "nearly
  1,200" of those cameras. The MVCPA board is "mostly appointed by [Abbott]." Note on tier: the
  $30M/3,200-camera figures themselves rest on the Tribune's own reporting/public-records work
  (which I have not independently re-derived from a raw MVCPA equipment ledger — no such
  itemized-by-vendor public document was locatable), so this is "confirmed by direct-fetch of a
  credible, named investigative source," not "independently re-derived from a primary financial
  record by this session." That is a meaningfully higher tier than WebSearch-snippet confidence,
  but short of the very top tier.
- **The funding mechanism and its scale are independently corroborated by an official state
  document, though it doesn't name Flock specifically.** **Sourcing: direct-fetch/local-read,
  TxDMV's own FY2025 Chairman's Annual Report** (official document, signed Dec 9, 2025, addressed
  to Gov. Abbott) — https://www.txdmv.gov/sites/default/files/report-files/FY-2025_Chairmans_Annual_Report.pdf.
  Confirmed by direct read of the PDF (Appendices D-E): in FY2025 alone, MVCPA awarded **82
  catalytic-converter-theft-prevention grants totaling $13,270,784** to Texas cities/counties, plus
  **30 separate motor-vehicle-crime-prevention grants totaling $25,429,698** — real, itemized,
  government-published dollar figures at the scale the Tribune's cumulative "$30M since 2023"
  claim implies. This report lists grantee/dollar-amount pairs only, not equipment purchased, so it
  does not itself say "Flock" — it corroborates that money at this scale genuinely moves through
  this specific program, not that this specific report proves the Flock end-use.
- **Abbott's response.** **Sourcing: direct-fetch, Texas Tribune.** Not a written executive order —
  news coverage's "order" language describes a verbal directive: Abbott "gave the order Thursday
  night" (**August 27, 2026, going into August 28**), confirmed Friday, Aug 28, by spokesman Andrew
  Mahaleris: *"To the extent any funding comes from Texas agencies, those agencies are clarifying
  that those funds cannot be used for Flock cameras."* Scope is **narrow and prospective only**:
  blocks future state-agency spending, does not mandate removing any of the 3,200+ cameras already
  built, and leaves local, federal, and private funding untouched. Directive reportedly landed the
  same day the Tribune was about to publish its investigation. Also newly surfaced this pass: the
  Tribune article names **one specific named misconduct case** — a Lufkin, TX police officer facing
  ~100 counts of misusing public information for allegedly surveilling 11 people over a year using
  Flock cameras — and states "at least six police departments and sheriff's offices across Texas in
  the past month" reported officers on leave, under investigation, or criminally charged, without
  further naming them.

### Confidence assessment
**Primary-source-anchored / high confidence** on: the statute and its $4→$5 mechanics and May 29,
2023 effective date (official TDI bulletin); the bill's silence on ALPR (official TX Legislature
bill analysis); the general scale of MVCPA grant dollars flowing through this exact program
(official TxDMV annual report); Abbott's directive, its date, its narrow prospective-only scope,
and the spokesman's exact quote (direct-fetched Tribune reporting). **High-but-investigation-tier**
(direct-fetched from a credible named investigative source, not independently re-derived from a
raw ledger) on: the specific cumulative "$30 million" and "3,200+ cameras" totals and the $15.9M/
~1,200-camera DPS contract. No fabrication risk either way — every figure above traces to a named,
fetched document.

## 2. Fit against the locked primary theory chain (assessment only — locked chain file untouched)

Locked chain (from `notes/2026-08-16-phase3-theme-review-and-theory-lock.md`): **disclosed vs.
secret default data-sharing practice** → **perceived procedural injustice/contextual-integrity
violation** (mediator 1) → **institutional trust** (mediator 2) → **opposition intention** (DV),
moderated by prior distributive-surveillance exposure and perceived crime-solving necessity.

**Assessment: a good, but not exact, fit — a real-world instance of the same higher-order
mechanism (institutional secrecy about how public authority/resources are actually being used for
ALPR, revealed by outside investigation, followed by a partial institutional correction), at a
different level than the chain's literal manipulated variable.**

- The chain's manipulated antecedent is specifically about a *data-sharing/retention default*
  (local-only vs. secret-federal-access-by-default) discovered after the fact — i.e., secrecy
  about what happens to data already being collected. The Texas story is secrecy about *where the
  money to build the surveillance network came from and what it was really for* — a fee collected
  for a narrowly stated purpose (catalytic-converter theft) diverted, without legislative
  disclosure, into an unrelated mass-surveillance buildout. That is a **funding-purpose-diversion**
  variant of institutional secrecy, not a data-access-default variant. Both are non-disclosure of a
  government practice, discovered by an outside party (journalist/auditor, not the public agency
  itself), followed by an institutional response that is narrower than what critics wanted — the
  same discovered-secrecy → partial-correction shape that recurs across the corpus (Illinois #16:
  audit reveals unlawful CBP sharing → Flock pauses its CBP pilot nationwide; Mountain View #25:
  audit reveals undisclosed federal/state access → city shuts down its program) — but it operates
  one level up, at state appropriations/legislative disclosure rather than at the individual
  agency's data-access-policy level.
- This actually strengthens the *theme's* generalizability claim (Theme 2 as a mechanism that
  recurs at multiple institutional levels — local PD data-sharing default, state audit of unlawful
  sharing, and now state-legislative-appropriations diversion) without being a clean instantiation
  of the *specific* Study 2 manipulated variable. It belongs with the corpus's existing roster of
  Theme-2 artifacts as evidence the mechanism is real and generalizes, the same evidentiary role
  #16 and #25 already play — not as new evidence for the vignette's exact operationalization, and
  not as a hypothesis-anchoring item on its own.
- No public-opinion/opposition-intention data exists for this specific item (it is an
  elite/journalistic/executive-action account, like most of the corpus's institutional-secrecy
  entries), so — consistent with how #16, #23-25 were treated — it should support Theme 2's
  existence and cross-level generalizability qualitatively, not anchor a quantitative claim.

**Judgment: fits well enough to add to the corpus as a Theme-2 artifact, explicitly flagged as the
appropriations/funding-diversion variant rather than the data-sharing-default variant** — same
treatment as other real-but-structurally-distinct entries in this corpus (e.g., #19's individual-
misuse framing vs. #1-4's institutional-secrecy framing under the same broader code family).

## 4. Fresh real-world news sweep (new since 09-03)

All items below are new since the 09-03 note (roughly Sept 1-4, 2026) and independently
direct-fetch confirmed unless noted.

- **California kills ALPR regulation bill for the fifth year running (Sept 1-3, 2026,
  direct-fetch confirmed).** SB 1013 (Sen. Sabrina Cervantes, D-Riverside) would have restricted
  law-enforcement ALPR use to locating suspects/missing persons, required privacy/cybersecurity
  training, restricted default national-database sharing, and mandated AG audits for misuse.
  Blocked by Assembly Majority Leader Cecilia Aguiar-Curry on Sept 1 (the Legislature's final day)
  before a floor vote could happen — the fifth consecutive failed California ALPR reform attempt
  since 2022. Direct-fetch: https://calmatters.org/economy/technology/2026/09/california-kills-flock-camera-regulation-again/
  (also carried by KPBS/CalMatters wire partners).
- **"More than 200 cities/agencies ended ALPR contracts in August 2026 alone" — the single
  largest month since 2021**, per Secure Justice (Brian Hofer), cited in the same CalMatters piece
  — a striking escalation of the municipal rejection-wave trend the 09-03 note already flagged as
  growing. New CA cities named as recent cancellations: **Los Angeles PD, Redwood City, and
  Campbell** (in addition to already-tracked El Cerrito). WebSearch/CalMatters-sourced; the 200+
  national figure is Secure Justice's own count, not independently re-verified against a raw list.
- **A federal "Flock-Off Act" introduced this week (week of Sept 1-3, 2026, direct-fetch
  confirmed).** Reps. Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Eric Burlison (R-MO), with a genuinely bipartisan
  cosponsor list — Ro Khanna (D-CA), Lauren Boebert (R-CO), Paul Gosar (R-AZ), Chip Roy (R-TX),
  Victoria Spartz (R-IN) — would bar federal funding of ALPRs except at the border or for toll
  enforcement. Massie: *"The federal government shouldn't provide 'security' grants to cities and
  police departments that have gone beyond the pale and turned their communities into a version of
  1984."* Direct-fetch: https://reason.com/2026/09/03/a-new-bill-reflects-the-bipartisan-backlash-against-flock-enabled-mass-surveillance/
  (same piece also cites 157 local ALPR-contract cancellations nationwide since early 2025 —
  a lower, differently-scoped count than Secure Justice's August-only 200+ figure; another instance
  of the recurring denominator/method problem already flagged 08-21/09-03, not resolved here).
- **Pennsylvania: four separate bipartisan state-legislative ALPR proposals surfaced Sept 2, 2026
  (direct-fetch confirmed, bill numbers not yet assigned/available at publication).** Sen. Doug
  Mastriano (R-Franklin): restrict permitted-use categories (stolen vehicles, missing/endangered
  persons, active manhunts, specific investigations, warrants, imminent-threat emergencies). Sen.
  Lisa Boscola (D-Northampton): criminal penalties for unauthorized personal/unlawful ALPR access.
  Sen. Dan Laughlin (R-Erie): prohibit *networked* ALPR systems that build automatic all-vehicle
  databases, while still allowing individual vehicle-mounted readers. Rep. Tarik Khan
  (D-Philadelphia, 7 cosponsors incl. one Republican): private right of action plus AG/DA civil
  enforcement authority. Direct-fetch: https://www.wesanews.org/politics-government/2026-09-02/bipartisan-legislative-proposals-limit-flock-cameras-pa
  Notable pattern across CA/PA/federal this week: genuinely bipartisan opposition (not a partisan
  privacy-left-only story), which is itself a point worth keeping for the paper's framing —
  reinforces that this is public-policy terrain, consistent with Britton's 2026-08-16 venue call.
