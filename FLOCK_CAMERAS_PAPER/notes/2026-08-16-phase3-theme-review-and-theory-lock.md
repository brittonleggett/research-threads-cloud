## Finalized thematic map (six themes → collapsed into one primary chain + two secondary/
robustness themes, reported honestly rather than forced)

The six Phase 2 candidate themes don't all belong in a single experimental design at equal
weight — same principle as Tariff/Data Center, where not every Study 1 theme becomes a Study 2
factor. Locking as follows:

- **Primary manipulation axis: Theme 2 (institutional secrecy / default-opt-in data sharing).**
  Best-evidenced across the most independent artifacts (6 of 22), most manipulable as an
  experimental factor (disclosed-local-only vs. secret-federal-sharing-default is a clean
  binary), and — unlike Theme 1 — not a direct repeat of Data Center Paper's own primary theme,
  which keeps this paper theoretically distinct rather than a rerun. It is also the theme most
  directly reachable through Tyler-tradition procedural-justice theory (see literature section
  below), which is both well-established and squarely on-topic for a public-policy/public-
  administration venue — a real methodological advantage the other three papers don't have to
  the same degree.
- **Retained as a moderator, not the primary driver: Theme 1 (distributive/EJ-style
  disparity).** Real and strong, but making it the primary axis would make this paper read as
  a straight repeat of Data Center Paper's own lead theme. Instead: community's prior
  surveillance/distributive-injustice exposure is proposed as a moderator of the secrecy→
  injustice→distrust chain (does a community already experiencing disparate camera placement
  react more strongly to a secrecy revelation?) — theoretically motivated by the corpus itself
  (Norfolk State's case combines both: disparate placement AND a federal-lawsuit-level secrecy/
  consent fight in the same artifact).
- **Retained as a moderator: Theme 6 (ambivalent safety-benefit framing).** Perceived
  crime-solving necessity/efficacy as a buffer on the injustice→distrust→opposition chain —
  direct structural parallel to Data Center Paper's "AI/tech seen as inevitable vs. optional"
  moderator and Tariff's dual-entitlement logic. Also protects the paper from the "only shows
  one-sided opposition" critique, same rule as the other three papers' honest-null-findings
  practice.
- **Reported as real Study 1 findings, not built into the primary Study 2 design:** Theme 3
  (function creep — real but thin-sourced, kept as a discussion-section/future-research thread,
  not a hypothesis), Theme 4 (algorithmic accuracy/dread-risk harms — real and vivid, but a
  distinct causal story from the secrecy/trust chain; flagged as a strong candidate for a
  follow-up paper or a Study 3 robustness check, not force-fit into Study 2), Theme 5
  (collective mobilization/venue-efficacy — better treated as an outcome-variable validity
  anchor for the opposition-intention DV than as its own theme).

## Locked primary theory chain

**Disclosed vs. secret default data-sharing practice** (manipulated: local-only, explicitly
disclosed retention/access policy vs. broad federal-access default, discovered only after the
fact) → **perceived procedural injustice / contextual-integrity violation** (mediator 1) →
**institutional trust** (in local police/government; mediator 2, serial) → **opposition
intention** (support for removal/moratorium, protest/political-engagement intention;
DV) — **moderated by** (a) community's prior distributive-surveillance-exposure history, and
(b) perceived crime-solving necessity/efficacy.

This keeps Britton's established antecedent→mediator(s)→moderator→outcome shape (same family
as Tariff and the Data Center Paper's own proposed chain) while giving this paper its own
distinct primary mechanism (a technical/procedural secrecy failure, not an NDA-style corporate
secrecy or a distributive-placement pattern already used in Data Center Paper) — the more
defensible move given (1) the strongest and most independent evidentiary support in the
corpus, (2) a mature, well-established, and highly on-topic theoretical home (procedural
justice/police legitimacy — see below), and (3) genuine differentiation from both Data Center
Paper and the Nhan & Helfers (2026, *The Police Journal*) precedent, which used elite
interviews rather than a manipulated-vignette experiment and did not test a trust-mediated
opposition-intention outcome.
