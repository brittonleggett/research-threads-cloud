## 2. New: Missouri Gov. Kehoe's Executive Order 26-18 (corpus #48)

While searching for the five jurisdictions, search results surfaced a Sept. 16, 2026 statewide
executive order from Gov. Mike Kehoe restricting ALPR/Flock use across Missouri. This is new — not
mentioned in the 09-16 note — and significant enough to verify and add on its own:

- Bans AI facial-recognition integration with ALPR systems.
- Requires captured plate/vehicle data be deleted within 30 days.
- Bars vendors from selling, sharing, or commercializing Missouri-generated plate data.
- Limits use to "legitimate criminal justice and lifesaving purposes."
- Directs the Missouri Dept. of Public Safety to write implementing rules.
- Makes misuse trigger criminal referral, disciplinary proceedings, and possible officer license
  revocation.

Kehoe explicitly framed this as an interim measure pending the Missouri General Assembly passing its
own statutory restrictions. Verified via two independent direct fetches that agree on every
provision, the EO number, and the date: the **Missouri governor's office's own press release**
(primary source, governor.mo.gov) and **STLPR** (NPR member station). Both name the St. Charles Co.
case (#47 in this corpus) as the direct trigger. STLPR's reporting also names two further
background items — a "Brentwood officer accused of stalking ex-wife using license plate readers"
and, per one other outlet (Hoodline, used only for context, not as a primary source), an out-of-state
case involving a New York sheriff's investigator's arrest for unauthorized Flock searches. **Neither
of these two background items was independently verified this session** — they are named here as
leads for a future pass, not asserted as fact, and were deliberately not given their own corpus
rows.

This is coded as the corpus's first state-executive-level regulatory response to the Flock/ALPR
controversy — prior state-level items (#16 Illinois Secretary of State audit, #26/#27 California)
were legislative or administrative-audit actions, not a governor's executive order. Worth flagging
for Theme/discussion-section purposes as a concrete "policy diffusion from local misuse case to
state executive action" data point, though no theme-chain edit was made here — that's a Britton call
