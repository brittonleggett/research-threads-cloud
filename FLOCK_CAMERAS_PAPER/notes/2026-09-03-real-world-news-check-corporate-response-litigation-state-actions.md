## 1. Flock's own corporate response to the backlash — new, strong Discussion-section material

CEO Garrett Langley announced platform changes on **August 13, 2026** ("a continuation of our
commitment to both safety and privacy"): default data retention cut from 30 days to **7 days** (unless
tagged for preservation); mandatory audit tooling for all customers; case-number requirement for
law-enforcement searches "by end of 2026"; agencies can toggle off "Federal Sharing" entirely; federal
orgs already removed from statewide/national lookup networks (Aug 2025 change, restated here); ICE has
no direct contract/access absent explicit agency permission.

Civil-liberties reaction (direct-fetch confirmed via Reason.com): Michael Soyfer (Institute for
Justice) — *"One word you don't see mentioned anywhere in Flock's proposed reforms is the word
'warrant.'"* ACLU: the prior "search rationale" safeguard was trivially evaded with vague entries like
"criminal justice" — or literally **"hehehe."** Robert Frommer (IJ): called it *"yet more window
dressing."*

**Relevance:** this is a real-world instance of exactly the mechanism the theory chain studies — an
institution responding to a secrecy/trust breach with a disclosed-policy change, critics arguing it
doesn't restore trust because it stops short of the underlying legal protection (a warrant
requirement). Also complicates "disclosed vs. secret" as a clean binary going forward, since Flock is
now marketing disclosure-oriented reforms industry-wide.

Sources: https://www.flocksafety.com/blog/does-flock-share-data-with-ice-or-federal-agencies (direct),
https://reason.com/2026/08/14/flock-safetys-new-privacy-rules-do-little-to-ease-concerns-over-alpr-surveillance/ (direct)

## 4. New state-level regulatory/legislative actions

- **Florida (Aug 31–Sept 1, 2026, direct-fetch confirmed):** FDOT revoked all previously issued
  permits for license-plate readers in state road rights-of-way, ordering removal within 30 days and
  barring future permits — cited reasoning: "recent exponential increase in deployments" plus "misuse,
  data privacy concerns, and surveillance schemes." Scope limited: doesn't touch local roads or private
  property, where EFF notes ~98% of these cameras actually sit.
  https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/florida-bans-flock-license-plate-readers-state-highways-rcna595420 (direct)
- **Texas (Aug 28, 2026, direct-fetch confirmed) — strong new candidate Theme-2 artifact.** Gov.
  Abbott banned state agencies from spending state funds on Flock cameras, following a Texas Tribune
  investigation revealing the state's Motor Vehicle Crime Prevention Authority quietly funneled **at
  least $30 million** — from a **2023 law adding a $1 fee to auto-insurance policies specifically to
  fight catalytic-converter theft**, passed unanimously with no legislative discussion of ALPR funding
  — into building out **3,200+ Flock cameras statewide** (~2,000 local-agency cameras plus a
  $15.9M/~1,200-camera deal with Texas DPS). Order applies only to future state-fund spending, not
  removal, and leaves local/federal/private funding untouched. **A public-safety fee quietly
  repurposed, without legislative disclosure, into a statewide surveillance buildout is structurally a
  clean fit for "institutional secrecy / undisclosed purpose diversion"** — arguably a
  state-legislative-appropriations-level analog of the same mechanism the locked primary theory chain
  already targets at the local-data-sharing level. Not added to the corpus here — flagging for
  Britton's/your judgment on whether it belongs. The $30M/insurance-fee/no-discussion detail is
  WebSearch-snippet confidence (multiple outlets converge, but not independently fetched from the
  Texas Tribune article itself).
  https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/28/texas-greg-abbott-flock-cameras-order-state-money/,
  https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/texas-and-florida-step-back-alprs (both direct-fetched via EFF)
- **Washington — Driver Privacy Act (SB 6002, signed March 30, 2026), WebSearch-summary confidence,
  needs a verification pass** (ACLU-WA's own page 403'd on fetch): bans ICE access to ALPR data,
  21-day retention cap, prohibits use for immigration enforcement/protected-activity monitoring/
  reproductive or gender-affirming healthcare surveillance, bars cameras near schools/churches.
  Background cited: "at least eight Washington law-enforcement agencies were sharing Flock data
  directly with Border Patrol and ICE" prior to the law.
- **Virginia — SB 274, reportedly signed by Gov. Youngkin, 21-day retention period** — appeared only
  in WebSearch snippets, **not independently verified at all**. Flag as unconfirmed.

## 5. Litigation updates

- **San Jose (EFF/ACLU-NorCal state case, filed Nov 2025):** a tentative ruling reported Aug 6, 2026
  sided with the city — the judge tentatively treated San Jose's 500-camera ALPR network as an
  ordinary surveillance camera system rather than a distinct high-tech tracking technology requiring a
  warrant. EFF disputes this, analogizing ALPR to cell-site location info/geofence warrant cases. Not
  final; expected to be appealed. Direct-fetch confirmed: https://www.ktvu.com/news/judge-issues-tentative-ruling-san-jose-alpr-suit
- **A separate, second lawsuit against San Jose** was filed in U.S. District Court in April 2026 by the
  Institute for Justice — distinct from the EFF/ACLU-NorCal state case, not previously in this
  project's notes. WebSearch-confidence only.
- **Gibbs Mura California class action against Flock** (already known: filed Feb 26, amended Apr 3,
  2026) — new detail: alleges San Francisco's Flock database was queried 1.6 million times by
  out-of-state/federal agencies over 7 months (mid-2024–early 2025), and Ventura County found 364,000
  unauthorized searches. Damages framed at $2,500/violation under Cal. Civil Code §1798.90.55 —
  "exposure runs into the billions" at that volume. Also new: **CA AG Rob Bonta separately sued the
  City of El Cajon in October 2025** for violating California's ALPR Privacy Act (SB 34) by sharing
  data with out-of-state agencies — a state-AG enforcement action distinct from the private class
  action. WebSearch-confidence only, not independently fetched.
