## What this is (original 2026-08-16 orientation — venue/background paragraphs below still
## accurate; the "not yet a locked design" framing is superseded by the section above)
Exploratory paper concept (started 2026-08-16): public opposition to Flock
Safety's automated license-plate-reader (ALPR) camera networks, motivated by
Britton's read that this is "another point of contention with the public,"
same category of live controversy as the Data Center and CCS papers.

## Why this fits the existing pipeline
Fourth parallel research thread alongside TARIFF_PAPER, DATA_CENTER_PAPER,
and CCS_PAPER. Same Study 1 (AI-assisted thematic analysis of real public
discourse) → Study 2 (survey/experiment) template — see
`Claude Knowledge\Thematic Analysis\Study1_AI_Thematic_Analysis_Publishable_Protocol.md`
for the reusable method. Structurally closer to Data Center Paper than
Tariff/CCS: Flock Safety is a single identifiable corporate brand making
public trust/purpose-limitation promises to both government customers and
citizens, then visibly breaking them — a cleaner classic
consumer-trust/corporate-communication angle than Data Center's
utility-regulator-heavy case, which may make this an *easier* marketing-
journal fit, not just an easier one.

## Venue: public policy, not marketing (Britton's call, 2026-08-16)
Britton's explicit read: "this probably wouldn't be accepted in marketing
lit, it would be a public policy paper" — overrides the orientation note's
initial "may be an even cleaner marketing-journal fit than Data Center
Paper" guess below. Reframe venue thinking around public-policy/public-
administration/surveillance-studies outlets (Journal of Public Policy,
Policy Studies Journal, Public Administration Review, Journal of Policy
Analysis and Management, Regulation & Governance, Surveillance & Society,
Government Information Quarterly), not marketing journals like JPP&M —
unlike Data Center Paper, where the corporate-actor angle (a utility/tech
company making public promises) kept a marketing frame plausible, this
paper's central actors are government bodies (police departments, city
councils, ICE) which reads as public administration/policy territory even
though Flock itself is a private company. Worth deciding early whether the
Study1(AI-TA)→Study2(survey/experiment) template still fits as-is — survey
experiments are common in political science/public administration journals
too (APSR, JPART), so the method likely transfers, but the write-up framing
and literature grounding should lead with policy/administration theory
(regulatory capture, principal-agent, policy diffusion, procedural justice
in public administration) rather than consumer-trust/marketing theory.

## Real-world grounding (orientation pass 2026-08-16, see notes/ for full detail + sources)
Four real, well-documented controversy threads: (1) ICE/federal immigration
data access via Flock's network without local consent — the dominant
national story (Evanston, Syracuse, Bend OR, Richmond VA all documented
cases); (2) distributive/environmental-justice-style disparity — a CNU study
found Black neighborhoods in Hampton Roads surveilled at ~4x the rate of
white ones; (3) wrongful-stop/accuracy harms (Toledo K9 mauling case, LAPD's
32.3% false-positive rate); (4) a large and growing municipal rejection wave
(95+ cities per the DeFlock tracker, one full ban so far). Full sourcing in
`notes/2026-08-16-orientation.md`.
