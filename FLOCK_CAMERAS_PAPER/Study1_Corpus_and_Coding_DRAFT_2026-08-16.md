## Phase 0.5 — Brief

- **RQ (draft, not locked):** How do US communities, courts, regulators, and Flock Safety
  itself discursively frame the adoption, misuse, and rejection of ALPR camera networks, and
  what typology of opposition (and support) frames emerges?
- **Theory frame (candidates, not locked — see `notes/2026-08-16-orientation.md`):**
  contextual-integrity violation (data flows beyond consented purpose); distributive/EJ-style
  disparity; function-creep/broken-promise; dread-risk/wrongful-stop harms; procedural
  exclusion/secrecy.
- **Data type:** public news coverage (with embedded resident/official/advocate quotes),
  federal/state audit findings, lawsuit filings, city council votes and public hearings. Not
  survey/interview data, not human subjects directly.
- **Geographic scope, deliberately national** — unlike Data Center Paper's original
  Louisiana-only design, this corpus was built national-first from the start, per Britton's
  Data Center Paper feedback (2026-08-16) that broad generalizability matters more here than
  single-state positioning.

**1-4, 13, 16. Federal/ICE access & institutional secrecy (Bend, Evanston, Syracuse,

deliberate top-down concealment, sometimes it's the vendor's own control failure.
→ Codes: `default-opt-in-secrecy`, `institutional-distrust`, `vendor-control-failure`
(new — distinct from Data Center's deliberate-NDA-style secrecy), `post-removal-reactivation`

**5, 19. Function creep / misuse beyond stated purpose (Texas abortion case, ABC News
stalking case)** — both thinly sourced but point to a real category: individual actors (a
Texas officer, an unnamed officer in the ABC story) using the network for purposes far outside

Distinct from the institutional ICE-access pattern above: this is individual abuse of a
working system, not a data-sharing architecture failure.
→ Codes: `individual-misuse-function-creep`, `dread-risk-personal` (new — the harm model here
is closer to stalking/intimate-partner-violence risk than aggregate privacy harm)

**6, 7. Wrongful-stop/accuracy harms (Toledo, LAPD)** — Toledo: a single-digit plate misread
(JLL 7637 vs. JLL 7362) led to a K9 mauling and arrest of an innocent driver, Brandon
Upchurch, before the department settled for $35K. LAPD's own inspector-general audit found
Flock wrong **nearly one in three times** on stolen-vehicle flags — CIO Dean Gialamas:
"This contract is not being renewed because of serious concerns around civil liberties and
civil rights issues, particularly around privacy and the data that is being collected."
→ Codes: `algorithmic-accuracy-harm`, `dread-risk-institutional`, `official-non-renewal-
accountability`

**8, 9. Distributive/EJ-style disparity (CNU Hampton Roads study, Norfolk State lawsuit)** —
CNU's Finn/Keener regression analysis: Black neighborhoods surveilled ~4x the rate of white
ones; 8 of the top 10 camera-density neighborhoods are majority Black; 75% of high-poverty
tracts have a camera vs. <40% of low-poverty tracts. The Norfolk State (HBCU) case sharpens
this into a single, vivid, litigable instance: 17 cameras around the only HBCU in Hampton
Roads vs. ~5 near Old Dominion and 2 near Christopher Newport. IJ attorney Michael Soyfer:
"law-abiding Norfolk residents shouldn't have to go about their day knowing that their
movements are being tracked and stored." Near-exact structural transfer of Data Center
Paper's Theme 1 — same finding, second infrastructure type, strengthens the case this is a
generalizable mechanism rather than a Louisiana-specific artifact.
→ Codes: `distributive-environmental-injustice` (same code family as Data Center Paper),
`institutional-target-disparity` (new — HBCU/campus-specific targeting)

**10-12, 20-22. Formal municipal rejection wave (Ord, Denver, Menominee, El Cerrito,
Appleton, League City)** — the clearest evidence of collective mobilization escalating to
institutional action, and (unlike most of Data Center Paper's Louisiana cases) formal
opposition **frequently wins outright** here: Ord's 5-0 removal vote, Denver's unanimous
non-renewal (110 cameras physically removed), Menominee's 8-0 rejection. This asymmetry —
local councils/commissions as a more responsive venue than state utility regulators — echoes
the same finding from the Data Center Paper's 2026-08-16 national scan (Clinton Co. IN,
Tucson AZ, NY's statewide moratorium all won; Louisiana's PSC/Caddo-commission fights mostly
lost). Worth treating as a cross-paper, not paper-specific, finding.
→ Codes: `collective-political-mobilization`, `formal-legal-opposition`,
`venue-dependent-opposition-efficacy` (candidate cross-paper code, matches
`regulatory-venue-shifting` from the Data Center national scan)

**14, 15. Formal litigation over warrantless searches (San Jose, California class action)** —
San Jose: 261,711 warrantless ALPR searches by SJPD alone (June 2024-2025) prompted an
EFF/ACLU-NorCal suit; council added a 30-day retention limit in March 2026 but plaintiffs say
that doesn't fix the core warrantless-search issue. Statewide Gibbs Mura class action alleges
Flock itself (not just individual PDs) violated SB 34 by letting out-of-state/federal
agencies query California data — SFPD alone was queried 1.6M times by outside agencies in
seven months. Distinguishes Flock-as-defendant (corporate accountability) from
city-as-defendant (institutional accountability) — a real fork in whom the paper would treat
as the accountable actor.
→ Codes: `formal-legal-opposition`, `corporate-vs-institutional-accountability` (new)

**17, 18. Ambivalent/counter-current: real crime-solving efficacy claims** — Flock's own
figures (800K-1M crimes/year, Brown University shooting response, 2,000+ missing persons
recovered) and named police officials crediting real cases (Ferndale murder and Hazel Park
double-murder, Oakland Co. carjacking response) are genuine counter-evidence, not just
industry spin — PBS NewsHour ran this alongside the privacy critique in the same piece,
suggesting mainstream coverage already treats this as a genuine two-sided story. **Important
to keep and report honestly**, same rule Data Center Paper's Theme 5 established — a paper
that only shows opposition would misrepresent the actual public conversation.
→ Codes: `crime-solving-efficacy-claim`, `ambivalent-economic-opportunity-framing` (code
name carried from Data Center Paper, functionally "ambivalent-safety-benefit-framing" here)

## Phase 2 — Candidate themes (draft thematic map — needs Britton's Phase 3 review)

**Theme 1: Distributive/environmental-style injustice** (CNU study, Norfolk State) — the
single strongest, most consistently evidenced theme, and notably the *same* theme (not just
same code family) that anchored Data Center Paper's Study 1. Worth flagging to Britton
directly: if this recurs as the lead theme in a third paper too, it's approaching a real
cross-paper theoretical contribution in its own right (distributive injustice as a
generalizable driver of infrastructure/technology opposition), not just a repeated
convenience.

**Theme 2: Institutional secrecy / default-opt-in data sharing** (Bend, Evanston, Syracuse,
Richmond, Littleton, Illinois) — the ALPR-specific version of procedural exclusion: harms
here are less "we weren't consulted before the deal" (Data Center's dominant secrecy mode)
and more "we didn't know the technical default was sharing our data until an audit/journalist
found out." **Candidate primary Study 2 manipulation axis**, and a genuinely distinct
mechanism from Data Center Paper's NDA-based secrecy — worth its own construct rather than
directly reusing Data Center's procedural-exclusion operationalization.

**Theme 3: Function creep / broken purpose-limitation promise** (Texas case, ABC stalking
case, arguably the ICE cases too) — sold as "find stolen cars," used for immigration/
reproductive-healthcare/personal-stalking purposes. This paper's most distinctive angle
relative to the other three — no direct equivalent in Tariff/Data Center/CCS. Two sourcing
caveats need resolving before this can anchor Study 2: the Texas and ABC cases are both
thin/single-source right now.

**Theme 4: Algorithmic accuracy / dread-risk harms** (Toledo, LAPD) — vivid, involuntary,
high-salience harms (K9 mauling, armed high-caution stops) driving opposition independent of
aggregate statistics, plus LAPD's own audit giving a hard number (32.3%) to anchor a
risk-perception argument empirically rather than just anecdotally.

**Theme 5: Collective mobilization and venue-dependent efficacy** (Ord, Denver, Menominee,
El Cerrito, Appleton, League City) — matches Data Center Paper's Theme 6 (there flagged as
possibly-a-DV-not-a-theme) but with a sharper empirical wrinkle: formal opposition **wins far
more often here** than in Louisiana's data center fights. Same asymmetry the Data Center
national scan just found (state regulators more industry-aligned than local councils) —
this corpus is independent evidence for that same cross-paper pattern, worth naming as a
finding that spans papers, not just topics.

**Theme 6: Ambivalent safety-benefit framing** (PBS feature, Ferndale/Oakland Co.) — real
counter-current, same function as Data Center Paper's Theme 5. Don't let the paper claim
uniform opposition; mainstream coverage already treats efficacy claims as credible alongside
the privacy critique.

**Candidate new cross-cutting code, not yet a theme: `corporate-vs-institutional-
accountability`** (San Jose vs. Gibbs Mura) — whether the paper's accountable actor is Flock
Safety itself (a private company) or the adopting government body is a real fork that shapes
which theory (corporate trust/marketing-adjacent vs. public-administration/regulatory) fits
best — directly relevant to Britton's 2026-08-16 venue call that this reads as public policy,
not marketing, since most of the sharpest artifacts here (Menominee, Littleton, Illinois,
Ord, Denver) have a *government body* as the actor being scrutinized, with Flock itself
more often a secondary/vendor-failure actor than the primary antagonist.
