## Study 1: Method

### Purpose and Theoretical Framing

Study 1 addressed an inductive research question — how do U.S. communities, courts, regulators,
and Flock Safety itself discursively frame the adoption, misuse, and rejection of automated
license-plate-reader (ALPR) camera networks, and what typology of opposition (and support)
frames emerges — with the goal of grounding Study 2's experimental design in real-world
controversy patterns rather than a researcher-generated scenario. The analysis was informed by
procedural justice and police legitimacy theory (Tyler, 1990; Sunshine & Tyler, 2003) and
Nissenbaum's (2010) theory of privacy as contextual integrity (see
`Introduction_and_Theory_DRAFT_2026-08-16.md` for full theoretical development).

### Data

The corpus consisted of 22 artifacts — public news coverage (with embedded resident, official,
and advocate quotes), federal and state audit findings, lawsuit filings, and city council votes
and public hearing records — documenting real Flock Safety/ALPR controversies across fourteen
U.S. states and one national-level pattern (Oregon, Illinois, New York, Virginia ×3, Texas ×2,
Ohio, California ×3, Nebraska, Colorado, Michigan ×3, Massachusetts, Wisconsin). Sixteen of the
22 artifacts were directly verified via primary-source fetch or multi-source triangulation; six
remain thinner-sourced (single article, headline-level, or missing a confirmable name/date) and
are flagged in the corpus file rather than treated as citation-grade. Because the corpus
consists exclusively of publicly available news coverage, government records, and court filings
with no interaction with or data collection from human subjects, Study 1 is expected to qualify
for IRB exemption under 45 CFR 46.102(e), consistent with Tariff Paper and Data Center Paper's
own Study 1s — **not yet formally confirmed with Britton's IRB office, flagged as a to-do.**

The corpus was built deliberately national in scope from the outset, rather than concentrated in
a single state — a direct methodological response to Britton's 2026-08-16 feedback on the Data
Center Paper's original Louisiana-only design, that broader geographic generalizability matters
more for this line of research than single-state positioning.

### Analytic Approach

Consistent with the six-phase AI-assisted thematic analysis protocol established across
Britton's parallel research threads (see
`Claude Knowledge\Thematic Analysis\Study1_AI_Thematic_Analysis_Publishable_Protocol.md`),
coding proceeded through: (0.5) briefing on the research question, data source, and candidate
theoretical frames; (1) inductive coding of individual artifacts; (2) clustering of codes into
candidate themes; (3) review and refinement of candidate themes; (4) definition and naming of
finalized themes; and (5) write-up.

**Departure from standing practice, disclosed plainly:** in this project's three prior papers,
Phase 3 (theme review) was conducted by the researcher alone, without AI involvement, "a
deliberate methodological choice reflecting the judgment that recursive, contextual theme
refinement is not a task current models perform reliably without a human interpretive check"
(language carried from Tariff Paper's own methods draft). For this paper, Britton explicitly
authorized AI to complete Phase 3 as well, on 2026-08-16, in order to move the paper to a
reviewable state without his active involvement at each step. Phase 3 was therefore AI-run: six
candidate Phase 2 themes were collapsed into one primary manipulation axis, two theoretically
motivated moderators, and two themes retained as reported findings outside the primary
experimental design (see `notes/2026-08-16-phase3-theme-review-and-theory-lock.md` for the full
reasoning behind each call). **Whether this AI-completed Phase 3 is retained, revised, or
redone by Britton himself is an open, flagged decision — this section states plainly what was
done, not what should ultimately stand in the manuscript.**

### Reliability and Validity

The same validation literature cited across Tariff/Data Center/CCS applies here: Hill et al.
(2026, *PLOS Digital Health*) found Claude 4 Sonnet's deductive-coding agreement statistically
indistinguishable from trained human coders; AlGhamdi (2026, *IJQM*) validated Claude Code
specifically, reporting a "hierarchical convergence" pattern (code-level agreement more variable
than theme-level agreement) that structured, multi-phase prompting mitigates. **No validation
pilot (second human coder, code- and theme-level agreement statistics, quote verification) has
been run for this corpus** — same outstanding item flagged for Tariff/Data Center/CCS, now
also outstanding here, and arguably more load-bearing given Phase 3 itself was also AI-run for
this paper specifically, unlike the other three.

### Finalized Themes (Phase 3 complete, AI-run)

Six Phase 2 candidate themes were resolved into:

1. **Institutional secrecy / default-opt-in data sharing** — locked as the primary
   experimental-manipulation axis. Best-evidenced across six independent artifacts (Bend,
   Evanston, Syracuse, Richmond, Littleton, Illinois); theoretically distinct from Data Center
   Paper's NDA-style corporate secrecy, since the mechanism here is an undisclosed technical
   default rather than a negotiated confidentiality agreement.
2. **Distributive/environmental-style injustice** — retained as a first-stage moderator
   (community's prior surveillance-exposure history) rather than the primary axis, to avoid the
   paper reading as a direct repeat of Data Center Paper's own lead theme, while still reporting
   this as real, strong, independently-replicated evidence (Christopher Newport University
   study; Norfolk State University federal lawsuit).
3. **Ambivalent safety-benefit framing** — retained as a last-stage moderator (perceived
   crime-solving necessity/efficacy), grounded in real named police-official crime-solving
   claims and Flock's own aggregate figures, reported honestly as genuine counter-evidence
   rather than omitted to strengthen the opposition narrative.
4. Function creep/broken purpose-limitation promise, and algorithmic accuracy/dread-risk harms,
   are reported as real Study 1 findings (see corpus artifacts #5, #6, #7, #19) but not built
   into the primary Study 2 hypotheses — the former rests on thinly sourced artifacts, the
   latter reflects a distinct causal mechanism flagged for a follow-up study or robustness
   check.

Full artifact-level evidence and coding for all six original candidate themes remains in
`Study1_Corpus_and_Coding_DRAFT_2026-08-16.md`, unedited — this methods section summarizes the
Phase 3 resolution, it does not replace the underlying coded corpus.
