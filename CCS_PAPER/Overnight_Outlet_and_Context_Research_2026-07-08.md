# CCS Paper — Outlet & Current-Context Research (overnight, 2026-07-08)

## 1. What's driving the political timeliness right now

Louisiana has more proposed CCS projects than any other state (~30 direct-air-capture proposals, 35 injection/sequestration facilities, concentrated in Cameron and Ascension parishes), which is why this has become a statewide fight rather than a niche issue:

- **Gov. Jeff Landry issued an executive-order moratorium** (effective Oct 2025) freezing review of *new* Class VI injection-well permit applications, while ~33 pending applications got a 45-day LDENR re-evaluation. Already-permitted projects proceed. ([Phelps law firm summary](https://www.phelps.com/insights/louisiana-freezes-new-carbon-capture-applications.html))
- **2026 legislative session**: nine lawmakers (8 Republicans) sponsored ~24 bills on CCS. The lead bill, **HB7 ("Louisiana Landowners Protection Act")**, would repeal the CCS unitization statute and strip expropriation/eminent-domain authority for CCS projects and CO2 pipelines. **HB5** (Rep. Mike Johnson) would have let every parish hold a referendum on allowing CCS — it failed 7–9 in House Natural Resources Committee in May 2026, along with five similar parish-opt-in proposals. ([Louisiana Illuminator](https://lailluminator.com/2026/05/20/carbon-storage-4/))
- **Air Products cancelled** its Louisiana blue-hydrogen/ammonia project (which would have piped CO2 through Maurepas Swamp for injection under Lake Maurepas) — a visible, named failure, not just abstract opposition. ([Earthjustice](https://earthjustice.org/press/2026/air-products-abandons-proposed-louisiana-blue-hydrogen-and-ammonia-plant-with-carbon-capture-scheme))
- **Organized opposition** has real names now: Louisiana CO2 Alliance and Save My Louisiana, pushing for local permitting control and against eminent domain. ([Louisiana Illuminator](https://lailluminator.com/2026/02/11/louisianas-nation-leading-proposed-carbon-capture-projects-alarm-environmentalists/), [Verite News](https://veritenews.org/2026/02/12/louisiana-carbon-capture-projects/))
- Regulator pushback: LDENR disputes an EIP watchdog report critical of CCS oversight, maintains its Class VI program is "the most stringent in the nation." ([KALB](https://www.kalb.com/2026/02/23/ce-disputes-eip-report-about-carbon-capture-louisiana/), [Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/louisiana-regulator-pushes-back-on-ngo-report-criticizing-ccs-oversight/))
- Louisiana also **approved its first CCS storage well** this year, opening the door to the broader industry. ([nola.com](https://www.nola.com/news/business/louisiana-carbon-capture-emissions-climate-industry/article_d8e8abaa-2e24-5f3a-bf23-3c37740cd1a7.html))

**Timing flag:** the House committee votes were May 2026; Louisiana's regular session typically wraps by early June, so by now (July) the legislative fight has likely moved to "what actually passed/died" rather than being an open, live floor fight — confirm current bill status before writing anything that assumes it's still moving through committee.
