  https://www.wafb.com/2026/06/30/air-products-will-not-move-forward-with-ascension-parish-carbon-capture-facility/ ,

  https://lailluminator.com/2025/11/20/central-louisiana-residents-leaders-sue-louisiana-over-carbon-capture-land-seizures/

  https://www.theadvocate.com/baton_rouge/news/business/gov-landry-declares-halt-on-new-carbon-capture-applications/article_04f77b21-20d8-46f7-83a7-fd8308d5957c.html

## 2. National comparison-case scan

- **Illinois — SB 1289** ("Safety and Aid for the Environment in Carbon Capture and Sequestration
  Act"), passed both chambers (House 78-29, Senate 43-12): moratorium on new CO2 pipeline construction
  until PHMSA finalizes federal CO2-pipeline safety rules or July 1, 2026, whichever first. Partly
  triggered by a real corrosion/CO2-migration incident at an ADM Class VI well in Decatur, IL
  (2023-2024). Farm Bureau opposition centered on eminent domain for pore space.
  https://earthjustice.org/experts/jenny-cassel/we-passed-a-law-in-illinois-to-protect-communities-from-ccs-heres-the-view-from-the-negotiation-table
- **Iowa — Summit Carbon Solutions** rerouted its pipeline (announced May 13, 2026) to drop 8
  counties/400+ landowners, now routing Iowa CO2 through Nebraska to Wyoming instead of North Dakota,
  explicitly to avoid South Dakota (banned eminent domain for CO2 pipelines March 2025; PUC denied
  Summit's route permit April 2025). Gov. Reynolds vetoed an Iowa eminent-domain ban bill, calling it
  "poorly written." https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/article/2026/05/21/summit-drops-iowa-counties-co2-route
- **West Virginia — 4th Circuit litigation over Class VI primacy itself**, a strong comparison-case
  candidate. EJ groups are litigating (case likely styled *West Virginia Surface Owners' Rights
  Organization v. [EPA]* — full docket number not independently confirmed, InsideEPA paywalled)
  arguing (1) standing and (2) that WV's Class VI program is less stringent than federal rules. Reply
  brief filed May 20, 2026; oral arguments tentatively late October 2026. **This is a structurally
  different and arguably more interesting fight than Louisiana's eminent-domain suit** — it's the
  first case found of an EJ-standing challenge to a state's Class VI primacy grant itself, not just a
  taking. https://insideepa.com/climate-news/ej-groups-renew-standing-claims-over-west-virginia-class-vi-primacy-rule
- **EPA Class VI primacy roster, corrected via cross-check** (an initial WebFetch of EPA's own page
  returned a bad/incomplete extraction — "Texas and Arizona only" — which is wrong; cross-checked
  against Baker Botts, Trihydro, Bipartisan Policy Center, and a frESH law blog post, which agree):
  **North Dakota (2018), Wyoming (2020), Louisiana (Jan 2024), West Virginia (Jan 2025), Arizona
  (2025), Texas (Nov 12, 2025)** — six states — with **Colorado pending** (EPA proposed rule March 19,
  2026; comment period closed May 4, 2026; not yet finalized as of Aug 6, 2026). Colorado also signed
  a **Colorado-Wyoming MOU** (May 4, 2026) on cross-border Class VI notification — a novel inter-state
  coordination mechanism, lower priority for this paper but worth knowing exists.
- **Louisiana's own primacy date, precision note:** independent search consistently found **January
  2024** for LA's Class VI primacy grant; a Cassidy Senate press release is dated Dec 29, 2023
  ("Cassidy: At Last, Louisiana Secures Class VI Primacy") — so the grant may straddle Dec
  2023/Jan 2024 depending on whether "announcement" or "effective" is being dated. Not a
  contradiction, but use "Dec 2023/Jan 2024" rather than a single bare year if precision matters.
- Alberta (Canada) comparison, lower priority: DeSmog's Aug 5, 2026 piece on grassroots opposition to
  Pathways CCS pipeline explicitly models itself on Iowa/Summit tactics, and cites the 2020 Satartia,
  MS CO2 pipeline rupture (45+ hospitalized) as the shared "dread risk" reference point across US/
  Canadian opposition — reinforcing the Gough et al. (2014) citation already grounding Theme 2.
  https://www.desmog.com/2026/08/05/inside-the-grassroots-revolt-against-carbon-capture-pipelines/

## 4. Literature-gap scan (novelty check, not a redo of the 58-entry list)

- **Karimi, F., Marzban, E., & Dahlberg, U. (2025). "Navigating the future of carbon capture and
  storage technology: the interplay of social acceptability and political development." *Journal of
  Integrative Environmental Sciences*, 22(1), Article 2529796.** DOI 10.1080/1943815X.2025.2529796 —
  verified directly via Crossref API. Europe-focused (post-Ukraine-invasion CCUS sociopolitical
  landscape), lit review + stakeholder workshop + expert survey. Thematically close to Theme 4
  (regulatory/political dimension of acceptance) but different geography (Europe, not US/Louisiana).
- **Aurland-Bredesen, K.J., Inderberg, T.H.J., & Kverndokk, S. (2026). "Public Acceptance and the
  Dynamic Evolution of Adopting Carbon Capture and Storage."** CESifo Working Paper 12412. Confirmed
  via WebSearch/ResearchGate/IDEAS-RePEc author profiles, not independently Crossref-verified (may not
  have a registered DOI). **Caveat: working paper, not peer-reviewed.** Evolutionary-game-theory model
  of how public acceptance drives CCS subsidy policy evolution in Europe — a formal-model treatment of
  the acceptance↔policy feedback loop the paper's Track A political-economy angle touches informally.
  Flag its unpublished status if considered.
- Xiao, T. et al. (2025/2026, Utah CCUS review) — already known/cited (DOI 10.1002/ghg.2381), not a
  new finding, confirming no independent rediscovery of something already closed.
- General OpenAlex sweeps ("CO2 pipeline environmental justice," "CCS Louisiana community opposition,"
  "CO2 pipeline eminent domain social license," 2024-06-01+ and 2025-01-01+ filters) returned mostly
  technical/engineering CCS papers, DOE lab reports, and unrelated keyword-collision hits — no
  additional on-topic academic hits, consistent with the project's own 08-16 conclusion that the field
