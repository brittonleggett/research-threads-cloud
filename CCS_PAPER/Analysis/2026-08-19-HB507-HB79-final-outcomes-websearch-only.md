**Why this matters more than a status update:** the 08-16 corpus note's framing — "local-
control/property-rights/liability bills all died; only an accountability/tracking measure
(HB820) survived" as a clean signaling-theory pattern (industry tolerates transparency, blocks
anything with teeth) — is now **directly contradicted**, not just complicated. HB79 didn't just
clear one committee (08-17's finding); it passed the full House by a wide, bipartisan-leaning
margin (70-25) and the Senate nearly unanimously (32-2). A bill removing a *substantive
liability protection* the industry had (the $250K damages cap) passed both chambers with real
margins. **Recommend not writing the "signaling theory" framing into any manuscript language
without accounting for this** — the cleaner story now might be "the industry lost on liability
exposure specifically, even while other property-rights/local-control bills died," which is a
different and arguably more interesting theoretical claim (it isolates *what kind* of
regulatory threat the industry couldn't block, not just that transparency bills survive and
teeth doesn't). That's a real judgment call for Britton, not something to lock in here.
