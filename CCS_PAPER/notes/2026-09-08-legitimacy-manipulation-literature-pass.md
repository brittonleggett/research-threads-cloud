# 2026-09-08 — Literature grounding pass for the institutional-legitimacy-manipulation pillar

Follow-up to `2026-09-08-theoretical-differentiation-test.md`'s reframed lead theory (technology-
legibility, moderated by institutional-legitimacy-manipulation). Theme 3 (technology-legibility)
was already well-grounded via Rodriguez (2023) in `2026-08-16-academic-literature-grounding.md`.
This pass targets the second pillar, which had no dedicated literature — the existing Theme 4
citations (Heern 2023, Carley 2026) are about regulatory capture via utility-commission
composition, a related but distinct construct from manufacturing/contesting the *appearance* of
grassroots legitimacy.

## Citations found (WebSearch/WebFetch, verification level noted for each — none confirmed at
full-text level, per the standing "never cite unverified" rule; a library/Consensus.app pull is
the recommended next step before manuscript use)

**Strongest, most direct hit — energy-sector-specific:**
Sassan, C., Mahat, P., Aronczyk, M., & Brulle, R. J. (2023). "Energy Citizens 'Just Like You'?
Public Relations Campaigning by the Climate Change Counter-movement." *Environmental
Communication*, 17(7). DOI via tandfonline.com/10.1080/17524032.2023.2255388. Citation/
search-snippet level only (403'd on full fetch). Studies "Energy Citizens," an API
(American Petroleum Institute)-created front group that bussed in industry employees and
advertised them as ordinary "truckers, farmers, homemakers" — documents the exact mechanism
(manufacturing the appearance of organic grassroots support) the corpus's astroturfing-adjacent
evidence needs. Robert J. Brulle is an established, frequently-cited climate-countermovement
researcher, which adds real credibility. Same phenomenon as the Louisiana CO2 Alliance finding,
inverse valence (pro-industry front group vs. a claimed-anti-industry voice that's actually
quasi-institutional).

**Good venue-fit candidate, business-ethics specific:**
Cho, C. H., Martens, M. L., Kim, H., & Rodrigue, M. (2011). "Astroturfing Global Warming: It
Isn't Always Greener on the Other Side of the Fence." *Journal of Business Ethics*, 104(4),
571-587. DOI 10.1007/s10551-011-0950-6. Citation-confirmed only (agreeing hits across
IDEAS/RePEc, PhilPapers, Semantic Scholar; full text/abstract paywalled everywhere tried). From
snippets: an experimental study testing whether astroturf organizations affect public certainty
about climate-change causes. Real venue-fit value — JBE is a plausible target-adjacent journal
for Britton's own work.

**EU energy-sector astroturfing, extraction-industry-specific (same author, three related
pieces):**
Lits, B. — "Astroturf lobbying in the EU: The case of shale gas exploration" (2019, *Networking
Knowledge*); "Exploring astroturf lobbying in the EU: The case of responsible energy citizen
coalition" (2020/2021, *European Policy Analysis*); "Detecting Astroturf Lobbying Movements"
(*Communication and the Public*, SAGE, DOI 10.1177/2057047320969435). All search-snippet level
only. Documents a real EU shale-gas astroturf case (the "Responsible Energy Citizen Coalition")
emphasizing fracking-safety while concealing industry sponsorship — an extraction-industry
precedent, methodologically close to a CCS astroturfing claim even though not CCS itself.

**Push-polling: no dedicated academic literature found.** What exists is professional/
methodological (AAPOR/AAPC/NCPP's 1996 joint condemnation statement, SAGE's *Encyclopedia of
Survey Research Methods* entry), not primary empirical research on push-poll effects, and
nothing applying it to an energy/environmental-infrastructure context. Cite the definitional
literature; don't expect a study measuring push-poll effects to lean on.

**Direct CCS/CCUS + astroturfing or push-polling precedent: none found.** A CCUS public-
perception systematic review and several CCS public-opinion studies turned up nothing on
astroturfing or push-polling in a CCS context specifically.

## Verdict

Real, citable academic literature exists behind astroturfing/manufactured-grassroots-legitimacy
as a construct, with at least one paper (Sassan et al. 2023) that's energy-sector-specific and
directly on-point in mechanism. But **none of it has reached CCS/CCUS specifically** — worth
naming to Britton as a genuine novelty angle for this paper (nobody has applied this construct to
CCS opposition specifically), not a gap to paper over. Push-polling has no comparable empirical
literature to lean on beyond definitional sources.

**Verification caveat, real and worth taking seriously:** every full-text fetch attempt this pass
hit a paywall (Springer, SAGE, Taylor & Francis all 403'd). These citations are confirmed only at
the citation/search-snippet level — meaningfully weaker than Theme 3's Rodriguez (2023) citation,
which was independently full-text-confirmed in the 08-16 pass. Recommend a library/Consensus.app
pull on Cho et al. (2011) and Sassan et al. (2023) specifically — the two strongest candidates —
before either goes into a manuscript.
