## 1. ND amalgamation-law appeal — verified and deepened

Confirmed via multiple independent outlets (North Dakota Monitor, KFYR-TV, Agweek, InForum,
TSLN.com, all reporting the same underlying filing, dated ~Aug 31, 2026): a North Dakota district
court voided Summit Carbon Solutions' CO2 storage permits for all three of its designated
North Dakota storage areas, on the basis that the state's "amalgamation" law (requiring
landowners to allow CO2 storage under their property if 60% of affected landowners agree) is
unconstitutional — it denies the jury-trial and pre-payment-of-compensation guarantees the state
constitution requires for takings. **Two separate district judges** (Jackson Lofgren, and
Northeast Judicial District's Anthony Swain Benson in a second suit) independently reached the
same unconstitutionality conclusion. One outlet (CBS2 Iowa) reports this ruling is already
"rippling into" a related Iowa case.

Summit and the state are now appealing to the **North Dakota Supreme Court**, but Summit itself
has filed asking the court to **limit the appeal to just one of the three areas ("Summit #3")** —
stating in its own filing that it has made "a matter of business judgment" not to proceed with
the other two ("Summit #1 and Summit #2"). This confirms and adds real legal-basis depth to the
project's existing note ("ND amalgamation appeal narrows, Summit forfeits 2 of 3 permits") — no
correction needed there, just richer grounding (the underlying constitutional theory, the two
independent rulings, the Iowa spillover) for whoever writes up the comparative-litigation section.
Sources: northdakotamonitor.com/2026/08/31/..., kfyrtv.com/2026/08/31/..., tsln.com (unconstitutional
ruling detail), cbs2iowa.com (Iowa ripple detail).
