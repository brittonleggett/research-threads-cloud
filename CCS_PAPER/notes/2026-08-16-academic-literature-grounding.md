## 1. Risk perception / involuntary exposure (grounds Theme 2 — the corpus's own leading
Study 2 axis candidate)

- **Gough, C., O'Keefe, L., & Mander, S. (2014). "Public perceptions of CO2 transportation in
  pipelines." *Energy Policy*, 70, 106-114.** Real, verified (via IDEAS/RePEc and ScienceDirect
  listing; full text 403'd, citation-level only). The field's classic CO2-pipeline-specific
  perception study — finds lay publics have decent general knowledge of CO2 but not its
  specific hazard properties, and that concerns extend beyond immediate physical risk to more
  abstract, "first-of-a-kind technology" unease. Directly grounds the corpus's own Satartia-
  reference-point pattern (artifacts #4, #7) — residents citing a real incident as their risk
  anchor is exactly the psychometric-paradigm "dread risk" pattern (Slovic) this paper's other
  siblings already use, now with a CO2-specific precedent rather than a generic risk-perception
  citation.
- **Ogle, J. et al. (2025). "Risk-based facility siting of CO2 pipelines." *Process Safety
  Progress* (AIChE/Wiley), DOI via aiche.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/prs.70018.** Real,
  technical/engineering-side siting-risk paper — useful background for understanding what's
  actually being regulated/contested (individual risk of CO2 pipelines estimated at
  10⁻⁶–10⁻⁷, generally treated as low-to-negligible in most countries' regulatory frameworks) —
  worth citing to show the *technical* risk assessment and the *lay* risk perception the corpus
  documents (artifact #4's careful-but-anxious safety explainer) are two different things, a
  real gap the paper can discuss.
- **Psychometric paradigm / dread risk (Slovic tradition)** — already used elsewhere in
  Britton's papers; the CO2-specific application here (Gough et al. above) is the paper's own
  citable bridge from the generic framework to this specific technology, same move Data Center
  Paper made with distributive-injustice theory.

## 2. Regulatory/institutional capture (grounds Theme 4 — dark-money disclosure rule,
state-capture metanarrative)

- **Heern, J. (2023). "Who's controlling our energy future? Industry and environmental
  representation on United States public utility commissions." *Energy Research & Social
  Science*, 101, 103091. DOI 10.1016/j.erss.2023.103091.** **Author confirmed 2026-08-17 via
  direct Ole Miss/ScienceDirect pull: solo-authored by Jared Heern, NOT Leah Stokes** (the
  earlier WebSearch guess was wrong) — full abstract read directly: original data on 800+ PUC
  commissioners 2000-2020, utility/fossil-fuel ties more common than environmental ones. Close
  empirical precedent for the corpus's own artifact #6 (the dark-money disclosure rule).
- **Carley, S. (2026). "Understanding Energy Justice Challenges Through a Regulatory and
  Institutional Lens." *The Energy Journal*, OnlineFirst 2026-06-16. DOI
  10.1177/01956574261460226.** **Journal name confirmed 2026-08-17** (was previously unconfirmed
  — resolved live via Ole Miss/Sage proxy, journal is *The Energy Journal*, not a law/policy
  journal as guessed). Sanya Carley, University of Pennsylvania. Open access. Directly
  cross-applicable — energy-justice-through-a-regulatory-lens framing is exactly the theoretical
  move Theme 4 needs.
- **NIMBY/siting-regime literature** — WebSearch surfaced a general finding that politically
  weak groups are disadvantaged by siting regimes' own structure, not just by individual bad
  actors — relevant framing for why the corpus treats regulatory capture as structural
  ("the state is captured," artifact #3) rather than a one-off bad decision.

## 3. CO2 storage vs. CO2-EOR public literacy/confusion (grounds Theme 3 — a genuinely
CCS-distinctive angle none of Britton's other three papers have)

- **Rodriguez, E. (2023). "Storing carbon dioxide for climate's sake: contradictions and
  parallels with enhanced oil recovery." *Frontiers in Climate*, 5, Article 1166011, DOI
  10.3389/fclim.2023.1166011.** Real, verified, and a near-exact theoretical match: identifies
  two competing academic/industry framings of CO2-EOR — a "gateway" framing (EOR builds
  infrastructure/knowledge that enables future dedicated storage) vs. an "incompatibility"
  framing (EOR extends fossil extraction, goal-conflicting with climate mitigation) — and
  argues the dominant techno-economic literature (often industry-authored) underweights social-
  science scrutiny of this framing conflict. **This maps almost exactly onto the corpus's own
  `EOR-greenwashing-reframing` code (artifact #2) and the `CO2-EOR-storage-conflation` code
  (artifact #4)** — the public confusion the corpus documents isn't just a lay-knowledge gap,
  it's downstream of a real, unresolved framing conflict in the field's own literature. Strong,
  citable anchor for Theme 3.
- **SPE/OnePetro industry-conference abstract, "Comparing Public Acceptance of Dedicated CO2
  Storage and CO2-EOR: Regional Trends and Their Impact on CCS Deployment" (2025).** Found via
  WebSearch, not independently fetched — industry-conference-tier source (OnePetro/SPE), lower
  academic weight than the Frontiers piece but useful corroboration that the storage-vs-EOR
  public-acceptance gap is recognized industry-side too, not just an academic/activist framing.

## 4. Environmental justice in CCS/CO2-pipeline siting specifically (grounds Theme 1's
capture/EJ overlap and would strengthen any Track A/B/C choice)

- **Lam, Y., Ventrella, J., Baptista, A. I., & Rodriguez, J. D. (2025). "Analysis of proposed
  carbon capture projects in the US power sector and co-location with environmental justice
  communities." *PLOS One*, 20(5), e0323817, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0323817.** Fully verified
  via direct fetch — real, strong, quantitative: of 35 proposed CCS facilities nationally,
  **33 (94.3%) are located in EJ communities**; of 497 EJ census block groups within 3 miles of
  proposed facilities, 85% already face heightened environmental stress (an environmental
  health index above the 80th percentile nationally). This is the single strongest citation
  found this pass — a national quantitative EJ baseline for CCS siting, directly parallel in
  structure to the Ancona et al. (2026, *Nature Cities*) data center-siting finding already
  added to Data Center Paper's national scan, and to the CNU Hampton Roads study anchoring Flock
  Cameras Paper's own EJ theme. **Worth flagging to Britton directly: this is now the third
  paper in his pipeline where a national quantitative EJ-siting-disparity study anchors the
  same theme** — a real, citable cross-paper pattern (distributive/environmental injustice as a
  generalizable driver of infrastructure opposition), not just convenient repetition.
- **Davis, J. A., Salehi, N., Li, L., & Shafiee-Jood, M. (2025). "Carbon dioxide pipelines are
  disproportionally located in marginalized communities in the United States." *Communications
  Earth & Environment* (Nature portfolio), May 2025.** Real, confirmed via multiple independent
  searches (full DOI/article-number not independently pulled — Nature's own page redirected to
  an auth wall; cite by title/journal/date, verify exact article number before final manuscript
  use). Key findings: proposed CO2 pipelines are more likely sited in areas with higher Black
  population share; areas with higher educational attainment show reduced pipeline likelihood;
  all pipelines skew toward rural siting. A second, independent quantitative EJ-pipeline-siting
  study — corroborates Lam et al. above from a different angle (pipeline routing specifically,
  not just facility siting).

## 5. Novelty check: has anyone done AI-assisted/netnographic discourse analysis of public
CCS opposition before?

**Partial precedent found — genuinely worth knowing before claiming full novelty:**

- **Keane, R. H. (2018). "Communication of Scientific Knowledge About Carbon Capture and
  Storage (CCS) Through Social Media (SM)." Presented at the 14th International Conference on
  Greenhouse Gas Control Technologies (GHGT-14), Melbourne, October 2018.** Real (confirmed via
  SSRN listing and the author's own Google Scholar profile, University of Queensland). Uses
  netnography on CCS-related social media — but a meaningfully different corpus and question
  than Track C: scraped Facebook/Twitter content from **19 pro-CCS institutes and 11 industry
  players** (i.e., institutional/industry communication, not resident/public discourse), asking
  who communicates about CCS and how, not what the public actually believes or how it opposes
  projects. **This is close enough that Track C's eventual write-up needs to explicitly
  differentiate against it** (same method family, different unit of analysis and RQ — public/
  resident discourse and opposition framing, not institutional communication strategy) — same
  move the Flock Cameras Paper already had to make against Nhan & Helfers (2026). Also 2018 is
  pre-dating the current Louisiana CCS boom by years, which itself is a point of differentiation
  (contemporary, live regulatory moment vs. a general communication-strategy snapshot).
- Also found, lower relevance: a comparative state-level CCS-discourse-among-stakeholders study
  and a UK BECCS newspaper-framing study (166 articles, 4 framings/8 storylines) — both
  discourse-analytic but neither AI-assisted nor Reddit/public-resident-focused; useful as
  "discourse analysis of CCS is an active method family" background, not close precedent.
- **Bottom line: Track C's specific combination (AI-assisted netnography + Reddit/public-
  resident discourse + Louisiana-specific + live 2024-2026 regulatory moment) still looks
  genuinely novel** — the one real precedent (Keane 2018) differs on unit of analysis, data
  source, and era. Same confidence level as Data Center Paper's own novelty finding, not
  stronger — this was a single-pass check, not the deep multi-source scan Data Center got.
