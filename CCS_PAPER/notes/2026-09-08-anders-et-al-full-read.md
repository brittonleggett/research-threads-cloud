## The actual experimental design (real detail, not the lit review's paraphrase)

**Seven attributes, three-to-six levels each, shown as 6 randomly-drawn vignettes per respondent
out of a 144-vignette orthogonal fractional-factorial design** (reduced from a full factorial of
8,748 possible combinations):

1. **Implementing body**: industry consortium (ref.) / government-industry partnership /
   national government
2. **Proximity to respondent's residence**: up to 50 km (ref.) / 50-100 km / more than 100 km
3. **CO2 mitigative capacity**: equivalent to 5% (ref.) / 10% / 20% of household emissions in the
   respondent's region
4. **Geographical origin of CO2**: domestic only (ref.) vs. domestic + imported from a named
   country (this is the paper's actual key IV — cross-border transport)
5. **Extent of public consultation** (six levels, not three — this is the one closest to the CCS
   paper's own governance-frame idea): no consultation (ref.) / relevant NGO only / affected
   communities / all communities / all state citizens / all country citizens
6. **Seismicity risk information**: no information (ref.) / information during approval only /
   information for as long as the project runs
7. **Compensation**: no compensation (ref.) / preferential contracts for local businesses /
   direct financial compensation to citizens

**Measurement:** two questions per vignette, both 11-point scales (-5 to +5): "How acceptable is
this CCS development scenario to you?" and "How fair is the proposed storage of CO2 ... to you?"
**Before the experiment:** a mandatory neutral information screen (plain-language CCS definition
+ a visual of the capture/transport/storage process), also available on-demand throughout — a
directly reusable design element for the CCS Study 2 instrument's own intro.

**Sample:** ~1,000 respondents per country (Canada, Germany, Netherlands, Norway, UK), quota-
matched to census age/gender/education/household-income, recruited via SurveyEngine (a European
panel vendor, not Prolific/CloudResearch). Ethics approval via University of Alberta's Human
Research Ethics Board.

## Real findings, two of which should directly change design thinking

1. **Cross-border CO2 import is the single strongest negative predictor of acceptance across
   every attribute tested** (-0.39 to -0.84 scale points depending on country pair) — bigger than
   consultation, risk info, compensation, or implementing body. Not directly transferable to a
   Louisiana-only design (no cross-border dimension there), but confirms this attribute class
   (geographic/jurisdictional framing) can dominate a multifactorial design when present.
2. **Consultation extent and risk-information transparency both robustly increase acceptance in
   every one of the five countries** — this is real, multi-country empirical support for the CCS
   paper's own H1 (consultation-type governance frames raise procedural justice/acceptance).
3. **Real null finding, worth taking seriously: implementing-body type (industry vs.
   public-private vs. government) did NOT significantly affect acceptance.** The authors note this
   explicitly as "interesting" since they expected public trust differences across CCS stakeholder
   types to matter. **Direct implication for the CCS lit review's proposed 6-level governance-frame
   manipulation:** two of its six levels (state regulator approval, industry expertise) are
   essentially this same "who approved it" dimension that this precedent found doesn't move
   acceptance. Worth treating that as a real caution rather than assuming all six frame levels are
   equally load-bearing — the consultation-type levels (affected-community, Tribal consultation)
   look like the more promising, better-supported half of the design; the implementing-body-type
   levels may be worth keeping as a secondary/exploratory comparison rather than an assumed
   headline effect.
4. **Financial compensation does not offset the negative effect of cross-border import** — the
   interaction term was not significant. If the CCS design ever crosses a governance frame with a
   compensation manipulation, this precedent suggests not to expect compensation to rescue a
   negatively-received frame.

## What this changes about the CCS Study 2 design conversation

- The lit review's own hypothesis ordering (consultation/recognition frames > industry/regulator
  frames) now has direct multi-country empirical backing from this precedent, not just theoretical
  support from Tyler (2003) and the SLO literature.
- Worth reconsidering whether all six governance-frame levels deserve equal weight, or whether the
  design should lean harder into the consultation-type gradient (no consultation → NGO → affected
  community → Tribal consultation) as the primary manipulated dimension, with implementing-body
  type demoted to a secondary factor given this precedent's null result on that dimension.
- The neutral pre-vignette information/education screen is a concrete, reusable design element.

  Section III) if an exact adaptation (rather than a structurally-informed new design) is wanted.
