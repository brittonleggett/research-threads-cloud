# Target Journals (Section 15)

Status: first-pass candidate list, 2026-09-03, based on general knowledge of
each outlet's scope and publication history. ABDC rankings below are from
memory and should be spot-checked against the current ABDC Journal Quality
List before being used in any submission decision — flagged per-journal
below where confidence is lower. Not optimizing for a single journal yet, per
the brief.

## Marketing / consumer research
**Journal of Public Policy & Marketing (JPP&M)**
- Scope fit: very high. JPP&M explicitly wants marketing research with
  public-policy relevance — pricing fairness, labeling/disclosure,
  food-system policy are core to its mission. This is plausibly the single
  best-fit outlet for the combined empirical-puzzle + marketing-contribution
  framing this project is building toward.
- ABDC (recall, verify): A.
- Why we might fit: COOL/labeling transparency and price-fairness framing
  map directly onto JPP&M's recurring interest in disclosure/labeling policy
  (nutrition labeling, sustainability labeling, etc. have precedent there).

**Journal of Consumer Research (JCR) / Journal of Consumer Psychology (JCP)**
- Scope fit: high if Study 2's contribution is squarely about a consumer
  psychological process (attribution, fairness judgment, opportunism
  inference) rather than policy per se.
- ABDC (recall, verify): A* (JCR), A (JCP).
- Caveat: JCR/JCP reviewers will expect a tight theoretical contribution to
  a specific psychological literature, not primarily a policy-relevance
  story — best fit only if Study 2's theory (once locked) is unusually novel
  at the construct level.

**Journal of Marketing (JM) / Journal of Marketing Research (JMR)**
- Scope fit: medium-high, contingent on framing. JM has published channel-
  power/supply-chain and pricing-fairness work; JMR would fit best if the
  paper leans quantitative/methodological (e.g., a strong SEM or
  text-mining contribution per Berger et al. 2020's framing, which is
  already the shared-method doc's anchor citation for Study 1's legitimacy
  in marketing).
- ABDC (recall, verify): A* (both).
- Caveat: highest bar of any outlet on this list; realistic only if the
  full three-study package lands as unusually clean and novel.

**Journal of Business Research (JBR)**
- Scope fit: high, more forgiving venue than the A* set above, and already
  the home of several of the shared-method doc's own precedent papers
  (Herhausen et al. 2025; Pueschel, Hao & Schmitt 2026 both cite the same
  AI-assisted-qualitative-methods lineage this project's Study 1 uses).
- ABDC (recall, verify): A.
- Why we might fit: broad scope, comfortable with mixed-method (qual +
  quant) marketing papers, demonstrated appetite for AI-assisted qualitative
  method papers specifically.

## Public policy / food policy
**Food Policy**
- Scope fit: high for the empirical-puzzle/policy layer (imports, COOL,
  concentration, price transmission) but this is not a marketing journal —
  a submission here would need to lead with the policy contribution, not
  the marketing-theory contribution. Useful fallback/companion-paper target
  if the marketing angle and the policy/econ angle end up better split into
  two papers rather than one.
- ABDC (recall, verify): A.

**Journal of Consumer Affairs**
- Scope fit: medium-high — consumer-protection and disclosure/labeling
  themes fit; less prestigious than JPP&M but a reasonable second-choice
  policy-adjacent marketing outlet.
- ABDC (recall, verify): B (verify — may have moved).

## Supply-chain management
**Journal of Supply Chain Management / Journal of Operations Management**
- Scope fit: medium. Channel power, dependence, transaction-cost framing
  (if those rank Strong in `THEORY_CANDIDATES.md`) would fit; less natural
  fit for the consumer-perception/Study 2 half of the project. Most
  plausible if Study 1's institutional-discourse findings and a channel-
  power/dependence theory framing end up carrying the paper more than the
  consumer experiment does.
- ABDC (recall, verify): A (JSCM), A* (JOM).

## Agricultural/food economics (context only — not the primary target)
**American Journal of Agricultural Economics / Agricultural Economics /
Journal of Agricultural and Resource Economics**
- Not a marketing-journal target, but the literature these journals publish
  (concentration measures, price-transmission studies) is exactly what
  `LITERATURE/Market_Concentration_Evidence.md` and
  `LITERATURE/Price_Transmission_Literature.md` need to draw on and cite
  correctly. Listed here as a citation source, not a submission target,
  unless the project ultimately splits into a companion econometric paper
  (see `STUDY3/Study3_Feasibility.md` Option 1's contribution-risk note).

## Recommendation (tentative, pending Study 1/2 results and THEORY_CANDIDATES.md)
Primary target: **JPP&M** — best fit for the combined empirical-puzzle +
marketing-contribution story this brief is aiming for, and the outlet most
likely to value both the archival/policy grounding and a consumer
experiment in the same paper. Secondary/stretch: **JM** or **JCR/JCP**
depending on which theory lens proves strongest. Realistic fallback: **JBR**.
Do not optimize the design around a single journal until Study 1 results and
`THEORY_CANDIDATES.md` rankings are in — journal fit should follow the
finished contribution, not dictate it upstream.
