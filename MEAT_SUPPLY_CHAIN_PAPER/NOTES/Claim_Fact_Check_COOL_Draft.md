# Claim Fact-Check — COOL Claims (fork draft, 2026-09-03)

Covers claims #10–#11 from the research brief's Section 13. To be merged into
the consolidated `NOTES/Claim_Fact_Check.md`.

## Claim #10: Can consumers currently determine whether beef came from an
animal born, raised, slaughtered, and processed in the United States?

**Verdict: Partially supported (context-dependent — true only for a subset
of product, and only from January 1, 2026 forward).**

There is **no mandatory** country-of-origin disclosure on beef or pork sold
at U.S. retail — Congress repealed mandatory COOL for beef and pork (muscle
cuts and ground product) on December 18, 2015 (P.L. 114-113), after losing a
multi-year WTO dispute with Canada and Mexico. For any package that carries
no voluntary origin claim, a consumer genuinely cannot tell from the label
alone. Where a package *does* carry a voluntary "Product of USA"/"Made in
the USA" claim, a new FSIS rule (published March 18, 2024, effective
May 17, 2024, mandatory compliance January 1, 2026) now requires that claim
to mean the animal was born, raised, slaughtered, and processed entirely in
the U.S. Before the January 2026 compliance date, and for the older/looser
standard, "Product of USA" could legally appear on meat from animals born
and raised abroad but merely slaughtered or processed domestically — so the
claim's meaning changes depending on when the product was labeled. Bottom
line: as of this research pass (Sept 2026), the label can tell a consumer
this *if and only if* it carries a Product of USA claim produced under the
post-compliance-date standard; absent that claim, no.
Source: `NOTES/COOL_Regulatory_Timeline.md`.

## Claim #11: How have country-of-origin labeling laws changed? (i.e., is
the common belief about current COOL protections for meat accurate?)

**Verdict: Unsupported, as commonly believed.**

Many consumers likely assume COOL still functions as it did after the 2008
Farm Bill — mandatory, government-required origin disclosure on beef and
pork at retail. That belief is out of date and has been for over a decade:
mandatory COOL for beef and pork was repealed in December 2015 specifically
because the U.S. lost a WTO trade dispute with Canada and Mexico and faced
authorized retaliatory tariffs exceeding $1 billion. What replaced it is not
a weaker mandatory rule but the *absence* of any mandatory rule for beef and
pork, paired with a voluntary claims framework (tightened in 2024, full
compliance required by January 2026) that only applies to producers who
choose to make a U.S.-origin claim. Mandatory COOL was *not* fully repealed
across the board — it remains in place for lamb, chicken, goat, venison,
seafood, and several other commodities — so "COOL was repealed" as a blanket
statement is itself an oversimplification in the other direction. The
accurate characterization is: mandatory COOL exists for some meat/food
commodities but not beef or pork, and voluntary origin claims for beef/pork
now carry a stricter (post-2024) meaning than they used to.
Source: `NOTES/COOL_Regulatory_Timeline.md`.
