# Country-of-Origin Labeling (COOL) Regulatory Timeline — Meat

Status: first pass, 2026-09-03 (fork research). Primary sources: Federal

## Era 1 — Voluntary COOL (2002–2008)

- **2002 Farm Bill (P.L. 107-171)**: Congress authorized mandatory
  country-of-origin labeling for beef, lamb, pork, fish, perishable
  agricultural commodities, and peanuts, amending the Agricultural Marketing
  Act of 1946. Implementation was delayed by Congress; AMS published
  voluntary COOL guidelines on **October 11, 2002**, and mandatory
  implementation did not begin until 2008–2009 (below).

## Era 2 — Mandatory COOL, original form (2008–2013)

- **2008 Farm Bill (P.L. 110-246, § 11002)**: Congress amended the COOL
  statute, expanding covered commodities (added chicken, goat, and additional
  perishable commodities) and adjusting requirements.
- **August 2008**: AMS published an interim final rule implementing the
  revised mandatory COOL law.
- **January 2009 / effective March 16, 2009**: AMS published the final rule;
  mandatory COOL for muscle-cut beef, pork, lamb, chicken, goat, and other
  covered commodities took effect.

## Era 3 — WTO dispute (2008–2015)

Canada and Mexico challenged mandatory COOL at the WTO as a discriminatory
technical barrier to trade that disadvantaged their livestock exports to the
U.S. (segregation/recordkeeping costs allegedly fell more heavily on
imported animals).

- **December 2008 (Canada) / June 2009 (Mexico)**: requested WTO
  consultations with the U.S.
- **October 2009 / panel established November 19, 2009**: WTO dispute panel
  formed.
- **November 18, 2011**: WTO panel ruled original COOL rule treated imported
  livestock less favorably than U.S. livestock (Art. 2.1, TBT Agreement).
- **March 23, 2012**: U.S. appealed.
- **June 29, 2012**: WTO Appellate Body upheld the discrimination finding
  (reversed one narrower sub-finding); DSB adopted the reports July 23, 2012.
- **May 23, 2013**: In an attempt to comply, USDA/AMS issued a **revised**
  COOL rule requiring labels to state where each production step (born,
  raised, slaughtered) occurred for muscle-cut meat — a more granular
  disclosure than the original rule.
- **October 20, 2014**: A WTO compliance panel found the revised rule
  *still* discriminated against imported livestock. U.S. appealed
  November 28, 2014.
- **May 18, 2015**: WTO Appellate Body again ruled against the U.S.
- **December 7, 2015**: WTO arbitration set the retaliation ceiling at
  roughly C$1.055 billion (~US$781 million) for Canada and ~US$228 million
  for Mexico — a combined figure commonly rounded to "just over $1 billion."
- **December 21, 2015**: WTO authorized Canada and Mexico to impose
  retaliatory tariffs at that level.

## Era 4 — Repeal for beef and pork (December 2015 – present)

- **December 18, 2015**: Congress repealed mandatory COOL for beef and pork
  (muscle cuts and ground product) via the **Consolidated Appropriations
  Act, 2016, Public Law 114-113** — enacted one day before the WTO's formal
  retaliation authorization, explicitly to head off the tariffs. (One
  secondary source found in this pass, the National Agricultural Law
  Center's general COOL overview page, cites this as "P.L. 114-114"; that
  appears to be a typo — govinfo.gov's own PLAW record and a separate CRS/
  trade-press search independently confirm **P.L. 114-113** as the correct
  citation. Flagged as a caught-and-corrected error, not silently fixed.)
- **March 2, 2016**: USDA/AMS published a conforming final rule in the
  Federal Register formally removing beef and pork muscle cuts, ground beef,
  and ground pork from the COOL regulations, aligning the CFR text with the
  statutory repeal.
- **What was NOT repealed**: Mandatory COOL remains in effect today for
  lamb, chicken, goat, venison, seafood (under a separate, earlier-enacted
  seafood COOL program), and for perishable agricultural commodities,
  peanuts, pecans, macadamia nuts, and ginseng. **Beef and pork are the
  commodities where mandatory, government-required origin labeling was
  removed; poultry (chicken) mandatory COOL was not repealed** — though see
  the practical caveat below.

## Era 5 — Voluntary "Product of USA" claims (2020s)

Between 2015 and 2024, "Product of USA" / "Made in the USA" was a
**voluntary** claim FSIS allowed under a looser standard: a product could
carry it if the *last substantial transformation* (e.g., processing/
repackaging) happened in the U.S. — meaning meat from an animal born and
raised abroad but slaughtered or processed domestically could still be
labeled "Product of USA." This is the specific gap the 2024 rule targeted.

- **March 18, 2024** (Federal Register document 2024-05479, 89 FR 19348):
  FSIS published a final rule, *"Voluntary Labeling of FSIS-Regulated
  Products with United States-Origin Claims."*
- **Effective May 17, 2024; mandatory compliance date January 1, 2026** for
  any establishment that wants to keep using a U.S.-origin claim.
- **What the rule requires**: A "Product of USA" or "Made in the USA" claim
  may now be used **only** for products derived from an animal **born,
  raised, slaughtered, and processed entirely in the United States** (for
  multi-ingredient products, all FSIS-regulated ingredients and other
  domestic components must meet the same standard, with a narrow exception
  for spices/flavorings). Establishments must keep documentation
  substantiating the claim (origin controls, traceability/segregation
  records, signed attestations).
- **Still voluntary, not mandatory**: this rule does not require any
  origin disclosure — it only tightens what a *voluntary* U.S.-origin claim
  can mean. A producer/processor can simply omit any origin claim and say
  nothing, which is legal.

## Bottom line: what can a consumer tell from a beef or pork label today?

- **Nothing is required.** Since the 2015 repeal, there is no mandatory
  country-of-origin statement on a package of muscle-cut or ground beef or
  pork sold at U.S. retail. A package with no origin claim at all could
  contain domestic, imported, or blended-origin meat, and the consumer
  cannot tell from the label.
- **If** a package voluntarily says "Product of USA" or "Made in the USA"
  **and** was produced under the post-January 2026 standard, that claim now
  does mean born/raised/slaughtered/processed entirely in the U.S. Before
  the 2026 compliance date, and for any product not making the claim, no
  such assurance exists.
- Poultry (chicken) technically remains under mandatory COOL by statute, but
  this pass did not verify current FSIS enforcement practice or how
  meaningful that mandate is in practice given the U.S. imports very little
  chicken relative to domestic production — flagged as **unresolved**, not
  yet checked against current FSIS retail labeling guidance. Follow-up item
  for a later pass.
