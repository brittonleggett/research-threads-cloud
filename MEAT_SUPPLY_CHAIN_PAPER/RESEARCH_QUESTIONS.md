# Research Questions

Status: draft, 2026-09-03. Not locked — sub-questions will be trimmed or
reframed once `NOTES/Research_Problem_Exploration.md` and the evidence base
mature.

## Motivating question
How do foreign sourcing, meatpacking/processing concentration, labeling
practices, and supply-chain power interact to influence the prices received
by U.S. producers and the prices paid by U.S. consumers — in beef, pork, and
poultry specifically?

## Empirical sub-questions (economics/policy layer — feed Study 3 / fact-check)
1. Is there measurable divergence between producer (farm-gate) prices and
   retail consumer prices over time, in each commodity, and is it growing?
2. What share of the retail food dollar do farmers/ranchers receive, and how
   has that share changed, per commodity?
3. What is the current and historical level of processing concentration
   (CR4/HHI) in beef packing, pork processing, and poultry processing, and
   how defined ("market")?
4. Do processor and/or retailer margins expand disproportionately during
   periods of elevated food inflation or supply disruption?
5. Is price transmission asymmetric (retail prices rise faster than they
   fall in response to farm-price changes) in these commodities?
6. What are the volumes, values, sourcing countries, and product categories
   (live animals vs. lean trim vs. commodity cuts vs. premium cuts vs.
   processed product) of U.S. beef, pork, and poultry imports, and how have
   they changed?
7. Do imports measurably suppress domestic producer prices, lower retail
   prices, both, or neither — and does the answer differ by commodity and by
   import category (e.g., imported lean trim blended into ground beef vs.
   imported finished cuts)?
8. Why does the U.S. simultaneously import and export beef (and, where
   applicable, pork/poultry)?
9. How have country-of-origin labeling (COOL) rules for meat changed, and
   what can a "Product of USA" / "Made in USA" / "Raised in USA" claim
   currently mean under USDA/FSIS rules — can consumers currently determine
   whether meat came from an animal born, raised, slaughtered, and processed
   domestically?

## Marketing-layer questions (candidate framings — see `THEORY_CANDIDATES.md`
and `NOTES/Marketing_Contribution_Options.md`; none selected yet)
10. When consumers are given (or denied) information about origin, producer
    compensation, or processor/retailer margins, does that change perceived
    price fairness, trust, or perceived corporate opportunism?
11. Does country-of-origin information interact with corporate explanations
    for price increases (cost-based vs. margin-based, foreign-competition-
    based, etc.) to shape consumer judgments of legitimacy?
12. How do supply-chain actors (processors, USDA, producer associations,
    retailers) attribute responsibility for price changes and justify
    outcomes for producers vs. consumers in public discourse — and does that
    attribution pattern predict/relate to consumer perceptions above?
13. Do consumers care about *who* captures value in the chain (farmer vs.
    processor vs. retailer vs. importer), independent of the price they pay?

## Explicit non-assumptions
- We do not assume corporations are engaged in price gouging.
- We do not assume farmers are being exploited.
- We do not assume imports help or hurt producers/consumers — direction and
  magnitude are empirical questions, and may differ by commodity and import
  category.
- We do not assume beef, pork, and poultry share the same structure or
  dynamics — see `NOTES/Commodity_Structure_Comparison.md`.
