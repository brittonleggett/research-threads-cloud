## 1. Sun & Moon (2025) — beef price fairness × freshness × information overload

**Full citation:** Sun, K.-A., & Moon, J. (2025). "Exploring the antecedents and consequences of
perceived fairness in beef pricing: The moderating role of freshness under conditions of
information overload." *Foods*, 14(11), 1844. https://doi.org/10.3390/foods14111844 (open access,
MDPI).

**Design:** Online survey, Clickworker panel, 415 U.S. consumers, fielded October 1-7, 2024
(68.7% female, median age 30-49, majority ate beef 1-2x/week).

**Constructs:** Organic perception → price fairness and revisit intention; freshness as a
moderator of the organic→price-fairness link, all under an "information overload" theoretical
frame (too many simultaneous product cues complicate the fairness judgment).

**Key finding:** Organic perception raises perceived price fairness and revisit intention; but
combining organic *and* freshness messaging together *reduces* perceived price fairness relative
to organic messaging alone — the authors read this as cue overload increasing perceived price
burden, not integrated positively.

**Relevance:** Directly on-topic (beef, U.S. consumers, price fairness as a named construct,
2024-fielded/2025-published) but a different antecedent (organic/freshness cues, not COO
disclosure or corporate price-explanation type) and a different theoretical frame (information
overload, not attribution/signaling theory). Published in a food-science journal, not a marketing
journal.

## 2. Sun & Moon (2025) — beef quality, trust, revisit intention, price fairness as moderator

**Full citation:** Sun, K.-A., & Moon, J. (2025). "Structural relationship between beef food
quality, trust, and revisit intention: The moderating role of price fairness based on heuristics
effect." *Nutrients*, 17(13), 2155. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17132155 (open access, MDPI).

**Design:** Same underlying data collection as #1 above (415 U.S. consumers, Clickworker,
same demographic split) — a companion analysis of the same dataset, not an independent sample.
Covariance-based SEM (maximum likelihood), not PLS-SEM; moderation tested via Hayes' PROCESS
Model 15.

**Constructs:** Four beef-quality dimensions (price fairness, freshness, portion size, packaging)
→ trust → revisit intention, with **price fairness as a moderator** (not a mediator, notably —
the opposite structural role it plays in most price-fairness literature, including this project's
own current design) between freshness→revisit-intention and trust→revisit-intention.

**Key finding:** All four quality dimensions positively predict trust; freshness is the strongest
predictor of revisit intention; price fairness moderates two of the paths in a "heuristic" pattern
consistent with "expensive means better" for a premium product like beef — i.e., *lower* perceived
price fairness (a cheaper-seeming price) can *reduce* revisit intention, the reverse of the
usual price-fairness-as-good-thing logic.

**Relevance:** Worth flagging for the manuscript's literature review specifically because it uses
price fairness as a **moderator**, not a mediator/outcome — a genuinely different structural role
than this project's own price-fairness-as-mediator design. If Britton's Study 2 design keeps price
fairness as a mediator (the current lean), this paper is worth citing as a reason to justify that
choice explicitly (against a plausible alternative structural role) rather than assuming mediation
is the only defensible position in the literature.

## 3. Casteran (2024) — cost transparency, price fairness, Fairtrade, construal level

**Full citation:** Casteran, G. (2024). "The central role of price fairness for cost transparency
strategy: What is the effect of conventional versus Fairtrade products and construal level?"
*Recherche et Applications en Marketing* (English ed.). https://doi.org/10.1177/20515707241256096
(SAGE; abstract/summary read via search-engine synthesis and the journal's own abstract page —
WebFetch was blocked (403) on the full article page, so this entry rests on the abstract only,
not full text — flagging that limitation explicitly).

**Design:** Two online experiments (per the abstract; participant counts/exact manipulations not
independently confirmed — full-text access needed for that).

**Constructs:** Cost transparency (revealing a price's cost components) → price fairness →
(implied) downstream behavior, tested for conventional vs. Fairtrade products, with consumer
construal level (abstract vs. concrete thinking) as a moderator. The abstract explicitly frames
this as filling two gaps: price fairness's mediating role is under-tested in the cost-transparency
literature, and no prior work had tested Fairtrade vs. conventional products or construal-level
moderation together.

**Relevance:** The single closest structural analog found tonight to this project's own core chain
(transparency → price fairness → outcome) published in an actual marketing journal (not
food-science) — genuinely useful as a "this chain is publishable in a real marketing venue"
existence proof, and RAM (Recherche et Applications en Marketing) is a legitimate, French
AMA-affiliated marketing journal, not a predatory outlet. But it is general retail/Fairtrade, not
meat, not COO-specific, and doesn't touch corporate price-explanation type or channel-power
framing. **Needs a full-text read before citing specific findings** — only the abstract-level
description above is verified tonight.

## Gap read (the point of this scan)

Nobody found tonight has published this project's specific combination — COO disclosure ×
corporate price-explanation type as antecedents of price-fairness attribution, in a *meat*
context, in a *marketing* (not food-science or general-retail) journal, with channel-power/
value-distribution transparency as the framing device. The two closest beef-specific studies
(Sun & Moon, both 2025) are food-science-journal, organic/freshness-focused, not COO/corporate-
explanation-focused. The one closest marketing-journal study (Casteran 2024) is transparency/
price-fairness-focused but general retail, not meat/COO. This is **a genuine, still-open gap** on
tonight's evidence — but this was a targeted single-night search, not a systematic review, and
MDPI food-science journals (where both Sun & Moon pieces appeared) publish quickly, so this
clearance should be treated as current-as-of-tonight, not permanent. Worth a fuller, more
systematic lit-search pass (Web of Science / Scopus-style coverage, not just WebSearch) before
this gap claim goes into an actual manuscript's contribution statement.
