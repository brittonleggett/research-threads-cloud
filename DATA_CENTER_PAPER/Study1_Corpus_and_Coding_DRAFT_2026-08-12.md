## Phase 0.5 — Brief

- **RQ:** How do Louisiana communities and public actors discursively frame their
  responses to hyperscale data center development, and what typology of opposition
  (and support) frames emerges that could ground a Study 2 design?
- **Theory frame (candidates, not locked — see orientation note):** distributive/
  environmental justice (cumulative burden on already-disadvantaged communities);
  procedural justice (exclusion from decision-making, secrecy); institutional
  trust/skepticism toward corporate cost promises. Psychological reactance and
  risk-perception frames remain plausible but are less directly evidenced in the
  corpus below than the justice-based frames — worth downgrading to secondary
  unless Phase 3 review says otherwise.
- **Data type:** public news coverage (with embedded resident/official/advocate
  quotes), public meeting records (city council), advocacy organization statements.
  Not survey/interview data, not human subjects directly.
- **Geographic focus, deliberate:** Louisiana-specific, not a generic national
  sample — matches Britton's stated goal of positioning this work around subjects
  that matter to Louisiana specifically, not just data centers as an abstract
  national trend.

## Phase 1 — Inductive codes

**1. The Lens (churches, Richland Parish)** — White congregations (Pastor Justin
Clark, First Baptist Rayville) frame the project as a "God-given opportunity,"
organize meal service for construction workers. Black congregations (Pastor Mark
Sledge, Macedonia Baptist — founded 1879) are protective/cautious: Deacon Glen Brown
warns "crime and addiction will rise" without local investment; alderperson Consuela
Hunter documents rising costs ("groceries, gas, electricity — everything going up").
Hard economic data cited: median household income $63K (majority-white areas) vs.
$37K (majority-Black, e.g. Rayville); farmland value $20K/acre vs. $3.2K state
average; median home price up 80% since early 2024, now over $270K; only 68% of
Richland residents have internet access vs. 85% statewide.
→ Codes: `racialized-benefit-distribution`, `religious-legitimation-framing`,
`protective-skepticism-framing`, `displacement-cost-of-living`, `digital-divide-irony`

**2–3. KNOE (road/traffic, residents split)** — Sheriff Neal Harwell: "we're in
uncharted territory" — round-the-clock truck traffic straining rural roads. Joyce
Piercy (81): trailer-park residents displaced, "some of them couldn't afford" to
relocate. Mike Steele, on local rent profiteering: "I don't see how they can sleep at
night." Coverage explicitly frames the community as split, not uniformly opposed.
→ Codes: `infrastructure-strain`, `displacement-narrative`, `moral-outrage-profiteering`,
`ambivalent-split-community`

**4, 6. Fox8 / NOLA.com (boomtown narratives)** — "$27 billion AI data center is
transforming rural Louisiana" / "It's changing fast" — rapid-transformation framing,
economic-opportunity emphasis, less critical in tone than the local-news pieces.
→ Codes: `rapid-transformation-narrative`, `economic-opportunity-framing`

**5. Louisiana Radio Network (utility-cost advocacy)** — Alaina DiLaura (Alliance for
Affordable Energy): explicit distrust of Meta/Entergy cost-coverage promises — "where
these data centers are operational... often those costs are shifted to residents";
flags a contract-duration mismatch (plants last 20+ years, cost-coverage agreements
may not); "because we share the grid, that means some of those costs are also going
to be shared by us." Monroe Mayor Friday Ellis deflects direct accountability to the
Public Service Commission, but concedes future data centers will "have to make
provisions and give reassurance to communities."
→ Codes: `institutional-distrust`, `cost-externalization-fear`,
`regulatory-responsibility-diffusion`, `demand-for-accountability`

**8. Louisiana Illuminator / DeSmog (statewide policy)** — NDAs kept residents and
some officials unaware of the Meta/Entergy deal until an October 2024 filing surfaced
publicly. Meta projected to receive up to $3.6B in sales-tax exemptions over a
decade; Entergy separately seeking to avoid $237M in local property taxes via PILOT
deals. 24+ AI-related bills filed in the Louisiana legislature this session.
→ Codes: `procedural-exclusion-secrecy`, `extractive-tax-framing`,
`legislative-backlash`

**9–11. Verite News / WWNO / Axios (New Orleans East)** — Advocates explicitly invoke
cumulative burden: the neighborhood has "long shouldered a disproportionate share of
industrial and environmental burden." Council President JP Morrell: the city needs to
"figure out what the hell a data center is in the law" — data centers aren't even
defined in the zoning ordinance. Proposed site sits in a residential neighborhood with
"deep roots" in the city's African American and Vietnamese American communities.
Result: council 6-0 vote for a one-year moratorium (Jan 2026), later coverage frames
this as part of a broader statewide "AI backlash" (Jul 2026).
→ Codes: `environmental-justice-cumulative-burden`, `place-attachment-identity`,
`regulatory-void`, `collective-political-mobilization`

## Phase 2 — Candidate themes (draft thematic map — needs your Phase 3 review)

**Theme 1: Distributive/environmental injustice** (The Lens, Verite/WWNO) —
burdens (cost-of-living increases, industrial/environmental exposure) and benefits
(land value gains, investment narrative) are perceived to fall along existing racial
and economic lines, in both the rural (Richland Parish) and urban (New Orleans East)
cases independently. The strongest, most consistently evidenced theme in this corpus
— not a theoretical reach, directly stated by named sources in both locations.

**Theme 2: Procedural exclusion** (Illuminator/DeSmog, Verite/WWNO) — NDA secrecy,
regulatory/zoning gaps, decisions made before communities had a chance to weigh in.
**Candidate primary Study 2 manipulation axis** — parallels Tariff's Theme 2
(causation attribution) in being the dimension with the clearest theoretical
mapping (procedural justice theory) and clear real-world variation (secretive deal
vs. the public, contested New Orleans process).

**Theme 3: Institutional distrust of cost promises** (Louisiana Radio Network) —
specific skepticism that company/utility assurances will hold over a facility's full
operational lifetime; who actually bears deferred/hidden costs.

**Theme 4: Displacement and cost-of-living disruption** (KNOE, The Lens) — rent and
home-price surges, direct displacement of lower-income residents, moral-outrage
framing toward perceived local profiteering (distinct from the corporate actor —
this frame targets landlords/opportunists in the community, not Meta directly).

**Theme 5: Ambivalent economic-opportunity framing** (Fox8, NOLA.com, The Lens's
white-church material, KNOE's "residents split" framing) — a genuine, real
counter-current, not just opposition: boomtown narrative, religious/mission framing,
jobs/investment enthusiasm. **Important to keep and report honestly** — this corpus
does not support a uniform "everyone hates data centers" claim, and a paper that
pretends otherwise would misrepresent its own evidence.

**Theme 6 (arguably an outcome, not a theme — flag for Phase 3 to decide):**
Collective political mobilization — individual grumbling escalating to institutional
action (the New Orleans moratorium, 24 state bills). This may belong in Study 2 as a
dependent variable (support for regulation/moratorium, political action intention)
rather than as a Study 1 theme.
