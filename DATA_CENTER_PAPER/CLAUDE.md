## Scope note — FINAL, locked 2026-09-10 (supersedes the 08-17 note below)
Britton locked the multi-state comparative design 2026-09-10, resolving a
CLAUDE.md/README inconsistency that had been sitting open since 08-17. His own words:

> Lock in the multi-state comparative design. Louisiana remains the primary/deep-dive
> case (Tier 1), with Georgia, Utah, Virginia, and Arizona as Tier 2 comparative cases.
> The purpose of Tier 2 is not to create five equally exhaustive state studies; it is
> to test the transferability and boundary conditions of the themes emerging from
> Louisiana. Keep region and community racial/socioeconomic profile as explicit
> comparative/contextual moderators where the evidence supports them. For Phase 3,
> prioritize comprehensive coding of the Louisiana corpus and use the Tier 2 material
> strategically for cross-case comparison, negative cases, replication of themes, and
> identification of state/community-specific themes. Do not automatically expand
> Tier 2 into exhaustive artifact collection unless the emerging analysis shows that
> we need it.

**Practical implications:**
- **Tier 1 (Louisiana)** gets Phase 3's primary coding attention — this is where
  comprehensive, exhaustive theme development happens.
- **Tier 2 (GA/UT/VA/AZ)** is used strategically, not exhaustively — for testing
  whether Louisiana's themes hold, finding negative/disconfirming cases, and
  surfacing state-specific variants. Do not keep growing Tier 2 artifact-by-artifact
  as a default; only add material when the analysis itself creates a specific need.
- Region and community racial/socioeconomic profile stay as moderators, but only
  where the evidence in hand actually supports the claim — not applied uniformly by
  default.
- **Note on IN and MS**: since the 08-17 note below was written, two more artifacts
  were added and primary-source verified outside the original GA/UT/VA/AZ four —
  Clinton County, IN (a rezoning-denial win) and the NAACP v. X.AI Corp. federal case
  in Southaven, MS. Both function the same way Tier 2 is meant to (boundary/
  transferability tests, not exhaustive coverage), so they're being treated as Tier 2
  under this same logic rather than triggering a fresh scope question. Flagging this
  here for visibility, not asking for separate sign-off — reopen it if that's wrong.

## Scope note (2026-08-17, historical — see FINAL note above)
Originally scoped Louisiana-only by design (see "Real-world grounding" below). A
2026-08-16 national scan found comparable, sourced opposition material in Georgia,
Utah, Virginia, and Arizona, with real regional variation (resource-scarcity framing
in the West, aesthetic/NIMBY framing in wealthy VA exurbs vs. racialized distributive-
injustice framing in LA/GA). Britton approved restructuring around this 2026-08-17 —
Louisiana stays the anchor/deepest case, but the paper is now a multi-state
comparative design with regional resource profile and community socioeconomic/racial
profile as explicit moderators, not a Louisiana-only study. See
`Study1_Corpus_and_Coding_DRAFT_2026-08-17_national-restructure.md`.

## Real-world grounding (facts gathered 2026-08-12, see notes/ for sources)
Three Louisiana projects anchor this: Meta's "Hyperion" hyperscale data center
in Richland Parish (needs 10 new gas plants, water use nearly doubling the
parish's total withdrawals), an Amazon project in Caddo Parish, and a smaller,
now-dead proposal (MS Solar Grid Data) in New Orleans East that triggered a
city council vote. New Orleans City Council passed a one-year moratorium on
new data center development (6-0 vote, Jan 2026) after residents raised
environmental-justice, water, and secrecy concerns. At least 11 states have
passed data center moratoriums; Louisiana legislators filed 24+ AI-related
bills this session.
