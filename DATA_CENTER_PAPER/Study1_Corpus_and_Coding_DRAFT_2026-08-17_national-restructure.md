## Phase 0.5 — Brief (revised)

- **RQ (revised):** How do communities and public actors across different U.S. regions
  discursively frame opposition (and support) to hyperscale data center development,
  and which frames generalize nationally versus vary systematically with regional
  resource profile and community socioeconomic/racial composition?
- **What changed from the 2026-08-12 RQ:** the original framed this as Louisiana-
  specific by design. The 2026-08-16 national scan found the core justice/distrust/
  mobilization frames recur across states, but *not* uniformly — grievance content and
  opposition venue both vary in ways that look like real moderators, not noise. That's
  a stronger, more publishable claim than either "LA is unique" or "it's the same
  everywhere," so the RQ now asks the generalizability question directly.
- **Theory frame:** same core as before — distributive/environmental justice,
  procedural justice, institutional trust/skepticism — now explicitly tested against
  two candidate moderators (below) rather than assumed uniform.
- **Geographic scope (revised):** Louisiana remains the anchor case — deepest corpus,
  already Phase-1-coded, three distinct site types (rural/Meta, urban/New Orleans,
  suburban/Amazon). It now sits inside a broader multi-state comparative corpus rather
  than standing alone. This supersedes the 2026-08-12 draft's "Louisiana-specific, not
  a generic national sample" framing and the `CLAUDE.md` brief's original geographic-
  focus note — both written before the scan found real, sourced material elsewhere.

## Restructured thematic map

**What stays the same (the generalizable spine — recurs across LA, GA, UT, VA, AZ):**

- **Theme 1 — Distributive/environmental injustice.** Unchanged core claim. GA's
  Stanton Springs case (discolored water, documented income gap between majority-white
  and majority-Black areas near the site) is a near-exact structural match to LA's
  Richland Parish material — this is the strongest evidence yet that Theme 1 isn't an
  LA artifact.
- **Theme 2 — Procedural exclusion/secrecy**, now with a named sub-mechanism:
  **`regulatory-venue-shifting`** (new code) — a developer blocked by one governmental
  body routes around it through a friendlier one. AZ's Project Blue (Tucson Council
  blocked water access → developer got a power deal from the Corporation Commission
  instead, proceeded via air-cooling) is structurally identical to two things already
  in the LA corpus: Amazon's Caddo council overriding its own planning commission (#15),
  and Meta's PSC overriding its own ALJ's document-production order (#12). Three
  independent states, same mechanism — this is a stronger, more precise claim than
  folding everything into generic "procedural exclusion," and it ties directly to
  regulatory-capture theory if you want that citation anchor.
- **Theme 3 — Institutional distrust of cost/safety promises.** Unchanged; GA's
  ratepayer-cost fight and UT's disputed "net-zero water use" claim both fit directly.
- **Theme 5 — Ambivalent economic-opportunity framing.** Unchanged as a genuine
  counter-current; no new state contradicts this, though none of the Tier 2 material
  adds a strong new instance of it either — worth watching whether it holds as
  evidence expands, not just assuming it does.
- **Theme 6, recast as a candidate DV (strengthened, not just flagged now):**
  collective mobilization escalating to formal institutional channels. Now has
  cross-state instances beyond LA's moratorium/litigation: AZ's Tucson annexation
  block, VA's Loudoun pause, NY's statewide executive order — plus Tier 3's Clinton
  County, IN denial if it verifies. Treating "did opposition escalate, and how far"
  as a measurable Study 2 outcome is more defensible now than it was with only the
  LA cases to point to.

**New — moderators, not themes (this is the actual restructuring):**

1. **Regional resource-scarcity profile.** Water/drought-scarcity framing dominates
   AZ and UT (`resource-scarcity-commons` code) in a way it doesn't in LA or GA, where
   distributive/racial-injustice framing dominates instead. Not a contradiction of
   Theme 1 — a moderator explaining *which* grievance content shows up where, tied to
   each region's actual resource constraints.
2. **Community racial/socioeconomic profile.** LA and GA cases (majority-Black,
   working-class, historically industrially-burdened communities) produce EJ/
   distributive-injustice framing. VA's Loudoun County case (wealthy DC exurb) produces
   almost none of that — instead a classic aesthetic/noise/property-value NIMBY frame
   (`aesthetic-noise-property-value-framing`, new code). Same underlying mechanism
   (large industrial land use imposed on a residential area), different grievance
   vocabulary depending on who's affected. This is the clearest single piece of
   evidence that a national version of this paper needs community profile built in as
   a moderator, not treated as a control variable to average away.
3. **Institutional venue as opposition-efficacy moderator (secondary candidate,
   weaker evidence so far).** In LA, formal opposition mostly lost (PSC upheld
   secrecy 3-1, Caddo commission's transparency measures failed). Clinton County, IN's
   3-0 rezoning denial and NY's statewide moratorium suggest local zoning boards and
   state legislatures/executives may be more responsive than state utility regulators
   — but IN is still Tier 3 (unverified), so this is a "watch for it," not yet a
   confident claim.

**GA-specific note not yet fitted into the map:** Stanton Springs coverage included an
acute health/contamination framing (discolored tap water, air pollution proximity)
that reads as more visceral than LA's version of Theme 1. Provisionally treating it as
an intensity variant of Theme 1 rather than a 7th theme, but flagging for your Phase 3
call — could go either way.
