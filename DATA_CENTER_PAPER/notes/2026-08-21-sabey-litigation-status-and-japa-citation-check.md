## 2. `federal-national-security-override` candidate code — JAPA citation checked out as real, but it's a **partial** fit, not a direct case-study match; independently corroborated the DOJ/xAI finding itself

### 2a. The JAPA article is real, correctly identified, and by a credible author — but check what it actually argues before citing it as if it studies *this* case

Verified beyond 08-19's search-summary level:
- **Real article, real journal, real author.** "Planning Under Preemption: State Power and Local
  Authority in the AI Data Center Era," by **Justin Kollar** (PhD, MIT Dept. of Urban Studies and
  Planning / Leventhal Center for Advanced Urbanism; AICP; prior Harvard GSD and Michigan
  Taubman degrees), *Journal of the American Planning Association*, **Vol. 92, No. 2 (2026), pp.
  326–332**, published online Feb 18, 2026. DOI matches exactly:
  10.1080/01944363.2026.2618221. Kollar has a Google Scholar profile, a DUSP faculty/people page,
  and other tandfonline-published work (e.g., a *Contemporary Social Science* piece on
  techno-statecraft and Arizona semiconductor fabs) — this is a real, credentialed academic, not
  a low-quality or predatory-journal hit. [Article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01944363.2026.2618221), [MIT DUSP page](https://dusp.mit.edu/people/justin-kollar), [Author's site](https://www.justinmkollar.com/), [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=TC_rfnAAAAAJ&hl=en)
- **What the article actually argues, per multiple independent search-summary hits (still not
  fetched/read in full):** its core throughline is **state**-level legislative preemption
  narrowing **local** planning authority (zoning, environmental review) over AI/data-center
  infrastructure — "statutory overrides, utility governance, fiscal incentives, and procedural
  constraints" shifting power "upward to state agencies, utilities, and corporate actors."
  National security and "global competition" are named as part of the *justifying rationale* for
  this preemption wave, alongside the framing that local control is "a core ideal of American
  planning" that in practice has always been contingent on state permission. It draws on cases
  "from Virginia to Arizona."
- **A separately-relevant federal thread the article connects to (or at least search coverage
  connects to it):** a **December 2025 Executive Order, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for
  Artificial Intelligence,"** directing preparation of a federal legislative push to preempt state
  and local AI laws, including "generally applicable permitting reforms" for AI data-center
  infrastructure specifically. That's a real, distinct federal-preemption data point worth its own
  look for the paper's federal-level thread generally, separate from the JAPA citation question.

**The fit assessment, stated plainly:** this is a legitimate, well-grounded academic citation for
the broader claim that "national-security/AI-competitiveness framing is being used to narrow
local authority over data-center siting" — but **no search-summary evidence found that the
article specifically discusses the MS/TN xAI/Colossus DOJ-intervention case**, and its central
empirical mechanism (state legislatures preempting local zoning/environmental review) is
meaningfully different from what actually happened in the corpus instance (a **federal executive-
branch agency intervening directly in ongoing private litigation** on national-security grounds —
no state legislature or preemption statute involved at all). If this code survives Phase 3, the
honest framing is "Kollar (2026) documents the broader pattern of national-security/competitiveness
framing narrowing local planning authority, of which the MS/TN DOJ intervention is one instance" —
not "Kollar (2026) analyzes this case." Worth being precise about that distinction before it goes
into a manuscript; a reviewer familiar with the JAPA piece would notice if it were cited as if it
were a case study of the DOJ intervention specifically. No copyrighted full text pulled or stored
here — citation only, consistent with the repo's no-paywalled-PDF rule.
