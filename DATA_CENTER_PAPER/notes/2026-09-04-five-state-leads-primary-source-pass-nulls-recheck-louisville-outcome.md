## 1. Five new state leads — verification results

### (a) Pennsylvania — UPGRADED to full primary-source confidence
Downloaded the actual executive order PDF directly from the official pa.gov
domain (not a WebFetch summary of a news article — the raw government-hosted
document, read page-by-page):
https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/governor/documents/eo2026_05_protecting%20pennsylvania%20consumers%20from%20data%20center%20impacts_final_executed.pdf

Confirmed: **Executive Order 2026-05, "Protecting Pennsylvania Consumers from
Data Center Impacts,"** signed by Gov. Josh Shapiro, dated August 18, 2026, 8
pages. Full text now in hand. Key facts not in the 09-03 note:
- GRID Requirements were first *announced* Feb 3, 2026, with detailed standards
  released May 27, 2026 — this EO doesn't invent GRID, it makes it binding via
  DEP's permitting process (Consent Order and Agreement template) for any
  project >25 MW peak demand.
- DEP had received permit applications for **20** proposed data center
  facilities as of the EO's effective date, against "over 100" reported
  proposals; 14 existing/in-development sites hold active Computer Data Center
  Equipment Program tax exemptions.
- Concrete ratepayer figures cited in the EO's own text: PJM's last four base
  residual capacity auctions assigned data centers **$29.4B (46%)** of total
  auction costs; the administration's negotiated price cap has "saved
  consumers over $30 billion" versus uncapped pricing.
- Operative provisions: DEP review process gated on a signed Consent Order and
  Agreement; NDAs banned for all agencies under the Governor's jurisdiction;
  annual public energy/water consumption reporting required of operating data
  centers starting July 1, 2027; data centers curtailed *before* residential/
  business customers during grid emergencies; all data center projects
  permanently removed from the PA Permit Fast Track Program; DCED tasked with
  model community-benefit-agreement/zoning best practices for municipalities.
- No enforcement penalty provision and no independent legal force beyond
  agency permitting discretion — Section 6 explicitly states the EO "does not
  create any right or benefit... enforceable at law or in equity by any party."

### (b) Tennessee (Nashville) — UPGRADED to full primary-source confidence
Downloaded and read the actual filed complaint (PDF, 37 pages) via a
Nashville TV-station-hosted court-document link:
https://www.wkrn.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2026/08/DC-Blox-v-Metro-Nashville.pdf

Confirmed: **DCB Nashville, LLC v. The Metropolitan Government of Nashville
and Davidson County**, Case No. **3:26-cv-01143**, U.S. District Court for the
Middle District of Tennessee, Nashville Division, **filed 08/11/26**. Claim:
Complaint for Declaratory and Injunctive Relief and Damages under 42 U.S.C. §
1983, jury demand. Facts confirmed directly from the complaint text:
- The moratorium is **Ordinance No. BL2026-1448**, signed by the Mayor **July
  24, 2026** (Metro Council passed it July 21 per prior notes — consistent).
- A separate, related ordinance — **BL2026-1391** — imposed new data-center
  zoning standards, effective **July 31, 2026**.
- DC BLOX's theory: its predecessor tenant (Asurion) had a lawful preexisting
  data-center/office use on the property (Tenn. Code Ann. § 13-7-208), and DC
  BLOX's own May 29, 2026 building-permit applications had vested under Tenn.
  Code Ann. § 13-4-310 — Metro's own Zoning Administrator allegedly determined
  this in writing before the moratorium passed.
- Alleges the moratorium was "materially expanded on third reading" to also
  reach administrative appeals and to push its end date to Dec 1, 2026,
  without re-referral to the Planning Commission — and that Council used
  "every tool" and "every avenue" to target this specific project, i.e., a
  federal equal-protection/due-process/regulatory-takings claim (5th & 14th
  Amendments), not just a generic zoning dispute.
- Relief sought: declaratory judgment voiding the ordinances as applied,
  injunctive relief, compensatory damages, attorney fees under 42 U.S.C. §
  1988.

This confirms and sharpens the 09-03 summary — the $23M Grassmere Road
purchase detail from 09-03 wasn't visible in the pages read but the core
claims, dates, and ordinance numbers are now primary-source-confirmed.

**Outcome:** Commissioners voted **5-3** to send the revised, stricter

Commission members met at the Old Jail Building on Thursday, Aug. 20" — as

September 3, 2026** — consistent with the Commission's known 1st/3rd-Thursday
meeting schedule, and Sept 3, 2026 is in fact a Thursday.)

Confirmed provisions in the adopted recommendation, matching and finalizing
what 09-02/09-03 had as secondhand: near-universal rezoning requirement
(enabling public comment + Metro Council final say), mandatory closed-loop
cooling, quarter-mile spacing from other data centers/nuisance uses, and the
500,000 sq ft hyperscale size cap retained. Quoted reactions: Commissioner Jim
Mims said the commission "captured a lot of the essence" of public concern;
No Data Center 502 activist Dani Ray called it "a step forward, but it's not
enough" and said her group will escalate to Metro Council.

**Next step, now dated:** Metro Council's Planning and Zoning Committee meets
**September 15, 2026, 1pm, City Hall** to take this up; committee chair
Andrew Owen (D-9) indicated a second meeting may be needed. LPM notes
committee meetings typically run under an hour with no public-comment period
— public input is being funneled to emailing Council members directly.
