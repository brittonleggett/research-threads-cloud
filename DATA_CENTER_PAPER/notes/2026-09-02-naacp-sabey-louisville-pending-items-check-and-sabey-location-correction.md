## 1. NAACP v. xAI — unchanged (pending)

*Mississippi State Conference NAACP / National NAACP v. xAI Corp. and MZX Tech, LLC*, filed April 14,
2026, U.S. District Court for the **Northern District of Mississippi** (Chief Judge Debra Brown) —
not Tennessee; the facility ("Colossus 2") is in Southaven, MS, just south of Memphis, TN, which is
why press shorthand calls it "the Memphis data center." Allegation: unpermitted methane gas turbines
(27, reportedly growing to ~59-60) operated without a Clean Air Act permit near homes/schools/churches
in an already-nonattainment airshed. Represented by Southern Environmental Law Center and Earthjustice.

DOJ filed a motion June 15, 2026 to intervene and dismiss the whole case, arguing (1) national-security
grounds (xAI's compute allegedly supports Pentagon-linked operations) and (2) that the executive branch
has exclusive Article II authority to terminate Clean Air Act citizen suits. No ruling on that motion
has been found as of today — this remains a fully-briefed, high-stakes motion sitting undecided. No
PACER access was available to rule out a very recent unreported ruling, so this is a search-based
negative result, not a docket-confirmed one.

## 2. Sabey/Decatur (Indiana) — unchanged on the legal track, one new PR item

Indianapolis's Metropolitan Development Commission approved the project March 18, 2026 via a **use
variance** rather than full rezoning. Seven Decatur Township residents filed a judicial-review
petition April 17, 2026 in Marion Superior Court 5, arguing the variance route was a "disguised
rezoning" that bypassed City-County Council approval, and sought an injunction pending the case.
Contemporaneous reporting estimated the case could take months to a year. No ruling or further docket
movement found since April — consistent with prior "unchanged" notes.

New, non-legal: on **August 27, 2026** Sabey issued a company press release ("Sabey Data Centers
Outlines Community Commitments") — $5M for area road improvements, $25M toward a new aquatic center,
local-hiring commitments (75 permanent jobs, ~$104k avg). This is a developer PR/community-relations
move, not a court or regulatory action; it doesn't resolve the judicial-review case, but it's a
corpus-relevant data point on the developer-mitigation-offer pattern this project already tracks
elsewhere (cf. TX/KY litigation notes).

## 3. Louisville Planning Commission, Sept 3 — venue/date confirmed, agenda content is secondhand only

Confirmed: two Planning Commission meetings on the calendar for **September 3, 2026** (1:00 PM and a
5:00 PM Special Meeting, Old Jail Auditorium). Per Louisville Public Media (Aug 20, 2026): the
Commission rejected an earlier draft of the data-center zoning text amendment (LDC 25-LDC-0014) that
day and directed revisions, with plans to finalize recommended regulations at the Sept 3 meeting
before forwarding to Metro Council. Provisions reportedly under discussion: near-universal rezoning
requirement for new data centers (vs. by-right approval), mandatory closed-loop cooling, mandatory
heat diversion from adjacent properties, quarter-mile spacing from existing data centers/nuisance
uses, and a retained cap barring facilities over 500,000 sq ft. This is downstream of the 180-day
moratorium Metro Council already passed 24-1 on Aug 13, 2026 — Sept 3 is about the permanent zoning
framework the moratorium bought time to write, not a moratorium vote itself.
