## Indiana — Clinton County (confirmed, richer than the draft's one-line summary)

**3-0 vote, January 20, 2026.** Clinton County Commissioners denied a rezoning request (715
acres, agricultural → light industrial) for a data center proposed by Logix Reality LLC, despite
the county's own Area Plan Commission having recommended approval two weeks earlier (Jan 6).
Stated reasons: negative impact to area property owners and the petitioner's failure to
negotiate 14 requested commitments. Under county rule, the same rezoning request can't be
resubmitted for 12 months.
- Sources: [Inside INdiana Business](https://www.insideindianabusiness.com/articles/rezoning-request-for-clinton-county-data-center-denied), [WTHR](https://www.wthr.com/article/news/local/clinton-county-commissioners-reject-data-center-project-over-growing-concerns-rezoning-plan-proposal/531-30cc9c05-69e5-4fb6-b510-78929ce6751b), [Fox59](https://fox59.com/news/more-negative-than-positive-clinton-county-commissioners-reject-data-center-project/)
- Still the clearest outright opposition **win** at the local-zoning-board level in the whole
  corpus — strengthens the "institutional venue" moderator candidate (#3 in the restructure
  draft) specifically for county commissions vs. state utility regulators.

## Indiana — Indianapolis eastside (DC Blox, Irvington/Warren Township): confirmed, and there's a second Indianapolis case plus a live moratorium vote

**July 15, 2026:** Indianapolis Metropolitan Development Commission approved (6-1) a
scaled-back DC Blox data center campus in Warren Township, despite months of organized
opposition from Irvington-area residents, elected officials (City-County Councilor Andy
Nielsen, D, on record disappointed), and protests outside the City-County Building the day of
the vote. Concerns: noise, energy use, impact on nearby schools and the Pennsy Trail. Developer
downsized the project citing community feedback.
- Sources: [WFYI](https://www.wfyi.org/wfyi-news/2026-07-15/east-side-data-center-wins-approval-despite-community-opposition), [Mirror Indy](https://mirrorindy.org/dc-blox-data-center-east-side-indianapolis-irvington-approval/), [WISH-TV](https://www.wishtv.com/news/politics/commission-approves-dc-blox-data-center-project-amid-neighborhood-opposition/)

**New, not in the 2026-08-16 scan note at all: Martindale-Brightwood**, a second, separate
Indianapolis-eastside data center (MetroBloks) that also moved ahead despite resident outcry —
worth adding as its own corpus candidate, not folded into the DC Blox entry, since it's a
different site/developer. [Mirror Indy](https://mirrorindy.org/martindale-brightwood-metrobloks-data-center-east-indianapolis/)
not yet individually verified beyond this headline-level search hit.

**New and potentially significant for the institutional-venue moderator: an Indianapolis
City-County Council committee advanced a data center moratorium through 2027 after months of
community pressure**, sent to the full council for an **August 10, 2026** vote — after both
site-level approvals above. If it passed, that's a city-level legislative body imposing a
moratorium *after* losing at the development-commission level — a different venue succeeding
where another one didn't, same pattern as NY's statewide executive order following losses
elsewhere. **Not verified whether the Aug 10 vote actually happened or passed** — this is a
same-night search hit, one step removed from confirmed; worth a direct check next session
(WebSearch alone, doesn't need WebFetch) before citing the outcome either way.

## Ohio — real number, not a full list, but the number itself is a strong data point

**35 data center moratoria across Ohio as of August 2026** (per aggregator coverage — Ohio
described as leading the country on this metric), not the "~18" the 2026-08-16 scan note
estimated. Individual confirmed examples: **Boardman** (township trustees, one-year
moratorium, voted April 28, 2026), **Kent** (temporary moratorium via signed ordinance,
capped at one year — the actual ordinance PDF is public), **Cleveland** (three-month
moratorium, July 15, 2026). A citizen-led effort to pursue a statewide ballot amendment
banning data centers in Ohio is also underway (per PBS/WGFN video coverage) — a more drastic
escalation than any other state in the corpus so far, worth its own line if it advances.
- Sources: [Green Data Center Guide](https://greendatacenterguide.com/2026-u-s-data-center-moratorium-landscape/), [Kent Ordinance 2026-31 (PDF)](https://www.kentohio.gov/media/yrhjyjrn/2026-31-data-center-moratorium_signed.pdf), [WKBN — Boardman](https://www.wkbn.com/news/local-news/boardman-news/boardman-places-moratorium-on-data-centers/)
- **Revise the draft's "~18 Ohio local moratoria (industry-tracker summary)" line to "35+ per
  aggregator tracking, individually confirmed for Boardman/Kent/Cleveland."** The Kent ordinance
  PDF in particular is a genuine primary-source document (a government-hosted PDF, not a news
  summary) that could be worth a WebFetch pull the moment that tool is unblocked — first
  candidate in the whole corpus for an actual primary legislative text, not secondary coverage.

## Mississippi/Tennessee — Southaven/Memphis xAI "Colossus": confirmed, and this is the richest single Tier 3 entry, with a new dynamic not yet in the thematic map

The 2026-08-16 note's one-line description undersold this badly. Full picture:
- **NAACP is suing xAI and its subsidiary MZX Tech** over 27 unpermitted methane gas turbines
  powering the Colossus 2 data center in South Memphis/Southaven — a Clean Air Act violation
  claim (major pollution sources need a permit before construction/operation), seeking to stop
  the turbines' operation, require public-health-impact offsets, and fines up to $124,426/day
  of violation. [NAACP](https://naacp.org/articles/naacp-sues-xai-illegal-pollution-data-center-power-plant), [Earthjustice](https://earthjustice.org/case/xai-illegal-gas-power-plant-data-center-colossus), [Mississippi Today](https://mississippitoday.org/2026/04/15/data-center-turbines-southaven/)
- **Separately, a resident-filed proposed class action against SpaceX** over Colossus II noise
  as a public nuisance.
- **Hundreds of community members packed a Mississippi Dept. of Environmental Quality hearing**
  pushing back on the gas plant. [Yahoo/AP](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/mississippi-holds-hearing-xai-data-170345074.html)
- **New dynamic, not anywhere else in the corpus: the U.S. Department of Justice intervened on
  the developer's side**, asking the Mississippi federal court to dismiss the NAACP suit,
  explicitly citing **national-security interests tied to the facility's AI-development use**.
  [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/doj-intervenes-xai-data-center-gas-turbine-lawsuit/823267/), [earth.org](https://earth.org/trump-administration-seeks-dismissal-of-lawsuit-against-elon-musks-ai-data-center-near-memphis/)

This last point is worth flagging for Phase 3 specifically: every other "regulatory-venue-shifting"
instance in the restructured thematic map (AZ's Corporation Commission, LA's PSC overriding an
ALJ, Caddo council overriding its planning commission) is a *state or local* government body
favoring the developer over another *state or local* body. This is the first **federal**-level
intervention on a developer's side in the whole corpus, and the stated rationale (national
security / AI competitiveness) is a distinct justification from anything else documented —
not really "regulatory-venue-shifting" in the same sense (no venue-shopping by the developer;
the federal government stepped in on its own). Candidate new code:
**`federal-national-security-override`** — flagging as a possible addition to Theme 2's
sub-mechanisms, not deciding it; this is exactly the kind of Phase 3 call the standing rule
reserves for Britton.

## Arizona — item #25 now has real sources (was missing entirely)

**Mesa/Avondale/Phoenix water caps:** all three cities' water utilities have passed ordinances
capping industrial water use and requiring developers to buy supplemental supply above the cap
(Mesa disclosed ~7,800 acre-feet secured from large water users, companies undisclosed).
[Grist](https://grist.org/technology/arizona-water-data-centers-semiconducters/)

**Buckeye/Pinal County (Tract):** Tract acquired 2,069 acres in Buckeye for a data center park
(up to 20M sq ft, 40 data centers at full build-out); the state government has blocked both
Buckeye and Pinal County from permitting new groundwater-reliant development.
[DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/tract-announces-18gw-data-center-park-in-phoenix-arizona/)

**New, and a strong second "opposition win" case alongside Clinton County IN: Pinal County's
La Osa project (Vermaland) — slated to be Arizona's largest data center project (3,300 acres,
59 data centers, 2 on-site gas plants, battery storage near Eloy) — was cut by roughly 80%
after Pinal County Board of Supervisors heard ~60 in-person comments and 50 emails, all
opposed.** [KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/fronteras-desk/2026-05-28/largest-proposed-data-center-in-arizona-to-be-scaled-back-80-after-opposition), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/6/27/with-water-cuts-looming-in-arizona-in-us-locals-fight-data-centres)
This is a materially different outcome than Project Blue (Tucson) — same state, opposite
result at the same county-supervisor venue type. Worth its own corpus line (distinct from
Project Blue, which is Tucson/Pima County, not Pinal), and a genuinely strong second data
point for the "county-level bodies more responsive than state utility regulators" moderator
candidate (#3 in the restructure draft).
