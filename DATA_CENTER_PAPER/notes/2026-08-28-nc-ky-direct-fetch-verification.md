## Durham County — primary-verified, with one real discrepancy flagged

Directly fetched both cited sources (not just WebSearch-summarized this time):

**WUNC** (direct fetch): 4-1 vote, "Monday, August 26, 2026," nine-month moratorium (until May
2027), dissent from Commissioner Nida Allam, exemptions for facilities under 100,000 sq ft using
closed-loop cooling and no diesel backup generators — confirms the 08-27 note's summary exactly,
plus a useful added detail: "hyperscale data centers operated by Apple, Meta, and Google easily
exceed 100,000 square feet," so the exemption doesn't functionally cover the facilities driving
the opposition.

**WRAL** (direct fetch): 4-1 vote, but states the date as **"Monday, August 25, 2026"** — one day
off from WUNC's Aug 26. **Neither date is actually a Monday** — Aug 24, 2026 is the Monday of that
week (Aug 25 = Tuesday, Aug 26 = Wednesday). This is a genuine, unresolved date discrepancy
between two credible outlets, not something I'm resolving here — flag for whoever finalizes this
corpus row to pin down the actual meeting date (likely via the Durham County Commissioners' own
meeting-minutes/agenda page, not attempted tonight). WRAL also adds: the exemption "drew criticism
from residents," one of whom is quoted (partial quote only, page truncated the rest) calling for "a
more straight-forward moratorium," and that a June amendment to the county's development ordinance
had permitted a longer pause than the initial 60-day one from May — a detail not in the 08-27 note,
worth folding in if this row gets drafted.

## Louisville Metro — primary-verified, with one correction to the 08-27 note

Directly fetched LPM (Louisville Public Media), the lead cited source: confirms 24-1 vote,
Aug 13, 2026. **Duration is more precisely stated than the 08-27 note had it**: "six months **or
until Metro Council approves comprehensive zoning rules, whichever comes first**" — not a flat
six-month term. Coverage: pauses new data-center construction *and* converting existing buildings
into data-center use without new construction (closing a loophole).

**Correction:** the 08-27 note described "a separate motion for a full one-year moratorium
[that] failed." LPM's own account doesn't support that framing — it describes the one-year ask as
coming from a resident advocacy group (No Data Center 502) holding signs and speaking during
public comment, asking the council to amend the moratorium to a full year; that request "was not
incorporated into the final ordinance." That's a public-comment ask that didn't get adopted, not
a formal competing motion that was voted down and failed — a real distinction if this ever gets
described in the manuscript as "a one-year proposal failed."
