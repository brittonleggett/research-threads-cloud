## 1. NAACP v. SpaceXAI — genuine update: the Aug. 24 hearing existed, but was postponed
08-25's note said "no new hearing date found." Tonight's search found more: there **was** a scheduled Aug. 24,
2026 preliminary-injunction hearing (Northern District of Mississippi) on the NAACP/Southern Environmental Law
Center's Clean Air Act claims over the ~69 unpermitted turbines at xAI's Southaven power plant. Per a notice
filed with the court **Aug. 21**, that hearing was **postponed**. No new hearing date surfaced in tonight's
searches — this is a "there was movement, then it stalled again" update, not a resolution.

Separately (and this is a different track from the federal lawsuit — don't conflate them): xAI entered an
**agreed order with the Mississippi Dept. of Environmental Quality (MDEQ)** setting a fixed removal timeline for
all 69 temporary/mobile turbines, running from as early as Aug. 2026 through full removal by **July 2027**, as
xAI's 41-turbine permanent 1.2GW plant (permitted under a Clean Air Act permit granted March 2026) comes online.
Multiple outlets are explicit that **this agreed order does not resolve the NAACP suit's core legal question**
(whether trailer-mounted turbines running continuously for a year-plus are a "stationary source" under the
Clean Air Act) — it just sets a hardware timeline. DOJ's motion to intervene/dismiss (filed June 16, arguing
Grok is critical to national security) — no ruling found, same as every check since 08-21.
[Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/doj-may-intervene-in-naacp-lawsuit-over-xais-data-center-gas-turbines/820363/),

## 5. NEW FINDING: North Carolina is a major, previously-uncovered moratorium-wave state
This is the biggest thing found tonight and wasn't on the original flagged-states list (GA/UT/VA/AZ/IN-OH/
MS-TN/NY/TX). Multiple independent outlets (WUNC, WRAL, WFAE, WHQR, GovTech, indyweek — a genuinely broad,
non-duplicative source set) report **more than 30 North Carolina jurisdictions have enacted temporary data
center moratoriums or zoning freezes since February 2026** — at least 11 counties and 17 towns per an
August 2026 tracker count. Three concrete, well-sourced examples from the past two weeks:

- **Durham County** — commissioners voted **4-1** for a **9-month moratorium** on large-scale data centers
  on **Aug. 26, 2026** (i.e., yesterday relative to tonight), aligning with the City of Durham's existing pause
  (through May 2027). Sole dissent (Commissioner Nida Allam) wanted it to go further, not less far. Exemptions:
  facilities under 100,000 sq ft using closed-loop cooling and no diesel backup generators.
  [WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/environment/2026-08-26/durham-county-data-center-moratorium),
  [WRAL](https://www.wral.com/news/local/durham-county-approves-data-center-moratorium-august-2026/),
  [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/artificial-intelligence/durham-county-n-c-votes-for-pause-on-data-centers)
- **Greensboro** — city council voted **8-0** (one recusal, Mayor Pro Tem Denise Roth) for a **180-day**
  moratorium (extended from an initially-proposed 120 days after public feedback) on Aug. 18, 2026, covering
  facilities over 10MW, running through Feb. 14, 2027.
  [Greensboro city gov](https://www.greensboro-nc.gov/Home/Components/News/News/22889/),
  [FOX8](https://myfox8.com/news/north-carolina/greensboro/greensboro-establishes-180-day-data-center-moratorium/)
  — note: an earlier Rhino Times headline ("Council Reverses Course") suggests some back-and-forth in the
  lead-up; didn't chase that thread down, flagging in case it matters for a "contested/reversed" narrative angle.
- **Alamance County** — commissioners voted **unanimously** for a **one-year** moratorium on Aug. 17, 2026
  (longer than the six-month version initially floated), after an hours-long public hearing.
  [FOX8](https://myfox8.com/news/north-carolina/alamance/alamance-county-commissioners-approve-1-year-data-center-moratorium/)

Other NC jurisdictions named in coverage but **not independently verified tonight** (search-summary only):
Charlotte (150-day moratorium, June 2026, through Nov. 2026), Chatham County, Orange County, Statesville, Lee
County and Sanford (UDO amendments rather than moratoria). Treat these as leads, not confirmed facts, until
checked individually.

**Assessment for Britton:** North Carolina looks comparable in scale to Texas as a national-scope case — arguably
bigger by jurisdiction count (30+ vs. Texas's ~5-6 documented tonight), though it reads as more homogeneous
(nearly everything is a temporary moratorium/pause, not the outright-ban/lawsuit-reversal variation Texas showed).
Not drafted as full corpus rows given tonight's time budget spent partly on NAACP re-verification — flagging as a
ready-to-deep-dive lead for a future session, and worth factoring into the corpus-restructuring conversation
alongside Texas.

## 6. NEW FINDING: Kentucky — Louisville Metro Council moratorium, Aug. 13, 2026
Also not on the original flagged-states list. Louisville Metro Council voted **24-1 (plus one no-vote)** to
approve a **six-month** moratorium on data-center-related rezoning, conditional-use permits, and large
development-plan applications; a separate motion for a full **one-year** moratorium **failed**. An amendment
closing a loophole (existing buildings can't be re-zoned into data-center use to dodge the pause) also passed.
Background: a pause was first proposed in September 2025 after a 1.6M-sq-ft hyperscale data center was approved
with what critics called minimal public input; the council sat on the vote for nearly a year before acting.
[LPM (Aug 13 vote)](https://www.lpm.org/news/2026-08-13/louisville-metro-council-approves-data-center-moratorium-as-new-proposal-sparks-controversy),
[WBKO](https://www.wbko.com/2026/08/14/louisville-metro-council-approves-6-month-moratorium-data-centers/),
[LouisvilleKY.gov](https://louisvilleky.gov/news/metro-council-meeting-highlights-thursday-august-13).
Single well-documented data point so far, not a multi-jurisdiction state wave like NC or TX — flagging as a
possible addition to an existing state grouping rather than a new anchor state on its own, pending Britton's call.
