## 4. National scan — five new state leads found, not previously in the corpus

- **Missouri (St. Louis) — new state, direct-fetched.** A coalition (Missouri Workers Center, Missouri
  Coalition for the Environment, Action St. Louis, Missouri Jobs with Justice, Sierra Club St. Louis,
  WEPOWER, Eco-Socialist Green Party of Eastern Missouri) opposing a **$3 billion "Armory" data
  center** in Midtown St. Louis filed suit against the city's Board of Adjustment, alleging the board
  erred in denying appeals and granting the conditional-use permit, and that health/environmental
  impacts were inadequately studied. **Filing date is unresolved** — two fetches of what appears to be
  the same underlying reporting gave inconsistent dates ("Friday, August 23" vs. "Friday, August 30";
  neither date actually fell on a Friday in 2026 — the nearest real Friday is Aug 28 — treat the exact
  date as unconfirmed). Plaintiffs: Daniel Pate and Kerry McCullen. St. Louis's Board of Aldermen
  separately advanced its own data-center regulatory framework the same period.
  https://www.stlpr.org/government-politics-issues/2026-08-29/opponents-of-st-louis-data-center-take-their-fight-to-court (direct),
  https://www.krps.org/missouri-news/2026-08-31/opponents-of-st-louis-data-center-take-their-fight-to-court (direct, republication)
- **Pennsylvania — new state, WebSearch-summary confidence (multiple converging outlets, not directly
  fetched).** Gov. Josh Shapiro signed an executive order Aug 18, 2026 establishing "GRID" (Governor's
  Responsible Infrastructure Development) standards: developers must bring their own power generation,
  pay their own infrastructure costs, meet clean-energy/water-conservation requirements, get local
  approval, and are barred from NDAs on data-center projects; all AI data-center proposals removed
  from the prior fast-track permitting process. Framed by multiple outlets as a reversal from
  Shapiro's earlier pro-development posture. A real, statewide, high-profile policy event — worth a
  direct-fetch verification pass before treating as corpus-ready.
  https://www.inquirer.com/politics/pennsylvania/josh-shapiro-data-center-order-20260818.html,
  https://www.pa.gov/governor/newsroom/2026-press-releases/gov--shapiro-s-remarks-at-signing-ceremony-for-an-exec--order-on
- **Nevada — new state, WebSearch-summary confidence.** NV Energy (the state's primary electric
  utility) sued data-center developer Tract in Washoe County's Second Judicial District Court (filed
  ~July 28, 2026), alleging Tract is trying to bypass the Public Utilities Commission of Nevada via
  private arbitration to shift infrastructure/power costs onto ratepayers; Tract disputes this, saying
  it committed ~$1B toward its own infrastructure. Notable as a **utility-vs-developer** suit — a
  different actor configuration than the developer-vs-citizens or developer-vs-government patterns
  already tracked. https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/nevadas-electric-utility-is-suing-a-major-data-center-company-heres-why
- **Tennessee (Nashville) — new state, WebSearch-summary confidence, concrete filing detail.**
  Nashville's Metro Council passed BL2026-1448 (data-center permit moratorium through Dec 1, 2026) on
  July 21, 2026, plus a July 1 ordinance restricting siting near churches/daycares/schools/zoos and an
  Aug 4 measure authorizing eminent domain against a specific developer's site. **DCB Nashville**
  (subsidiary of DC BLOX) sued Metro Nashville (reported Aug 12, 2026) claiming the ordinances amount
  to a "targeted attack" violating federal constitutional protections, tied to a $23M Grassmere Road
  property purchase (closed July 16, 2026) for a ~70,000 sq ft data center; DCB seeks a declaratory
  judgment voiding the ordinances or compensation. Structurally close to the TX ultra-vires and KY
  city-vs-county theories already flagged as a developer-litigation-countermobilization pattern

  https://www.brushwoodmedianetwork.com/tennessee/news-tennessee/nashville-facing-legal-challenge-to-data-center-moratorium/
