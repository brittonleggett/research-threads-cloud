## New tonight: Hill County, TX — a real regulatory-reversal finding, not yet in any note
The 08-30 note flagged Hill County (not Austin County) as actually the **first** Texas county to
pass a data-center moratorium (May 2026) and left it unchased. Ran it down:

- **May 12, 2026:** Hill County Commissioners Court voted **3-2** for a 1-year moratorium on new
  data center construction in unincorporated areas — first Texas county to do so.
- **Late May 2026:** a data-center developer sued the county for **over $100 million in damages**,
  calling the moratorium illegal under Texas law.
- **~June 10, 2026:** facing the federal lawsuit, the county **repealed the blanket moratorium**
  and replaced it with a developer-disclosure checklist (water use, traffic, noise, economic
  effects, public notice requirements) instead of a pause.
  (Sources: [KWTX](https://www.kwtx.com/2026/05/12/hill-county-commissioners-approve-1-year-moratorium-data-center-construction/), [KWTX lawsuit report](https://www.kwtx.com/2026/05/28/illegal-under-texas-law-data-center-developer-files-lawsuit-against-hill-county-moratorium/), [K&L Gates legal alert](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/Facing-a-Federal-Lawsuit-Hill-County-Repeals-Blanket-Pause-on-Data-Center-Construction-Adopting-New-Approval-Procedures-Instead-6-10-2026), [FOX 4 Dallas](https://www.fox4news.com/news/hill-county-texas-data-center-moratorium-lifted))

**Why this matters for the paper:** this is a genuinely different outcome pattern than every other
row in the corpus — a moratorium enacted, then **reversed under direct legal/financial pressure
from industry**, not through public reversal-of-opinion (cf. the existing "Home Depot reversal
narrative" is about disclosure tone, not regulatory reversal) or venue-shifting (developer routing
to a friendlier body). This is closer to a **regulatory-capture-via-litigation-threat** pattern —
worth a look alongside the existing `regulatory-venue-shifting` code as a possibly-distinct
mechanism, or at minimum a strong counter-example to cite when discussing opposition efficacy
(some moratoriums don't hold). Not added as a corpus row — that's a sizing/inclusion call, per
standing rule.
