## 1. Clinton County, IN — verified, promoted from Tier 3 to Tier 2

Direct-fetched `clintoncountydailynews.com`'s own coverage (a second source, since
WTHR 403'd and ftimes.com rate-limited): confirms the Clinton County Commissioners
voted **3-0 on January 20, 2026** to deny a rezoning request (agricultural to light
industrial, ~715 acres) for a proposed data center. The article frames the denial as
following "a review of the proposal and consideration of public input," and confirms
the county's own 12-month reapplication bar. The developer name ("Data One"/"Logix

## 2. NAACP v. X.AI Corp. — the significant find of the night, verified via the actual federal docket

Southaven, MS/South Memphis, TN's "Colossus 2" turbine dispute (flagged Tier 3, single
outlet) turned out to be much bigger and better-documented than the corpus draft's
one-line description suggested. Verification chain, primary-source-first:

1. **Earthjustice's own case page** (a party's own site, not press paraphrase) and
   **Utility Dive** (independent trade press) were direct-fetched and cross-checked —
   both agree: NAACP and the Mississippi State Conference of the NAACP sued xAI and
   its subsidiary MZX Tech over 27 unpermitted methane gas turbines (~495MW) powering
   the "Colossus 2" AI data center in Southaven, MS, alleging Clean Air Act violations.
   Cited potential annual emissions: 1,700+ tons NOx, 500 tons CO, 180 tons fine
   particulate matter, 19 tons formaldehyde. Site is roughly half a mile from homes and
   a mile from an elementary school.
2. **Found and read the actual federal docket directly** (not from search synthesis):
   `courtlistener.com/docket/73188848/national-association-for-the-advancement-of-
   colored-people-v-xai-corp/` — **case 3:26-cv-00074, N.D. Mississippi (Greenville
   division), Judge Debra M. Brown**. Complaint filed **April 14, 2026**. 122 docket
   entries as of tonight's fetch, read directly rather than trusting a synthesized
   summary.
3. **The novel finding, read from the docket entries themselves:** the **U.S.
   Department of Justice filed an Amended Motion to Intervene and Dismiss on July 7,
   2026** (entry 85), attaching a "Declaration of Cameron Stanley" and a **letter from
   Mississippi Governor Tate Reeves** as exhibits — i.e., the federal government
   intervening on a private AI company's side against a civil-rights organization's
   environmental suit, with the state's own governor apparently aligned with that
   intervention. Utility Dive's reporting (independently corroborating, not the source
   of this finding) quotes the DOJ's stated rationale: Grok is "one of only four
   proprietary state-of-the-art frontier AI currently capable of supporting national
   security applications," and that hindering xAI "will be impaired[sic]" DOD's
   mission — with Utility Dive noting the DOJ's filing disclosed Grok's use in an
   actual named military operation ("Operation Epic Fury").
4. **This is live and moving fast**: the docket's three most recent entries (Sept 2-8,
   2026) show DOJ asking the court for a **written ruling on its intervention/dismissal
   motion no later than September 10** (today, relative to this session's date), NAACP
   opposing that request (Sept 3), and DOJ replying (Sept 8). **Worth a follow-up check
   soon** — a ruling may land within days of this note.

**Why this matters for the paper, without touching the locked design/moderator
question:** every procedural-exclusion instance in the corpus so far (LA's PSC/Caddo
council, AZ's Project Blue) is about one *government body* overriding another, or a
developer routing around a less-friendly regulator. This is qualitatively different —
the **federal executive branch itself intervening to override community/civil-rights
litigation on national-security grounds**, with a state governor's letter attached in
support. Flagging as a potential new sub-mechanism or boundary case for whatever the
Theme 2/`regulatory-venue-shifting` code becomes once Britton does the actual Phase 3
review — not deciding that here, just surfacing it as unusually theory-relevant
material.
