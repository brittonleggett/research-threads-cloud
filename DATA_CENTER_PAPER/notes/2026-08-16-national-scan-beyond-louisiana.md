## Georgia — richest vein after Louisiana, strong theme match

- **Meta Stanton Springs (Newton/Morgan County):** ~10% of Newton County's daily water supply;
  residents report discolored tap water and dry wells since 2018 land-clearing; county projects
  a water deficit by 2030; rates up 33%. Rep. AOC pressed EPA Assistant Administrator Jessica
  Kramer on this at a House Energy & Commerce hearing (May 20, 2026), holding up jars of
  discolored water: "a basic public health issue." Meta says water is "fully sourced from the
  local water utility, not groundwater," backed by an independent no-well-impact study.
  [CBS Atlanta](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/aoc-presses-epa-over-morgan-county-drinking-water-concerns-tied-to-georgia-data-center-development/)
  → codes: `institutional-distrust`, `distributive-environmental-injustice`, plus a
  **health/contamination framing** not present in the LA codebook.
- **Covington/Newton County power plant (VoltaGrid/ServerFarm):** 33 gas engines + 37 diesel
  generators (90MW, ~90,000 homes) proceeded without required air permits per Georgia's SIP.
  Sustainable Newton, Altamaha Riverkeeper, and the Southern Environmental Law Center filed a
  formal complaint June 25, 2026. Nancy Schulz (Sustainable Newton): "We're very concerned
  about the air pollution. There are families within three miles of here." ServerFarm VP Kurt
  Widmann: "Every action taken... has been in careful consultation with the applicable
  authorities." [Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/07/02/advocacy-groups-say-covington-data-center-power-plant-are-building-without-proper-permits/)
  → codes: `procedural-exclusion-secrecy` (regulatory end-run via draft permits),
  `formal-legal-opposition`.
- **State legislative fight:** HB1063 and SB410 (ratepayer-cost-shielding bills) both died at
  the April 3, 2026 adjournment; separately, Georgia PSC froze Georgia Power's base rate
  through 2028 and now requires data centers to fund their own upstream generation/transmission
  — even as Georgia Power adds ~10GW by 2032 at $50-60B in eventual ratepayer-financed cost.
  [Stateline](https://stateline.org/2026/02/05/with-electricity-bills-rising-some-states-consider-new-data-center-laws/),
  [Georgia Recorder](https://georgiarecorder.com/2026/01/20/outrage-over-surge-of-data-centers-in-georgia-inspires-wave-of-bipartisan-bills/)
  → codes: `institutional-distrust`, `legislative-backlash`. Explicitly **bipartisan** —
  contrast with LA's party-line PSC vote (#12 in the LA corpus).
- **Read:** same core themes as LA, plus a genuinely new, more visceral element — acute
  health/contamination framing (discolored water, air pollution near homes).

## Utah — resource-scarcity/commons framing dominates, distinct flavor

- **Stratos Project (Kevin O'Leary-backed, Millard County via MIDA):** ~400 formal protests
  filed with Utah Division of Water Rights (May 2026) against transferring 1,900 acre-feet from
  Bar H Ranch, part of ~13,000 acre-feet secured/contracted overall — enough for 20,000+
  households, partly directed to a gas station and server cooling. Named residents: Monika
  Norwid (Salt Lake City), Stephanie Higginbotham (Provo, Great Salt Lake devastation), Kalee
  Lambert (Logan, disputing the "net zero water use" claim as unevidenced). Developer side:
  Austin Pritchett (West GenCo), Hilary Venable/Paul Morris (MIDA — a state-created authority
  that itself bypasses normal county land-use process, a procedural-exclusion angle on its own).
  [Cache Valley Daily](https://www.cachevalleydaily.com/news/hundreds-of-utahns-file-to-block-kevin-o-leary-s-proposed-massive-data-center-campus/article_eea61d19-f3fd-4951-a517-b14b3353dd1f.html)
  → codes: `resource-scarcity-commons`, `institutional-distrust` (disputed water-neutrality
  claims), `procedural-exclusion-secrecy` (MIDA venue).
- **Statewide:** Rep. Jill Koford (R-Ogden) bill to force aggregated/anonymized public
  disclosure of data center water use; Utah Rivers Council's Alta Fairbourne — no reason to
  incentivize data centers "during a water crisis."
  [Utah News Dispatch](https://utahnewsdispatch.com/2026/01/23/bill-to-force-data-centers-to-publicly-disclose-water-use-advances/).
- **Bluffdale NSA facility:** real prior/ongoing water controversy (126M+ gallons Oct
  2024-Sep 2025, city ordered to release usage numbers) — a genuine pre-AI-boom precedent,
  useful for a "this isn't new, AI just accelerated it" framing.
- **Read:** resource-scarcity/commons-dilemma is the dominant frame here, much more than
  distributive/racial-EJ — a real point of regional divergence from LA/GA.

## Virginia (Loudoun County) — most mature opposition, distinct frame

- 250+ data centers, 53.3M sq ft (footprint doubled in 5 years). Board of Supervisors moved
  (article dated July 24, 2026) to pause all new applications/site plans/substations pending
  new zoning, board vote scheduled Sept 15, 2026; also stripped by-right fast-track status
  (no zoning/planning hearing) from several residential-adjacent zone types. Supervisor Juli
  Briskman: "Loudoun became the data center capital of the world because data center
  development occurred with little oversight," residents "tired of the visual and noise
  impacts, the costs to our electrical grid and wallets, and the harm to the very air we
  breathe." Rep. Suhas Subramanyam: the county is now "over reliant on data center revenue to
  the point where it's going to be more than half of their revenue."
  [NBC Washington](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/northern-virginia/loudoun-county-considers-putting-pause-on-data-center-development/4134004/)
  → codes: `procedural-exclusion-secrecy` (by-right bypass), `institutional-distrust`, plus a
  **new** code — `aesthetic-noise-property-value-framing`. Much less racial/economic-EJ-coded
  than LA/GA, much more classic-NIMBY/quality-of-life, in a wealthy DC-exurb context.
- **Read:** clearest case of regional heterogeneity — same procedural-exclusion mechanism as
  LA, but the grievance content and community profile differ meaningfully.

## Arizona — procedural venue-shopping as its own distinct pattern

- **Project Blue (Beale Infrastructure, south Tucson):** Tucson City Council voted against
  annexation in 2025, blocking direct Tucson Water access. Beale then partnered with Tucson
  Electric Power and won Arizona Corporation Commission approval for a new power deal, letting
  the project proceed via air-cooling instead of city water. Protest April 24, 2026 — Vivek
  Bharathan (No Desert Data Center Coalition): "But Beale lied." Reed Spurling: "We will
  continue fighting until we stop this data center." Lee Ziesche (Tucson DSA): "I do not think
  that this project will ever be fully built."
  [AZ Luminaria](https://azluminaria.org/2026/04/24/we-said-no-dozens-protest-as-construction-of-project-blue-data-center-begins/)
  → **new code** `regulatory-venue-shifting` — a developer blocked by one governmental body
  routes around it through a friendlier one. Structurally identical to LA's Amazon case (city
  council overriding its own planning commission) and Meta's PSC (overriding its own ALJ).
- Mesa/Avondale/Phoenix have passed industrial water-use caps requiring developers to buy
  supplemental supply; Buckeye/Pinal County are state-blocked from permitting new
  groundwater-reliant development, yet a $20B, 2,000-acre complex (Tract) proceeded anyway on a
  repurposed stalled residential site.
- **Read:** the clearest, most transferable evidence for `regulatory-venue-shifting` as a
  cross-state mechanism — deserves to be a named code, not folded into generic "procedural
  exclusion."

## Indiana / Ohio — where formal opposition sometimes wins outright

- **Clinton County, IN:** commissioners voted 3-0 (Jan 20, 2026) to deny Data One's rezoning
  request (715 acres, ag→light industrial) after the Area Plan Commission had already
  recommended approval. Commissioner Kevin Myers: "I received a significant amount of public
  comment, and it was more negative than positive... the majority of our residents are against
  this project." Commissioner Jordan Brewer (the ambivalent-framing counter-voice): "I want to
  clear up the idea that this means Clinton County is not open for business. We do want
  development..." Denial triggers a mandatory 12-month resubmission wait.
  [Frankfort Times](https://www.ftimes.com/news/3-0-vote-halts-proposed-data-center-rezoning-in-clinton-county/article_e2e3a3e7-f14c-551b-b1e4-4dcadb5b8192.html)
  → codes: `formal-legal-opposition` (successful, unlike most LA attempts),
  `ambivalent-economic-opportunity-framing`.
- Contrast, same state: Indianapolis Metropolitan Development Commission approved an eastside
  center in July 2026 despite community opposition — possible EJ angle, needs direct
  verification if pursued.
- Ohio: at least 18 municipalities have enacted or are considering local moratoria (per
  Latitude Media/industry-tracker summary — WebSearch-confidence only, not independently
  fetched).
- **Read:** Midwest cases show local zoning bodies as a genuinely more responsive venue than
  state utility regulators — a real, citable asymmetry in opposition efficacy by institutional
  venue (contrast with LA's PSC and Georgia's PSC/EPD pattern).

## National academic baseline for distributive injustice — citation verified 2026-08-16

**Ancona et al. (2026), "Urban infrastructure and fossil fuel industrial legacy drive US data
center siting," *Nature Cities*, DOI 10.1038/s44284-026-00487-z.** Verified real this pass (was
only WebSearch-level-confident before tonight). Quantitative, national-scale spatial analysis
of 4,283 US data center facilities. Two findings worth citing directly: (1) 97.5% of facilities
sit inside metro/micropolitan areas — directly undercuts a "rural cloud expansion" framing if
any reviewer assumes one; (2) **data centers cluster near *retired coal-plant* sites at roughly
double the rate of other urban areas** — a strong, citable "the digital economy is inheriting
the fossil-fuel economy's geography" finding. This is the real national academic baseline the
LA-specific CNU-style evidence (Data Center Paper doesn't have its own CNU-equivalent study,
but Georgia/Newton County and the Norfolk State HBCU case from the Flock Cameras Paper's own
corpus make the same structural point) sits inside — good citation to establish that Theme 1
(distributive/environmental injustice) isn't a Louisiana-specific artifact before drilling into
the state-level cases above.

## Does "generalizable across contexts" hold up? Partial — and the heterogeneity is itself the finding

**Recurs robustly nationally (defensible as generalizable):** procedural exclusion/secrecy
(LA, GA, UT-via-MIDA, VA-via-fast-track, AZ-via-venue-shift); institutional distrust of
cost/water/safety promises (LA, GA, UT, VA); ambivalent economic-opportunity framing as a
genuine counter-current (LA, IN explicitly, GA implicitly); collective mobilization escalating
to formal channels (LA, AZ, IN, MS/TN litigation).

**Does NOT recur uniformly — needs to be built into the design, not assumed away:**
1. **Dominant grievance content varies by regional resource profile.** Water/drought scarcity
   dominates AZ/UT; racialized distributive injustice dominates LA/GA (majority-Black,
   historically industrially-burdened communities); pure aesthetic/property-value/noise
   NIMBYism dominates wealthy VA exurbs with comparatively little EJ framing. This is a real,
   citable moderator (community racial/economic profile × regional resource scarcity), not
   noise.
2. **Opposition efficacy varies sharply by institutional venue.** In LA, formal opposition
   mostly lost (PSC upheld secrecy 3-1; Caddo commission rejected both transparency measures).
   Nationally it sometimes wins outright — Clinton County IN's 3-0 denial, Tucson's annexation
   block, NY's statewide moratorium. State utility regulators look more industry-aligned than
   local zoning boards or state legislatures/executives across multiple states — a genuinely
   new, more interesting finding than "opposition happens everywhere," one the LA-only corpus
   could not have surfaced.
3. **New mechanism-level code warranted: `regulatory-venue-shifting`** — a developer blocked
   by one body routes around it through a friendlier one (Beale/Tucson→ACC/TEP;
   Amazon/LA council overriding its own planning commission; Meta/LA PSC overriding its own
   ALJ). More precise and more clearly generalizable than folding everything into "procedural
   exclusion" — ties directly to regulatory-capture theory.

**Bottom line:** the six-theme LA codebook isn't wrong, but presenting it as-is as "the"
national pattern would overstate uniformity the evidence doesn't support. Stronger, more
publishable move: keep the core justice/distrust/mobilization themes as the generalizable
spine, add regional resource-scarcity and community-socioeconomic-profile explicitly as
moderators (not noise), and add `regulatory-venue-shifting` as a new cross-cutting mechanism
code. Not decided here — Britton's call on how (or whether) to restructure Study 1 around this.
