## 1. Novelty-defense scan for "benefit–burden asymmetry" (BBA)

**Result: No scoop found. BBA as a named, multidimensional construct for infrastructure/data-center

close variant name.** This is a real (not perfunctory) finding worth reporting plainly, and it is
reassuring rather than concerning.

**Searches run:** `"benefit burden asymmetry" infrastructure siting justice 2025 2026`,
`"benefit-burden asymmetry" construct 2026`, `"benefit-burden asymmetry"` (bare phrase, all-time),
`"benefit-burden asymmetry" OR "benefit burden asymmetry" site:scholar.google.com`,

sovereignty"/"cloud ESG" framings and AI-infrastructure "legitimacy" construct papers.

**What the exact-phrase searches actually turned up:**
- No peer-reviewed or database-indexed (Crossref/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar) hit for "benefit-burden
  asymmetry" or "benefit burden asymmetry" as a named academic construct, in any domain, let alone
  infrastructure siting.
- One WebSearch **synthesized summary** claimed a page on `pollution.sustainability-directory.com`
  discussed "benefit-burden asymmetry... particularly evident in data center siting." **This did
  not hold up under direct WebFetch of the actual page** — the phrase does not appear on the page
  at all, and the site itself is a non-academic glossary/directory ("Sustainability Directory
  Digital Ltd," CC-BY-4.0 educational content, no peer review, no named authors), not a citable
  source even if it had used the phrase. Flagging this explicitly because it is exactly the kind of
  false-positive an automated search-summary can produce — worth remembering that WebSearch's own
  synthesized text is not itself evidence and should be checked against the source page.
- Existing, already-known adjacent terminology (all previously catalogued in the 2026-09-10 audit's
  Novelty Threat Matrix, nothing new here): "benefit asymmetry" in the nutrient-management/water-
  quality literature (different domain, low collision risk); Wilson's (1980) diffuse-benefit/
  concentrated-cost framework; the energy-justice distributive tenet; Soja's spatial-justice
  framing. No new instance of any of these closing the gap further than the 09-10 audit already
  found.

**Adjacent-but-distinct constructs found (worth naming, not worth worrying about):**
- "Compute sovereignty" / "capacity extraction" — a 2026 *ScienceDirect* article ("Data centres and
  the new strategic infrastructure governance: From cloud ESG to compute sovereignty," in a journal
  this session could not fully identify past the ScienceDirect landing page, blocked at 403) frames
  "community tolerance" as one input rendered into a "capacity" resource alongside electricity,
  water, and land. This is a geopolitical/industrial-policy framing (national sovereignty, hosting
  vs. control of value), not a justice/legitimacy psychological-mediator model — different unit of
  analysis and different literature entirely. Not a threat to BBA, but worth knowing it exists if
  the manuscript ever discusses data centers as "strategic infrastructure."
- "AI Legitimacy in Energy" (ScienceDirect, 2026) — proposes AI systems/initiatives need a "license
  to operate" via transparent, accountable corporate narratives. This is about legitimizing the use
  of AI *as a decision-making technology* inside energy companies, not about the legitimacy of
  physical AI/data-center *infrastructure* to host communities. Different construct, different
  causal chain — not a collision.
- A large, fast-moving 2026 trade-press conversation about "social license to operate" for AI data
  centers exists (World Economic Forum, Utility Dive, Data Center Dynamics, Teneo, Data Center
  Knowledge — all general-web, non-academic sources, flagged as such). None of it proposes a named,
  multidimensional benefit/burden diagnostic construct; it is practitioner commentary urging
  transparency and community engagement, using "social license" descriptively. This is useful
  *motivating/framing* color for an Introduction (it shows the phenomenon is live and urgent right
  now) but is not competing academic theory and carries no citation risk to BBA specifically.

**Bottom line for Britton:** the novelty case the 2026-09-10 audit already built (BBA's defensible

## 3. General literature-currency check (2025–2026)

Several genuinely new items surfaced this session that were not in the 2026-09-10 audit or any
follow-up pass. None of these were assessed for citation-readiness (full text not obtained for any
of them) — flagging them as candidates for a future targeted verification pass, in descending order
of apparent relevance.

1. **Yale Program on Climate Change Communication — data center benefit/cost public-opinion
   finding (reported ~2026-09-08/09, via NPR-affiliate coverage: KJZZ, KNPR, KUNC, KVNF, Wyoming
   Public Media, KSUT, Utah Public Radio).** Widely syndicated public-radio coverage reports a Yale
   "Climate Change in the American Mind" survey finding that **74% of respondents said they would
   be more supportive of data centers if developers offset their home electricity costs**, and
   frames opposition as resistance to "a bad bargain, where communities are asked to absorb the
   costs while somebody else gets the benefit" — language that maps almost exactly onto BBA's core
   intuition and directly onto the benefit-localization moderator (P5). **Important caveat:** the
   only YPCCC page this session could access directly (`climatecommunication.yale.edu/news-events/
   how-public-opinion-is-affecting-data-center-development/`) is an event-announcement/press page
   for a September 3, 2026 panel discussion, not a research report itself — it names no
   methodology, sample, or the 74% figure. The 74% figure and framing come only from secondary
   public-radio coverage of what is described as a survey report, not from a primary YPCCC
   publication this session could locate and read directly. **Do not cite the 74% figure or exact
   wording as verified until the actual underlying YPCCC survey report/brief is located and read** —
   right now this is real-world grounding/motivating color at best (and good news that the paper's
   real-world premise is topical and empirically active), not a citable, verified academic finding.
2. **Cartwright, E.D. (2026), "The Environmental Justice and Community Impacts of Data Centers,"
   *Climate and Energy* (Wiley), 42, pp. 15–17, DOI `10.1002/gas.70012`.** A real, DOI-bearing 2026
   Wiley publication specifically on data-center environmental-justice/community impacts — directly
   relevant to the audit's Section 8 claim that "a dedicated peer-reviewed empirical study of
   data-center-specific public acceptance/opposition does not appear to exist yet." Full text was
   not obtained (Wiley Online Library returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch). Two things worth flagging
   plainly: (a) *Climate and Energy* is a short-format Wiley practitioner/trade-report journal (this
   piece runs 3 pages, 15–17), which reads more like a commentary/briefing note than a full
   empirical research article with its own methods and data — worth confirming before treating it
   as filling the empirical gap the audit identified; (b) even if brief, its existence means the
   audit's "no dedicated data-center-EJ literature yet" framing should be revisited and possibly
   softened rather than restated as-is in the manuscript without checking this source first.
3. **Ancona, Lauber Bonomo, Rozhkov & Porfiri (2026), "Urban infrastructure and fossil fuel
   industrial legacy drive US data center siting," *Nature Cities*, DOI `10.1038/s44284-026-00487-z`
   (NYU Tandon).** A genuinely new, real, peer-reviewed empirical study (mapped 4,283+ US data
   center sites) finding data centers cluster on/near existing power-generation and legacy
   fossil-fuel-infrastructure sites, largely in metro/micro areas. Directly useful for the
   objective-BBA / geographic-dispersion side of the model (siting is not random — it tracks
   existing energy infrastructure) and for the LBNL-style objective-grounding citations the audit
   already recommended adding. Full text not obtained this session; abstract-level only.
4. **Ngata, Bashir, Westerlaken, Liote, Chandio & Olivetti, "The Cloud Next Door: Investigating the
   Environmental and Socioeconomic Strain of Datacenters on Local Communities," Proceedings of the
   2025 ACM SIGCAS/SIGCHI Conference on Computing and Sustainable Societies (COMPASS '25),
   DOI `10.1145/3715335.3736324`.** A mixed-methods community-impact study centered on Northern
   Virginia's "Data Center Alley," explicitly examining "competing narratives between stakeholders
   who differ widely in their awareness of datacenter impacts and their influence over development
   processes" — directly relevant to the visibility-asymmetry dimension and to P2/P6 (institutional
   distance/decision-maker proximity). Computer-science-conference venue (ACM), not a
   management/social-science journal, so positioning would need care, but the empirical content
   looks squarely on-point. Abstract-level only this session.
5. Two new 2026 CSREM-family Wiley articles surfaced that were not in the 09-10 CSREM literature
   map: **Steindl, "Artificial Intelligence and Corporate Social Irresponsibility," CSREM (2026),
   DOI `10.1002/csr.70841`**, and **Tian, "Artificial Intelligence and the Rise of Corporate Digital
   Responsibility: Unlocking Ethical Governance," CSREM (2026), DOI `10.1002/csr.70254`.** Both are

   precedents for AI-adjacent CSR theorizing and could supplement the Zarzycka (2025)/Jansen et al.
   (2024) CSREM-positioning citations already recommended. Neither was fetched for full text this

6. Nothing found this session changes or undermines any of the four BBA-dimension audits
