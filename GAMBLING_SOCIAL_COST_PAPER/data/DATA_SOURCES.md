## Summary judgment on feasibility

- **Fully feasible with public data, no restricted access needed:** legalization/launch dates (AGA + state sites + Legiscan), revenue (AGA), bankruptcy (US Courts), suicide (CDC WONDER), substance use (BRFSS), crime/IPV (NIBRS via FBI CDE), demographic controls (Census/ACS/BLS).
- **Not realistically feasible at the scale the top published papers used:** individual-level credit/transaction-panel data (NY Fed CCP microdata, proprietary transaction panels like Baker et al. and McCarthy et al. used) — restricted-access, would require a formal data-use agreement this project does not currently have. **This is the single biggest constraint on Design A and Design B as originally scoped** — Britton's team can study the same question at the *state-aggregate* level (bankruptcy rates, credit trends from public NY Fed regional reports) but cannot replicate the individual-household causal precision of the papers already published in this space. Any design choice needs to be explicit that it is intentionally working at a coarser level of resolution than the existing anchor papers, and justify why that's still a contribution (e.g., broader outcome set, different states/years, or a different theoretical lens — see `research_design/candidate_designs.md`).
