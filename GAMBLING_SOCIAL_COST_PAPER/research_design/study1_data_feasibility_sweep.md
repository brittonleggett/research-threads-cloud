# Study 1 Data Feasibility Sweep — Design C vs. Design D

_Quick sweep, 2026-09-04. Purpose: determine which design (or what combination) is buildable as Study 1 from free secondary data alone, since Study 1's findings + gaps are meant to directly shape Study 2's primary-data survey/model. Builds on `data/DATA_SOURCES.md`; only resolves the two things that file left open._

## Design C — Fiscal dependence → policy laxity

**1. Dependence measure (numerator + denominator).** VERIFIED FEASIBLE. Census Bureau's **Quarterly Survey of State and Local Tax Revenue (QTAX)** added sports betting as its own separable tax-revenue line starting Q3 2021, state-quarter resolution, continuing to present (confirmed via census.gov/library/stories/2025/12/sports-betting.html — the article cites concrete state-quarter figures, e.g. New York's ~$200M/quarter). The **Annual Survey of State Government Tax Collections (STC)** separately reports total state tax revenue by state-year, which is the natural denominator for a dependence ratio (sports-betting tax revenue ÷ total state tax revenue, or ÷ general-fund revenue with an added NASBO cross-check). NASBO's Fiscal Survey of States was not confirmed this session as breaking out sports-betting gaming revenue specifically as a share of general fund — Census QTAX/STC is the more directly verified path and should be the primary source; treat NASBO as a secondary cross-check only, unverified until checked directly against a NASBO report PDF.
→ **A state-quarter fiscal-dependence panel back to 2021 is realistically buildable now, free, no restricted access.**

**2. Policy-laxity measure.** PARTIALLY VERIFIED. The American Gaming Association's **Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide** (2025 edition, PDF at americangaming.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/AGA-2025-Responsible-Gaming-Regulations-and-Guidelines.pdf) is a real, existing compiled reference covering 38 states + DC's responsible-gaming statutes and regulations as of Jan 2025 — this is close to the compiled dataset the brief hoped might exist, but it is a **PDF reference document, not a structured/downloadable spreadsheet** (its own resource page returned a 403 to direct fetch this session, consistent with it being a static PDF, not an API or table). It would still need to be **manually coded into a state × policy-dimension matrix** — the source material exists, the structured dataset does not. Realistic scope: ~38-40 states with sports betting, likely 5-8 policy dimensions (advertising-to-minors restrictions, "risk-free"/"free bet" marketing-language bans, promotional-deduction tax treatment, college-prop-bet bans, self-exclusion mandates, push-notification limits), each state read against 1-2 primary sources (the AGA guide plus Legal Sports Report's state tracker for verification/cross-check, since that tracker is kept more current for legislative changes AGA's annual PDF may lag on). This is a **bounded, single-person hand-coding task on the order of days, not months** — comparable in scope to the state-policy coding already done for the Data Center and Flock Cameras paper threads — not a from-scratch 30-state statute-reading project, since the AGA guide gives a real starting scaffold rather than 38 blank state codes.
→ **Buildable, but requires real hand-coding labor; no shortcut dataset exists to skip that step.**

## Design D — Promotional intensity / mobile access → vulnerable-consumer harm

**1. Promotional-intensity data.** NOT FREELY AVAILABLE at the state-panel resolution this design needs. National aggregate ad-spend figures exist and are citable (sportsbooks spent ~$1.2B on advertising in 2024 per multiple trade-press reports; AGA commissioned a Nielsen study on 2024 ad trends), but these are **national totals and channel-level trends, not a state-by-state or state-by-quarter panel** a causal design requires. The underlying tracking data (Nielsen, Comperemedia/Vivvix/Kantar-style ad-monitoring panels) is commercial and was not found free or academically mirrored this session. No public dataset of bonus-offer frequency or sponsorship-deal counts by state was located either. **This is a genuine, confirmed data gap, not a search failure** — treat "no free state-level promotional-intensity data exists" as a real finding.
→ **Not buildable as a proper independent variable from secondary data alone.** The only public proxy available is state *regulatory permissiveness* itself (i.e., a state's advertising-restriction stringency, which is really Design C's dependent variable) — using it as Design D's promotional-intensity proxy would collapse the two designs into one variable rather than two separable constructs.

**2. Subgroup-outcome data (for whether harm concentrates in vulnerable populations).** MIXED.
- **BRFSS** — individual-level microdata with real demographic detail (age, sex, race, income, education) exists, AND CDC has offered states an optional gambling module since 2014. But module adoption is **state-by-state opt-in and varies year to year** — coverage is real but incomplete/inconsistent, a genuine limitation to document rather than assume away.
- **CDC WONDER** (suicide) — state-year with demographic breakdown, but small-cell suppression actively limits granularity in exactly the young-men subgroup the literature flags as highest-risk.
- **NIBRS/FBI Crime Data Explorer** — incident-level, does carry victim/offender age and sex, but agency-level reporting coverage is incomplete and expanded unevenly over time (a potential confound with legalization timing, not just a data-quality footnote).
- **Bankruptcy (US Courts)** — state/district-quarter aggregate only, **no demographic subgroup breakdown available** at all.
→ **Some subgroup analysis is possible (BRFSS gambling module where adopted, WONDER/NIBRS with real granularity limits), but nothing here supports a full "differential harm by vulnerability" design with the resolution and consistency Design D as originally scoped would want.**

## Verdict: Design C is substantially more buildable as a secondary-data-only Study 1

Design C has a verified, free, state-quarter fiscal measure (Census QTAX/STC) and a real-but-labor-intensive path to a policy-laxity measure (AGA guide + Legal Sports Report, hand-coded). Design D's core independent variable — promotional/advertising intensity — has **no free state-panel data source**, which is a hard blocker, not a labor problem like Design C's coding task. Design D's outcome side is partially usable (BRFSS gambling module, with real coverage caveats) but can't stand alone without the promotional-intensity predictor.

**Recommended Study 1 scope:** Lead with **Design C as the main panel design** (state fiscal-dependence → policy-laxity, 2021-present, Census QTAX/STC + hand-coded AGA/Legal Sports Report policy variables), and fold in Design D's outcome side as **secondary/subgroup dependent variables where the data actually supports it** — specifically BRFSS gambling-module states (as an exploratory, coverage-limited harm-by-subgroup check) rather than as the study's primary causal claim. Do not attempt to build a standalone promotional-intensity independent variable from secondary data; that requires primary collection.

**Direct implication for Study 2's primary-data model:** because promotional/advertising exposure cannot be measured from any free secondary source, **Study 2's survey instrument should directly ask bettors about their perceived promotional/advertising exposure** (frequency of bonus offers seen, sportsbook ad recall, push-notification frequency) — this is the construct Study 1 cannot answer with existing data, and it should become a core measured construct in Study 2's model rather than an assumed mechanism. Similarly, if Study 1's hand-coded state policy variables show real state-to-state variation in consumer-protection stringency, Study 2 should include *state of residence's* policy stringency as a moderator on individual-level survey outcomes (financial distress, perceived vulnerability), directly linking Study 1's macro findings to Study 2's micro model.
