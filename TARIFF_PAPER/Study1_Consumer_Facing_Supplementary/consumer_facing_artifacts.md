## Artifact 1 — KDAR Company (Hot Max brand)

- **Date:** April 4, 2025
- **Source:** https://www.kdarco.com/news/letter-to-customers-regarding-changing-tariffs
- **Audience:** Direct customer-facing letter (distributors/end customers of
  the Hot Max tool brand), not an earnings call or IR material.
- **Verbatim/paraphrased content (per direct fetch):** Names specific tariff
  actions explicitly — IEEPA 10% tariffs (Feb 4 & Mar 3, 2025), Section 232
  steel/aluminum tariffs at 25% (Feb 11, 2025), reciprocal tariffs on China
  (34%) and India (26%) (Apr 3, 2025). States: "Our primary goal is to manage
  tariff costs without placing an undue financial burden on you," while
  acknowledging "the current political climate may lead to unavoidable
  adjustments." Commits to 30-day notice on price changes where possible; a
  Feb 2025 price change was announced effective May 15, 2025 (90-day notice).
  Frames itself as the "importer of record" that must pay tariffs within 21
  days or face customs delays.
- **Classification:**
  - Attribution Frame: **Explicit** — names specific legal bases (IEEPA,
    Section 232, reciprocal tariffs) by name, more granular than most of the
    locked corpus's investor-facing statements.
  - Cost-Response: **Restraint signal, not a clean absorption or
    full-pass-through claim** — "manage...without undue burden" reads as an
    intent-to-restrain statement, but the letter stops short of quantifying
    or promising absorption, and explicitly flags "unavoidable adjustments"
    coming.
  - Theme fit: **Theme 2 (causation attribution, explicit)** clearly;
    **Theme 1 (restraint signaling)** as a softer, hedged version — notably
    paired with explicit attribution, which is directly relevant to the H3
    co-occurrence question (see analysis file).

## Artifact 2 — General/aggregated pattern note (not a single citable artifact)

Multiple marketing/consulting sources (Clutch.co, Boost Pricing, Responsory,
Mailchimp, TrackStreet, Tinuiti, Brandit — see search below) converge on a
**template pattern** for how companies are being advised to write
tariff-related price-increase communications to consumers: name the external
cause (tariffs) explicitly for legitimacy, express reluctance/restraint
("we've absorbed as much as we can," "we delayed this as long as possible"),
give advance notice, and avoid the topic being read as opportunistic. This
is consultant/marketer *advice* content, not verified real company output,
so it is **not logged as a data artifact** — but it is worth noting as
independent, non-academic confirmation that the restraint-signaling +
explicit-attribution pairing (Theme 1 + Theme 2) is the advised industry
norm, not just a pattern this project's academic corpus happened to find.
Cited only as motivating context, not as evidence.

## Artifact 3 — Amazon Haul tariff-surcharge reversal (April 29, 2025)

- **Date:** April 29, 2025
- **Sources (cross-confirmed, 5+ independent outlets):**
  - https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/29/amazon-slams-the-door-on-displaying-tariff-surcharges-wisely-diffuses-white-house-attacks.html
  - https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/29/amazon-considers-displaying-tariff-surcharge-on-low-cost-haul-products.html
  - https://www.cbsnews.com/news/amazon-tariffs-tariff-price-karoline-leavitt-trump-hostile-political-act/
  - https://www.cnn.com/2025/04/29/business/white-house-calls-report-that-amazon-is-adding-a-tariff-charge-a-hostile-action
  - https://www.axios.com/2025/04/29/tariffs-amazon-prime-day-sellers-report
- **What happened:** Punchbowl News reported Amazon's budget "Haul" storefront
  was considering displaying an itemized tariff/import-charge line next to
  product prices — the most explicit possible form of causation attribution
  (a literal cost breakdown, more granular than anything in the locked
  15-artifact corpus). After White House pressure (Trump personally called
  Bezos; press secretary called it "a hostile and political act"), Amazon
  reversed course same-day: a spokesperson said the idea was "never approved"
  for the main site, was only ever under consideration for Haul specifically,
  and confirmed "nothing has been implemented on any Amazon properties."
- **Classification:**
  - Attribution Frame: **Would have been maximally explicit** (line-item
    breakdown) — but ultimately reverted to **silent/no attribution** (no
    surcharge shown anywhere, on any Amazon property).
  - Cost-Response: Not directly about absorption/pass-through per se — this
    is about *disclosure* of an already-passed-through cost, not a
    restraint/absorption signal.
  - Theme fit: **Best mapped to the "reversal narrative" deviant-case
    pattern** (Home Depot in the locked corpus) — a real second example of a
    company's public position on tariff communication shifting under
    external pressure within days, not the six static-framing themes. Unlike
    Home Depot's shift (economic pressure driving *more* disclosure over
    time), Amazon's reversal ran the opposite direction: maximum disclosure
    proposed, then fully withdrawn under political pressure. Worth noting
    this is the single most well-documented, highest-profile artifact in
    this supplementary pass — real, verifiable, cross-confirmed by 5+
    major outlets same-day.

## Artifact 4 — Dame (sexual wellness DTC brand): explicit checkout-page tariff surcharge

- **Date:** 2025 (reported via CBS News)
- **Source:** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-surcharge-prices/
- **What happened:** Dame added a literal **$5 "Trump tariff surcharge"** line
  item, automatically applied at online checkout — the real-world version of
  what Amazon considered and abandoned for Haul (Artifact 3). CEO stated the
  fee "doesn't cover all of its extra costs" (explicit partial pass-through,
  quantifiable) and said the goal was for customers to "understand why it's
  more expensive—that it's because of political decisions."
- **Classification:**
  - Attribution Frame: **Maximally explicit** — names the cause in the
    checkout line-item text itself, more direct than any artifact in the
    locked corpus.
  - Cost-Response: **Quantified partial absorption** — the $5 surcharge is
    stated to not cover the full extra cost, meaning the company is eating
    the remainder. Directly parallel to Birkenstock's quantified
    partial-absorption logic in the locked corpus.
  - Theme fit: **Theme 2 (causation attribution, explicit) + Theme 3
    (mitigation-effort narrative, quantified variant)** — real evidence
    these two co-occur in genuine consumer-facing (not investor-facing)
    communication, and via the single most transparent channel possible
    (the checkout cart itself).

## Artifact 5 — Honeywell Building Automation: explicit percentage surcharge

- **Date:** Effective March 4, 2025
- **Source:** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-surcharge-prices/
- **What happened:** Applied a 6.4% "tariff surcharge" to building management
  systems (software excluded), explicitly framed as temporary — committed to
  removing it "as soon as such tariffs are no longer in force."
- **Classification:** Attribution: explicit. Cost-response: full pass-through
  of a specific, bounded percentage, explicitly time-limited. Audience is
  B2B/industrial buyers, not individual consumers — logged for completeness
  but weaker fit for the consumer-facing gap this pass targets than Dame or
  Amazon.
  - Theme fit: **Theme 2 (explicit attribution)**, closer to full
    pass-through than restraint/mitigation.

## Artifact 6 — Labucq (DTC footwear brand): full pass-through via social media

- **Date:** April 15 and May 7, 2025 (two sequential increases)
- **Source:** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-surcharge-prices/
- **What happened:** Announced two price increases (~10% each) via social
  media, tied to the 20% EU tariff, framed as necessary to "remain
  sustainable." Not packaged as a distinct line-item surcharge — folded into
  the sticker price itself.
- **Classification:** Attribution: explicit-but-general (ties increase to
  tariffs, doesn't cite a specific statute/section the way KDAR or Williams-
  Sonoma do). Cost-response: **full pass-through**, no absorption claim.
  - Theme fit: closest real match to a **silent-restraint-absent** cell —
    explicit cause, no mitigation signal — directly useful as a contrast
    case for the co-occurrence analysis (see next file).
