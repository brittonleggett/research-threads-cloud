# Tariff Study 1 — Validation Pilot: Blind Coding Worksheet (Full Corpus, 2026-08-27)

**For the second coder (grad assistant), not AI-generated content beyond the neutral facts
below.** Supersedes the 2026-08-13 5-artifact version — that one was built for Britton alone
to code and was never finished. This version covers the **full 15-artifact corpus**, not a
subsample.

**Why the full corpus, not a subsample:** classic content-analysis convention (Neuendorf 2002)
recommends double-coding at least 10% of a sample or 50 units, whichever is larger — but that
rule is built for large-N media samples and produces a nonsensical floor at this corpus size
(10% of 15 is 1.5 artifacts). Our own key methodological precedent, AlGhamdi (2026,
*International Journal of Qualitative Methods*, the Claude-Code-specific validation study this
paper's methods section leans on directly), double-coded its **entire** 16-transcript corpus
against AI output and explicitly names "only one human analyst, no second human coder" as its
#1 acknowledged limitation — this worksheet closes that exact gap. A published *Journal of
Consumer Marketing* qualitative study (Vol. 43, Issue 5, "But you weren't there!") also used
full-sample independent double-coding with only a narrative reliability description, no formal
statistic — so reporting Gwet's AC1 here, as planned, already exceeds precedent at the target
venue.

**How to do this:**
1. For each artifact, read the neutral facts (click the source link(s) for fuller context if
   you want it), then fill in:
   - **Your codes** — short inductive labels for what's happening rhetorically/strategically in
     the artifact. However many feel right — don't force a count, and don't worry about matching
     any "correct" number.
   - **Anything latent/interpretive you noticed** that a surface reading might miss (tone, what's
     conspicuously *not* said, audience-shifting, hedging, etc.) — this is the layer most likely
     to diverge from the AI's coding, per the literature, so it's the most valuable thing to flag.
2. Once you've coded all 15, look across them and note any patterns/clusters you see in your own
   words — your own candidate themes, before seeing the AI's.
3. **Don't open `Study1_Validation_Pilot_AI_CODES_SEALED_2026-08-27_FULL_CORPUS.md` until you're
   done** — that file has the AI's codes for these same 15 artifacts, kept separate so this stays
   a genuine blind comparison, not an anchored one.
4. When you're done, tell Britton (or whoever's running this) and the agreement stats (Gwet's
   AC1 at the code level and separately at the theme level) get computed from the comparison,
   plus a quote-verification spot-check and an error log, feeding into a reviewer-facing
   Validation Report and a plain-language Coauthor Brief.

Order below matches the corpus table's numbering, not draw order (this is a full-corpus
exercise, not a random sample) — don't read significance into it.
