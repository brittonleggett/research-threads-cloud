## Data (revised)

The corpus consisted of publicly available corporate communications — earnings-call
transcripts, official press statements, and price bulletins — addressing tariff-driven
pricing decisions, drawn from **15** firms spanning apparel, toys, automotive, HVAC, building
materials, home goods, furniture, footwear, general merchandise, steel/industrial, and food
service. Eleven of the 15 artifacts were verified against primary or near-primary sources
(official investor-relations pages, earnings-call transcripts hosted directly by the company,
or on-the-record statements to a named outlet, cross-corroborated across independent sources);
the remaining four (La-Z-Boy, Birkenstock, Insteel Industries, Home Depot) were verified via
cross-corroborated web search results pending a direct-fetch confirmation pass, which has been
queued since 2026-08-14 behind a network-wide tool outage rather than left undone by choice.
**[PENDING]** That direct-fetch pass, and a primary-source (8-K exhibit, investor-relations
page) upgrade for the original seven artifacts (currently secondary-sourced news coverage of
primary events), remain outstanding before final analysis. Because the corpus consists
exclusively of publicly available corporate disclosures with no interaction with or data
collection from human subjects, Study 1 was determined exempt from IRB review under 45 CFR
46.102(e).

## Coding Procedure and Preliminary Themes (revised)

**[PENDING — provisional, awaiting final Phase 3 sign-off]** Inductive coding of the corpus to
date has surfaced five candidate themes, subject to the researcher's ongoing review: (1)
*restraint signaling*, in which firms frame price actions as the minimum necessary response;
(2) *causation attribution*, ranging from explicit naming of tariffs as cause to vague or
silent framing, present in some form across the full corpus and the strongest candidate for a
theoretically motivated experimental manipulation; (3) *mitigation-effort narrative*, in which
price increases are paired with visible internal cost-reduction or sourcing efforts; (4)
*normalization through relabeling*, in which price increases are framed as routine rather than
exceptional; and a fifth, single-case-supported candidate, (5) *asymmetric disclosure* between
price increases and decreases, which requires additional corpus support before being treated as
a stable theme. A sixth candidate — *full-absorption promise*, surfaced in the corpus-expansion
pass — remains an open Phase 3 decision as to whether it constitutes a distinct theme or a
variant of restraint signaling; that decision is complicated by a verification finding not yet
reflected in the 08-11 draft: the artifact supporting it (Chipotle) is dated a full year earlier
than the rest of the corpus (March 2025 vs. 2026), raising a scope question about whether it
represents the same messaging environment as the rest of the sample.

The corpus-expansion verification passes (2026-08-12 through 08-14) also surfaced six further
candidate codes not yet clustered into the theme map above: `no-further-increase-anticipated`,
`partial-absorption-quantified`, `partial-pass-through-explicit`, `quantified-cost-absorption`,
`reversal-narrative`, and `section-232-specific`. At least three of these
(`partial-absorption-quantified`, `partial-pass-through-explicit`, `quantified-cost-absorption`)
read as plausible enrichments of existing Theme 3 (mitigation-effort narrative) rather than
distinct themes, but that clustering judgment belongs to Phase 3, not to this coding pass.
`section-232-specific` is a legal-basis metadata tag rather than a thematic code, relevant only
insofar as the corpus's data-collection window spans multiple distinct tariff-authority regimes
(IEEPA, Section 122, Section 301, and separately Section 232) — a framing question already
flagged for the researcher and not revisited here.
