## What Consensus found

**Campbell (1999) itself does not appear to spell out the exact item
wording in extractable form** — consistent with the earlier verification
pass's dead end (two direct PDF fetch attempts blocked by binary/encoded
streams). Consensus's Deep Research read what full text it could access and
explicitly reported: "The provided text does not contain the exact scale
items or full wording for the inferred motive or opportunism measures from
the 1999 paper itself."

**But Campbell's own 2007 follow-up paper documents how she operationalized
the same inferred-motive/opportunism construct, building directly on the
1999 work.** Per Consensus's synthesis of Campbell (2007):

- **Inferred motive:** participants rated the motive for the price change
  on a 7-point scale anchored "bad" (1) to "good" (7).
- **Opportunism ("take advantage") item:** agreement with the statement
  *"The intent in this situation was to take advantage of you (the
  customer)"* — 7-point scale anchored "agree" (1) to "disagree" (7).
  **Note the reverse coding** — agree is the low end (1), not the high end,
  which is the opposite convention from most Likert fairness/agreement
  scales in this instrument (e.g., Chaudhuri & Holbrook's trust items are
  standard strongly-disagree(1)–strongly-agree(7)). If this item is
  adopted, either flip the anchors for consistency with the rest of the
  instrument or clearly reverse-score it in analysis — don't silently mix
  scale directions across the instrument.

Campbell (2007) is described (via Consensus) as extending the 1999
framework by showing both inferred motive *and* stimulus-induced affect
mediate price-fairness perceptions, with information source (human vs.
nonhuman) as a moderator — a different design than what this project needs,
but the *scale operationalization* is the same underlying construct as
1999, from the same author.
