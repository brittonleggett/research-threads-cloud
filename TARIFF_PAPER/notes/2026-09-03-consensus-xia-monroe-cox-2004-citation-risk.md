# 2026-09-03 — Xia, Monroe & Cox (2004) citation risk: it's a conceptual
paper, not a scale source

Follow-up Consensus.app Deep Research query, same session as
`2026-09-03-consensus-campbell-1999-opportunism-scale.md`. This one is a
**warning, not a resolution** — flag before the instrument locks.

## What Consensus found

**Xia, L., Monroe, K.B., & Cox, J.L. (2004), "The Price Is Unfair! A
Conceptual Framework of Price Fairness Perceptions," *Journal of
Marketing*, 68(4), 1–15, is a conceptual/theoretical integration paper —
not an empirical study.** It synthesizes dual entitlement, equity theory,
and procedural justice into a framework and argues price fairness and
unfairness are distinct constructs (with affect — anger, outrage —
distinguishing "truly unfair" from merely "less fair"). **It does not
introduce or test a multi-item scale.** Consensus's explicit bottom line:
"The 2004 Journal of Marketing paper does not contain exact scale items,
semantic differential wording, or an opportunism measure."

This directly contradicts `notes/2026-08-04-scale-items-verification-
status.md`'s "moderate confidence" reconstruction, which attributed a
3-item semantic-differential scale — unfair/fair, unacceptable/acceptable,
unreasonable/reasonable — to this paper. **That attribution appears to be
wrong**, or at minimum unconfirmed and likely misattributed.

## The likely actual source — a live lead, not yet confirmed

The *same* wording pattern ("unfair/fair," "unreasonable/reasonable," plus
a third item) showed up in the **Campbell (1999)** search from the same
Consensus session, in this note from that project's own earlier
verification pass (`2026-08-04-scale-items-verification-status.md`,
Campbell section): *"one search summary described 'a three-item measure of
price fairness perceptions using a 1-7 scale with the items unfair/fair,
unreasonable/reasonable, and wrong/right' in connection with this paper."*
That's Campbell's paper, not Xia/Monroe/Cox's — and it's a much closer
structural match (three semantic-differential items) than a conceptual
paper with no scale at all.

**Working hypothesis: the "unfair/unacceptable/unreasonable"-style fairness
scale this project has been reconstructing may actually belong to Campbell
(1999)'s *fairness* measure (a different item from the opportunism/
"take advantage" item resolved in the companion note), not to Xia, Monroe &
Cox (2004).** Not confirmed — needs a direct pull of Campbell (1999) to
check, same as the opportunism item.

## Where an actual empirical scale might legitimately live, if not Campbell

Consensus pointed to related *empirical* papers by the same authors that
Xia et al. (2004)'s framework fed into — any of these could be where a
real, testable fairness scale first appears, if it isn't Campbell's:
- Xia & Monroe (2010) — transaction value vs. fairness, three experiments.
- Xia, Monroe & Cox (2010) — effort/deservingness as fairness antecedents.
- Herrmann, Xia, Monroe & Huber (2007) — 246 car buyers, fairness/
  satisfaction survey.
- Kukar-Kinney, Xia & Monroe (2007) — price-matching policy fairness,
  three experiments.

## Recommendation
1. **Do not cite Xia, Monroe & Cox (2004) as the source of a fairness
   scale in the Method section** — it's the right theory citation (dual
   entitlement/equity/procedural-justice synthesis, and it's already
   correctly used that way in `Introduction_and_Theory_DRAFT_2026-08-12.md`
   for the *theoretical* framing) but the wrong citation for item wording.
2. Before the instrument locks, check Campbell (1999) directly for a
   fairness-perception scale (separate from the opportunism item already
   resolved) — this is the more promising lead than the four related-paper
   options above.
3. If Campbell (1999) doesn't pan out, the four related empirical papers
   above are the next places to check, roughly in the order listed (Xia &
   Monroe 2010 first, as the most directly on-topic given the "distinguish
   fairness from unfairness" framing already used in the Introduction
   draft).
4. This is exactly the kind of citation-precision issue the project's own
   2026-07-08 overnight pass caught before (the Rawal et al. author-list
   error) — worth a dedicated citation-accuracy pass on the full Method
   section once the actual scale source is confirmed, not just this one
   item.
