# 2026-08-04 — Vignette drafts, v1 (pending your review + pretest)

Fictional retailer used throughout: **"Meridian Home"** — generic national retailer, no real-brand
language borrowed from Study 1's actual corpus (Nike/Mattel/etc. text is not reused, per the
data-provenance discussion — these are new stimuli, only the *rhetorical patterns* are
informed by what the real corpus showed). All 6 versions held constant on: brand name, format
(short email from the company), subject line, opening/closing framing, and roughly matched
word count (~55-65 words) to avoid a length confound in the pretest.

**Shared instructions (goes before all 6, respondent sees only one):**
> Imagine you are a regular customer of Meridian Home, a national retailer where you often shop.
> You receive the following email from the company:
>
> **Subject: An Update on Pricing at Meridian Home**

---

## Cell 1 — Tariff-explicit × Full pass-through
> As you may have seen in the news, new tariffs on imported goods have significantly
> increased the cost of many of the products we sell. Because of these tariff-related costs,
> we are raising prices on select items starting next month. These increases reflect the full
> amount of the added tariff costs we are now paying to bring these products to you.

## Cell 2 — Tariff-explicit × Shared-burden
> As you may have seen in the news, new tariffs on imported goods have significantly
> increased the cost of many of the products we sell. Because of these tariff-related costs,
> we are raising prices on select items starting next month. We are absorbing a significant
> portion of these added tariff costs ourselves, and only passing along part of the increase
> to our customers.

## Cell 3 — General-cost-explicit × Full pass-through
> Due to rising costs across our supply chain, the cost of many of the products we sell has
> significantly increased. Because of these higher costs, we are raising prices on select
> items starting next month. These increases reflect the full amount of the added costs we
> are now paying to bring these products to you.

## Cell 4 — General-cost-explicit × Shared-burden
> Due to rising costs across our supply chain, the cost of many of the products we sell has
> significantly increased. Because of these higher costs, we are raising prices on select
> items starting next month. We are absorbing a significant portion of these added costs
> ourselves, and only passing along part of the increase to our customers.

## Cell 5 — Silent × Full pass-through
> We value you as a customer and want to keep you informed about changes at Meridian Home.
> Prices on select items will be increasing starting next month. This adjustment reflects the
> full increase in what it now costs us to bring these products to you. We appreciate your
> continued business and look forward to serving you.

## Cell 6 — Silent × Shared-burden
> We value you as a customer and want to keep you informed about changes at Meridian Home.
> Prices on select items will be increasing starting next month. We are absorbing a
> significant portion of the increase in what it now costs us to bring these products to you,
> and only passing along part of it. We appreciate your continued business and look forward
> to serving you.

---

## One design tension worth your explicit sign-off

The Silent condition (Cells 5-6) still references "cost" in a generic, unattributed way
("what it now costs us") — it has to, or there's nothing for the Cost-Response factor
(full/shared) to manipulate in those two cells. This is a compromise, not true silence in the
sense Study 1's real BMW artifact showed (which mentioned no cost concept at all, just framed
the increase as routine). Two ways to resolve, your call:

1. **Keep as drafted** — defensible, and close to how Campbell (1999) and Xia/Monroe/Cox
   handle attribution-ambiguity conditions in the literature (attribution is about *whether a
   cause is named*, not whether "cost" as a concept appears at all).
2. **Make Silent truly silent** — drop the Cost-Response manipulation from the Silent cells
   entirely and treat them as 2 additional single cells outside the full factorial (i.e., a
   2×2 + 2 design, 6 cells total but not a clean 3×2) — more faithful to the real BMW example,
   but breaks the clean factorial structure and complicates the ANOVA.

I'd lean toward option 1 (keep as drafted) since it preserves the clean 3×2 design that's
already grounded in Study 1's themes, but flag this explicitly rather than deciding
unilaterally.
