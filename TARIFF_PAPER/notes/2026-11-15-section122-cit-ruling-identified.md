# 2026-11-15 Section 122 CIT ruling identified (closes T013)

Source level: one law-firm article read directly (Skadden, May 2026) plus search-result headlines from several other firms. The court opinion itself was not read.

- Case: Oregon v. United States and Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States, decided together at the Court of International Trade. Slip Op. 26-47 (as cited by Skadden). Docket numbers not found.
- Decided 2026-05-07, 2-1 (Barnett and Kelly majority, Stanceu dissent). Holding: the 10% global tariff under Section 122 was unlawful because the President did not identify the balance-of-payments deficit the statute requires.
- Relief was party-specific: injunction and refunds with interest only for three importers (Washington State, Burlap and Barrel, Basic Fun). Other importers still pay. Non-importer state plaintiffs were dismissed for lack of standing. Search snippets disagree on who is covered (one says two importers plus Washington); Skadden's version is the one I read.
- Government appealed to the CAFC on 2026-05-08; temporary stay granted 2026-05-12 (Skadden). Current CAFC status NOT found. Section 122 tariffs were set to expire July 24 absent congressional extension; I did not verify what happened after that.
- Context: follows Learning Resources v. Trump (Feb 2026, IEEPA tariffs struck down), per the search summary; not independently checked tonight.

Why it matters for the paper: the legal basis for the surcharges firms cite keeps changing, and refunds went only to a few importers. That is relevant to Study 2 realism (a firm blaming "tariffs" for a price rise when the tariff's legality is contested) and to any refund pass-through framing.
