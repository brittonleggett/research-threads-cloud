# Tariff Paper — Social Listening Scan (2026-08-13)

Scope: live Reddit search via the Chrome extension for consumer discourse on tariff-driven price increases, to check whether real public conversation validates (or complicates) Study 1's five candidate themes / Study 2's Attribution Frame × Cost-Response design. Read actual comment threads, not just headlines.

## Headline finding: consumer discourse maps cleanly onto the existing theme/factor structure

The top-voted current thread, r/AdviceAnimals "Privatize the profits, socialize the tariffs" (23d old, 11K votes, 286 comments — https://www.reddit.com/r/AdviceAnimals/comments/1v2numu/), is essentially a live public argument over exactly the constructs Study 1/2 already formalize:

- **Attribution skepticism (maps to Theme 2, causation attribution)**: multiple commenters explicitly frame corporate tariff-attribution as a pretext — "Naive. Companies are going to pocket that money and keep the price high cuz (lame excuse)"; "This way the corporations get paid twice for gouging us all." This is the consumer-side mirror of the *silent/vague/explicit* attribution spectrum Study 1 already codes on the corporate-messaging side — worth citing as real-world motivation for why Attribution Frame is worth manipulating experimentally (people don't take corporate tariff-attribution at face value).
- **Partial vs. full passthrough debate (maps to Cost-Response factor)**: a long, well-reasoned comment from a business owner (`cubbiesnextyr`) walks through their own company's real mitigation mix — vendor renegotiation, margin absorption, and a partial (7-10%) price increase despite a 75-95% tariff rate on their inputs — i.e., a real-world case of the *partial-absorption* condition, given in the commenter's own words. Another comment (`AndyVZ`) gives a detailed lay explanation of why companies typically eat *some* tariff cost rather than fully passing it through (contract timing, price-elasticity limits, economy-of-scale loss). Genuinely well-informed, useful as texture/quotes if the paper wants a "does the public actually understand cost-passthrough mechanics" angle.
- **Sticky-price cynicism**: "People don't seem to understand that even if inflation numbers come down, prices will never come down. You've already proven to the company that you're willing to pay that price" — a distinct, related-but-separate belief (permanent price ratchet, not just tariff attribution) that shows up unprompted and might be worth distinguishing from the Attribution Frame /Cost-Response manipulation in the Discussion section if reviewers ask "why would consumers care about attribution at all if they think prices are sticky regardless."

## Relevance / recommendation

- **Not a new data source for Study 1 or Study 2/3** — Study 1's corpus is corporate messaging (documents/press releases/IR calls), and Study 2/3 are a controlled vignette experiment, so this doesn't slot into either as-is. But it's strong **motivating/contextual material for the Introduction or Discussion**: real, current (23-days-old, 11K-voted) public discourse that independently reproduces the paper's core theoretical distinctions (attribution skepticism, partial vs. full passthrough) without having been prompted by the study design — good "this isn't just an academic abstraction" evidence.
- Possible **future Study 4 / extension paper idea**: consumer-side netnography of tariff-price-increase reactions (parallel to the CCS paper's Track C), to pair with the existing corporate-messaging Study 1. Not suggesting this now given Study 1/2/3 are already the priority, but worth a line in `Claude_Knowledge/Research_Stream_Ideas.md` if that file's still being maintained.
- Reddit search itself is noisy on broad terms ("tariffs price increase" sitewide, sort=new, returns mostly keyword-collision junk); sort=top/t=month surfaced the real signal. Same lesson as the CCS scan done same day — subreddit-restricted or top-sorted search works, sitewide-new does not.
