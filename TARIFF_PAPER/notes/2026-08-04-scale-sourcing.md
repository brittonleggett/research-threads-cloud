# 2026-08-04 — Scale sourcing for the finalized lean model

Recommended validated scales for each construct, per the design locked in
`2026-08-04-design-locked-jcm-fit.md`. All are established, widely-cited marketing scales —
**exact item wording should still be pulled directly from the original articles** (via
McNeese library/Google Scholar) before finalizing the Qualtrics instrument; what's below is
accurate on scale identity, structure, and citation, not verified verbatim item text.

## Perceived Price Fairness
**Xia, Monroe & Cox (2004), "The Price Is Unfair! A Conceptual Framework of Price Fairness
Perceptions," *Journal of Marketing*, 68, 1–15.** The standard citation for price-fairness
measurement in this literature — semantic-differential/Likert items on fair–unfair,
acceptable–unacceptable, reasonable–unreasonable, typically 7-point. Often paired with
Campbell (1999) below for convergent items. This is the construct Study 1's Theme 1
(restraint signaling) and Theme 4 (normalization) map onto most directly.

## Perceived Opportunism (inferred firm motive)
**Campbell (1999), "Perceptions of Price Unfairness: Antecedents and Consequences,"
*Journal of Marketing Research*, 36(2), 187–199.** Measures whether the price increase is
attributed to a legitimate/cost-driven motive vs. an exploitative/profit-driven one — exactly
the causation-attribution logic behind the Attribution Frame manipulation and Study 1's
Theme 2. This is the most load-bearing citation in the whole design, since it's the paper
that established that *inferred motive* (not just the price change itself) drives fairness
judgments — directly justifies why Attribution Frame is manipulated at all.

## Trust in the Company
**Chaudhuri & Holbrook (2001), "The Chain of Effects from Brand Trust and Brand Affect to
Brand Performance," *Journal of Marketing*, 65(2), 81–93.** One-dimensional brand-trust scale
(reliance on the brand to perform as stated) — this is also the paper cited in
`user_research_corpus.md` as recurring in Britton's own MLB brand-equity paper's theoretical
lineage, so it's already inside his citation comfort zone. Consider supplementing with
Delgado-Ballester (2003) if reviewers want a two-dimensional (reliability + intentionality)
trust measure — flag as an option, not a requirement.

## Purchase Intention
**Dodds, Monroe & Grewal (1991), "Effects of Price, Brand, and Store Information on Buyers'
Product Evaluations," *Journal of Marketing Research*, 28(3), 307–319.** The field-standard
3-item purchase-intention scale (likelihood/willingness/probability of buying), 7-point
Likert. Ubiquitous in pricing-experiment literature — safe, expected choice for a JCM
reviewer.

## Word-of-Mouth Intention
**Maxham & Netemeyer (2002), *Journal of Marketing*, 66(4), 57–71** (service-recovery /
WOM intentions) — items on likelihood of saying positive things about the company,
recommending it, encouraging others to do business with it. Zeithaml, Berry & Parasuraman
(1996) is the other common citation for this construct if Maxham & Netemeyer's exact items
don't fit the retail-pricing context as cleanly on inspection — worth comparing both before
locking the final item set.

## Manipulation checks (custom, not from a published scale)
Two checks needed per the 3×2 design:
1. **Attribution Frame recall** — multiple-choice: "According to the message, what did the
   company say was the reason for the price increase?" (tariffs / general rising costs / no
   reason given), plus a continuous "how clearly did the company explain the reason" item.
2. **Cost-Response recall** — multiple-choice or continuous item on whether the company said
   it was passing along the full cost increase or absorbing part of it.
