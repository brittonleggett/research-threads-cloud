# 2026-08-28 — Refund-era wave addendum: Home Depot's actual refund figure found, Walmart's too

Follow-up to `2026-08-27-webfetch-check-refund-wave-expansion-lazboy-wsm-new-calls-scotus-still-
pending.md`, which had Target/Lowe's/Williams-Sonoma refund postures but not Walmart's or Home
Depot's own refund *dollar figures* (only their earlier 2025 pricing-stance statements). Direct-
fetched [Supply Chain Dive's roundup](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/how-big-box-retailers-are-using-tariff-refunds/828717/)
(published after the Q2 2026 earnings-call wave, mid-to-late Aug 2026) — this closes that gap
and adds real numbers, still verify-and-hold per the standing corpus-scope rule, not inserted.

**Six distinct corporate postures now on record for this same refund wave, not five:**

- **Walmart** — **$2.9 billion** refund, "substantially all" received as of the Aug 2026 Q2 call.
  CFO **John David Rainey**: "Looking forward, our Q3 guidance reflects the continued impact of
  pricing actions taken in Q2 alongside continued prioritization of tariff refunds" — i.e., used
  for price reductions on groceries and general merchandise. This is the same Rainey already in
  the corpus (artifact 13) for an earlier, different quote ("tariff-related costs lifted prices
  across many categories") — now he has a second, later, refund-specific quote on record too.
- **Target** — refined to ~$1B ("significant majority" of what was applied for), $994M of it
  booked as a cost-of-sales reduction. New named executive: CFO **Jim Lee** — "We think that's a
  better measurement, just especially given the timing of how the refunds are coming through our
  P&L."
- **Home Depot** — **new figure, not previously in any note: $730 million**, majority received
  near end of June 2026, $45M more still clearing via inventory. Used to offset fuel/energy/input
  cost pressure through year-end, not price cuts. **Real quote from CFO Richard McPhail** (his
  actual, correctly-attributed quote — distinct from the Aug 2025 pricing-stance quote that's
  actually EVP Bastek's, per the same-day correction note): "Tariff refunds are a market-borne
  benefit, they're not unique to The Home Depot."
- **Lowe's** — confirmed $80M, "smaller portion" of what was paid in, more being filed for. CEO
  **Marvin Ellison** (new named executive) on declining to match rivals' refund-funded price cuts:
  "We did not choose then to match some of those promotions because they were not in our
  financial plan."

**Why this matters:** the refund-wave corpus-scope question (still Britton's call, not resolved
here) now has richer material than the 08-27 note captured — Walmart's $2.9B is the largest single
figure in the whole set, and it's a *price-cut* posture like Target's, while Home Depot's $730M is
a *cost-offset* posture like Lowe's — meaning the four largest home/general-merchandise retailers
split 2-and-2 on price-cut vs. cost-offset, a cleaner pattern than the 08-27 note's five-way
mixed read. Combined with the $166B/$86.3B aggregate CBP figures found in tonight's SCOTUS check
(`2026-08-28-direct-fetch-tier-b-upgrade-and-scotus-primary-check.md`), there's now a real
macro-to-micro framing available: ~330,000 importers, $166B collected, $86.3B refunded so far,
with these named companies as illustrative, verified individual cases spanning both postures.
