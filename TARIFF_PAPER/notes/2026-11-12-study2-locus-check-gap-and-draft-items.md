# 2026-11-12 — Does Study 2 measure perceived locus/blame? (No.) Draft add-on items

Draft-only. Answers last night's open question by reading `Study2_Qualtrics_Instrument_READY_2026-09-09.md` (Blocks 4-9). The READY file was NOT edited.

## Finding
- Block 4 manipulation checks cover only: stated reason (tariffs/general costs/none), clarity of explanation, and full vs. partial absorption. These confirm the *manipulations were perceived*, not *how the reader attributed responsibility*.
- No item measures perceived locus, controllability, or blame directed at the firm vs. government. Block 6 (Opportunism: motive good/bad; "intent was to take advantage of you") is the closest, but it is firm-intent, not locus of cause.
- Consequence: if H3 on fairness comes out null (the Morwitz & Fitzsimons-style "blame moves, reactions don't" risk, see 11-11 memo), the data could not distinguish "attribution didn't shift blame" from "blame shifted but fairness didn't follow."

## Proposed addition (Britton's call; nothing adopted)
Place as a short Block 4b right after the manipulation checks, before Fairness (so it doesn't prime). Wording below is my own draft, NOT a validated scale; mark as such in any methods text.
1. "How much was the price increase due to factors outside the company's control?" 1 = Not at all, 7 = Completely.
2. "How much could Meridian Home have avoided this price increase?" 1 = Not at all, 7 = Completely. (reverse-keyed relative to item 1; scoring note needed)
3. "How much is each of the following responsible for the price increase?" (allocation or 7-pt each) — the company / the government / other. Gives firm-vs-government blame directly.

Established alternative: the Revised Causal Dimension Scale (CDSII; McAuley, Duncan & Russell, 1992, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, doi:10.1177/0146167292185006 — DOI/title/authors confirmed via Crossref tonight; items and licensing not checked). It has locus of causality, external control and controllability subscales, but it is written for personal achievement outcomes and would need adaptation; check reuse permissions before adapting item text.

## Tradeoffs to weigh
- Adds 3 items (~30 seconds). Cost small; the gain is interpretability of a null and a candidate mediator for H1 -> fairness.
- Order effect: measuring blame before fairness could prime. Option: randomize Block 4b vs Block 5 order, or put 4b after the outcome blocks (but then it's contaminated by outcome ratings). Recommend before, with a note.
- Item 2 reverse polarity adds a second backward item; Block 6 item 2 already has the unresolved flip decision. Suggest decide both together.
