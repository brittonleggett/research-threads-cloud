## Word-of-Mouth (WOM) — Maxham & Netemeyer (2002) — RESOLVED

Full-text PDF of the actual cited paper was fetched directly and read in full:
Maxham, James G. III, and Richard G. Netemeyer (2002), "A Longitudinal Study of
Complaining Customers' Evaluations of Multiple Service Failures and Recovery
Efforts," *Journal of Marketing*, 66 (4), 57–71.
(https://www.thecustomerconnection.nl/docs/Maxham%20III%20&%20Netemeyer%20-%20A%20longitidunal%20study%20of%20complaining%20customers%20evaluations%20of%20multiple%20service%20failures%20and%20recovery%20efforts.pdf
— a bank-hosted open copy of the actual JM article; content matches the published
citation, journal, volume, issue, and page range exactly.)

Appendix A of the paper ("Measurement Scales") gives the **Favorable WOM** scale
verbatim, 3 items, all 7-point ("strongly disagree" to "strongly agree" except item 1,
a likelihood item), measured at every time period in the study:

1. "How likely are you to spread positive word-of-mouth about [firm name]?"
2. "I would recommend [firm name's] banking services to my friends."
3. "If my friends were looking for a banking service, I would tell them to try [firm
   name]."

Reliability: the paper reports coefficient alpha ranging from .83 to .97 across all
measures and all time periods (not broken out per-construct in the text, but WOM is
included in that range). For adapting to the tariff-messaging context, "banking
services" / "firm name" needs to be genericized to the target company/product framing
used in this study's stimuli — straightforward find-and-replace, not a substantive
change to the construct.

This is a direct, correctly-cited, real quote — not an inference, not a proxy from a
different paper. **This scale can go into the instrument as-is (adapted for
company/product framing) with full confidence in the citation.**

## Purchase Intention — Dodds, Monroe & Grewal (1991) — substantially improved, not fully resolved

Could not access the original 1991 *Journal of Marketing Research* article's own
appendix directly (paywalled, no legitimate open copy found — consistent with last
night's finding and the project's own copyright rule about not pulling paywalled
full text into this repo). But found something better than last night's Fennell et
al. (2025) proxy: a **paper co-authored by Dhruv Grewal himself** (one of the three
original 1991 authors) that explicitly attributes a 3-item scale to Dodds, Monroe &
Grewal (1991) and reproduces the items verbatim in its own published appendix:

Grewal, Dhruv, R. Krishnan, Julie Baker, and Norm Borin (1998), "The Effect of Store
Name, Brand Name and Price Discounts on Consumers' Evaluations and Purchase
Intentions," *Journal of Retailing*, 74 (3), 331–352. Full text fetched directly
(https://www.dhruvgrewal.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/1998-JR-STORE-BRAND-DISCOUNT.pdf
— hosted on the author's own site).

Table 1 of that paper ("Measurement Model"), under "Purchase Intention (PI)," states
explicitly: **"The willingness to buy construct was measured using a three item scale
(Dodds, Monroe, and Grewal, 1991)"** and lists the 3 items (adapted to the paper's own
product, a bicycle — bracket the product name for genericizing):

1. "I would purchase this [bicycle]." (item reliability .92)
2. "I would consider buying at this price." (item reliability .90)
3. "The probability that I would consider buying [this product] is [high]." (item
   reliability .94)

Scale reliability (composite) = .92; variance extracted = .92 — both strong.

**Why this counts as real evidence, and what its limits are:**
- It is a genuine, correctly-cited secondary source, not a guess or an AI
  reconstruction — the wording is quoted from a real published appendix.
- Grewal's own co-authorship on both the 1991 original and this 1998 paper makes this
  about as authoritative a secondary source as is available without pulling the 1991
  JMR text itself — he would know if he were misattributing his own scale.
- It is *not* the verbatim 1991 text — Grewal et al. (1998) adapted item wording to
  their own product (a bicycle, with an explicit price-referencing item, "I would
  consider buying at this price," that ties to their study's price-discount
  manipulation). The 1991 original may have used different exact phrasing for a
  general-purpose (non-price-specific) willingness-to-buy measure. Papers in this
  research tradition routinely adapt this scale's exact wording slightly to their own
  stimuli, which is normal practice but means this is not a word-for-word transcript
  of the 1991 appendix.
- A second, independent corroboration: William B. Dodds's own later solo paper
  (Dodds, William B. (2002), "The Effects of Perceived and Objective Market Cues on
  Consumers' Product Evaluations," *Marketing Bulletin*, 13, Article 2 — open-access,
  fetched and read in full at
  https://marketing-bulletin.massey.ac.nz/V13/MB_V13_A2_Dodds.pdf), which explicitly
  extends "Dodds, Monroe and Grewal's (1991) market cue-product evaluation model,"
  uses a single-item Willingness to Buy measure: **"The probability that I would
  consider buying the [SEARS TELEVISION] is very high"** (Appendix B). This matches
  item 3 above almost exactly and independently confirms that phrasing traces back to
  the DMG 1991 tradition.

**Comparison to this project's existing 3-item draft**
(`2026-08-04-scale-items-verification-status.md`): "The likelihood of purchasing this
product is high" / "If I were going to buy a product like this, I would consider
buying this one" / "The probability that I would consider buying this product is
high." Item 3 of the existing draft is now essentially confirmed correct (matches
Grewal et al.'s item 3 and Dodds's 2002 single item almost word for word). **Items 1
and 2 of the existing draft do not match anything in the Grewal et al. (1998)
reproduction** — the real scale's other two items are "I would purchase this
[product]" and "I would consider buying at this price," not the existing draft's
current phrasing. Recommend replacing items 1–2 of the existing draft with these
before the instrument locks, keeping item 3 as-is.

**Bottom line on Purchase Intention: meaningfully better-sourced than last night
(a real co-author's own published, verbatim reproduction, explicitly cited to Dodds,
Monroe & Grewal 1991, beats the Fennell et al. 2025 proxy on directness of
attribution) — but still short of the original 1991 JMR appendix text itself.** If
Britton has JSTOR/library access this weekend and 5 minutes to spare, pulling the
actual 1991 appendix would be the last remaining verification step; if not, the
Grewal et al. (1998) version is defensible to use directly, with citation to both the
1991 original (construct/attribution) and the 1998 paper (exact item source) in the
methods section.
