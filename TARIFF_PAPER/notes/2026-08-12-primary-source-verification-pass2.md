## #8 IKEA — upgraded, and sharpens its Cost-Response coding

**Original draft entry said:** "Explicit tariff attribution, quantified: sofas +~$50,
bedroom sets +~$100," codes `causation-explicit`, `numeric-transparency`.

**Primary-adjacent source (Ingka Group official statement to the Wall Street
Journal):** Tolga Öncü, Ingka Group's retail manager, on the record: the company has
had to pass on "part of the cost increase to the customers" because it "can't stay
immune to absorb all the costs" itself. Exact price moves confirmed: Uppland sofa
$849 → $899, three-piece oak bedroom set $959 → $1,049 (August pricing). Ingka is also
publicly pursuing domestic sourcing (mattress/upholstery capacity) to reduce future
exposure.

**Why this matters for Phase 3:** this is a genuine **partial pass-through** statement
in the company's own words — "part of the cost," explicitly not all of it — which
sharpens IKEA from an ambiguous Cost-Response case into a clean example for whichever
side of that factor implies shared burden rather than full pass-through. Worth
cross-referencing against how Study 2's Cost-Response vignette wording is drafted.

**Source:** [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/ikea-raises-prices-trumps-furniture-tariffs-hit-retailer)
and [qz.com](https://qz.com/ikea-raising-prices-tariffs-trump-retail), both reporting
the same on-record Ingka/WSJ statement — the Öncü quote is consistent and specific
enough across outlets to treat as reliably attributed, though neither is Ingka's own
press release; still recommend a direct Ingka/IKEA newsroom check before final citation
if one exists.
