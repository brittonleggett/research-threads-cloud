# 2026-11-11 — Positioning H1/H3 against the surcharge-blame literature (analysis memo, draft-only)

Source basis: only what earlier notes already recorded (2026-11-03, 11-04: Crossref abstract of Pallas, Bolton & Lobschat 2018, J Service Research 21(3); Columbia grant-page description of Morwitz & Fitzsimons; Greenleaf et al., JCP 26(1)). No full texts read tonight. Nothing here is a new find; this is a how-it-bears-on-our-hypotheses memo. Phase 3 untouched.

## What each source can and cannot support
1. **Pallas et al. (2018), abstract level.** Surcharge information shifts blame for a price increase by locus of causality: internal-locus surcharges raise blame; external-locus surcharges can make surcharge pricing beneficial vs. all-inclusive pricing.
   - Supports **H1a/H1b direction** (naming an external cause helps) in a service-surcharge setting. Does not test cost *absorption*, so it says nothing directly about H2 or H3.
   - Caveat: a tariff is "external" only if consumers see it that way. Our theory draft already notes consumers may attribute tariffs to the firm's sourcing choices. So the locus of a tariff cue is itself something to measure (a manipulation check on perceived locus/controllability), not assume.
2. **Morwitz & Fitzsimons (tariff itemization, per Columbia page description).** As previously noted: blame shifts to government but reactions can stay negative. If true, it is a boundary condition on H1: attribution may move blame without moving fairness/purchase intent. Unverified beyond the grant-page text; no working paper located.
3. **Greenleaf et al. (2016) partitioned-pricing review.** Background on itemized add-ons; relevant to how an itemized tariff line is read, not to absorption.

## What this means for H3 (word vs. deed)
- None of the three tests attribution **crossed with** firm cost-response. H3's compensatory (cue-substitution) claim therefore remains un-preempted by these sources, consistent with the 09-10 check. (Campbell et al. publishes the H2 main effect only, per CLAUDE.md; not re-reported here.)
- The Morwitz-Fitzsimons "blame moves but reactions don't" result suggests a **competing reading of H3's pattern**: attribution might matter for blame and opportunism (H1b) but not for fairness/intent outcomes, in which case H3 on fairness could be null even if blame shifts. Suggests keeping perceived blame/locus as a measured mediator or manipulation check so a null can be interpreted.
- A possible additional prediction (for Britton to accept or drop, not adopted): under full pass-through, an external-locus attribution should reduce *firm-directed blame*; under absorption, blame is already low (floor), which is the same compensatory logic as H3 but measured on blame rather than fairness.
