## #11 Chipotle — upgraded, with a correction

**Original draft entry said:** "CEO explicitly stated intent to absorb tariff costs...
('It is our intent as we sit here today to absorb those costs')," implicitly dated
alongside the rest of the 2026 corpus.

**Correction found:** the quote is real and correctly transcribed, but it's from an
**NBC Nightly News interview aired March 2, 2025** — a full year before the rest of
this corpus's artifacts (which are 2026). CEO Scott Boatwright also referenced, in an
earlier earnings call, an expected ~60 basis point ongoing impact on cost of sales if
tariffs took effect. Context worth keeping: Chipotle sources only ~2% of ingredients
from Mexico and <0.5% combined from Canada/China — a genuinely low-exposure position,
which may partly explain why full absorption was a credible promise to make.

**Why this matters for Phase 3:** Chipotle is currently the corpus's only clean
"full-absorption" case (`full-absorption-promise` code, flagged as a possible Theme 6).
Being from March 2025 rather than 2026 means it's not just a different messaging
strategy — it's temporally *earlier*, from the first wave of tariff
announcements/reactions, not the same news cycle as the rest of the corpus. Worth
deciding explicitly whether to (a) keep it and note the corpus spans two tariff waves,
which could itself be an interesting temporal dimension, or (b) treat it as a
different data-generating context and either drop it or handle it as a distinct
case — this is a Phase 3 call, not one to make here.

**Sources:** [LiveNOW/FOX](https://www.livenowfox.com/news/chipotle-ceo-trump-tariff-costs)
(reports the NBC interview + earnings-call figure); Q1 2026 earnings call transcript
checked directly and does **not** mention tariffs at all (inflation discussion covered
beef/freight/dairy/avocado/produce, no tariff framing) — so the 60bps figure is from an
earlier call, not Q1 2026. Exact originating call not yet pinned down; flagging rather
than guessing.

## #10 Lovesac — upgraded, richer and more quantified than the draft coding captured

**Original draft entry said:** "Tariff mitigation plan" combining price increases +
sourcing diversification + supplier concessions, called "richest mitigation-narrative
language" in the expansion pass.

**Primary source confirms and adds real numbers:** official investor-relations press
release (investor.lovesac.com, FY2026 Q4 results, ~March 2026) details a **four-pronged
tariff strategy** launched in April: (1) renegotiated supplier agreements, (2) shifted
production out of China (cutting China-sourced share to the mid-teens for the fiscal
year), (3) price increases, (4) vendor concessions. Also includes a "Made in America"
domestic-manufacturing initiative (Sactionals seat inserts) launching that summer.
**Quantified cost impact:** more than $22 million in additional tariff costs absorbed
into gross profit, which still landed at 56.4% gross margin; net income fell to $4.1M
from $11.6M the prior fiscal year — i.e., the mitigation narrative is backed by a real,
disclosed financial hit, not just messaging.

**Why this matters for Phase 3:** this is the strongest, most quantified
`mitigation-narrative` artifact in the whole corpus (original 7 + expansion 8) — worth
considering as the lead illustrative example for that theme in the write-up, given it
has both the strategy narrative AND the dollar figure showing real cost absorbed.

**Source:** [Lovesac IR press release](https://investor.lovesac.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/161/the-lovesac-company-reports-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2026)
(primary — official investor relations page); corroborated by
[Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/lovesac-enacts-four-step-tariff-response/802623/).
