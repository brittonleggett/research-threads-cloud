## Purchase Intention — Dodds, Monroe & Grewal (1991)

**Confirmed:** this is a real empirical paper (factorial experiment on
price/brand/store cues → perceived quality, perceived value, willingness
to buy; 5×3×3 design in the phase-two study, student sample) — unlike Xia
et al. (2004), this one genuinely has a scale, just not one Consensus could
extract verbatim from what it could access (no appendix/measure table in
the retrieved excerpts).

**Best available proxy, with attribution:** Fennell, Niedrich & Rice
(2025) state they adapted **four** purchase-intention items directly from
Dodds et al. (1991):
1. "The likelihood that I would purchase the [product] from [retailer] is..."
2. "The probability that I would purchase the [product] from [retailer] is..."
3. "My willingness to buy the [product] from [retailer] is..."
4. "I would definitely try the [product] at [retailer]'s current price."

**Compare to this project's existing 3-item reconstruction**
(`2026-08-04-scale-items-verification-status.md`): "The likelihood of
purchasing this product is high" / "If I were going to buy a product like
this, I would consider buying this one" / "The probability that I would
consider buying this product is high." Items 1 and 3 of the existing
reconstruction are a reasonable paraphrase-match to Fennell's items 1–2;
the existing item 2 doesn't match anything in Fennell's four, and Fennell
has a fourth item (the "definitely try" statement) the existing
reconstruction is missing entirely. **These are not the same scale as
currently drafted — worth reconciling before the instrument locks**, ideally
by pulling the actual 1991 JMR appendix rather than choosing between two
unconfirmed reconstructions.

**Status: still not verbatim-confirmed. Genuine research-tool ceiling, not
a lack of effort** — this is exactly the kind of appendix/measures-table
content that often doesn't make it into whatever full-text a search tool
can access. Needs your library pull, same conclusion as the original
2026-08-04 note reached, but now with a better-sourced fallback proxy
(Fennell et al. 2025) if you need to move without waiting on that.

## Word-of-Mouth Intention — Maxham & Netemeyer (2002)

**Confirmed:** this is a real 20-month longitudinal field study of
complaining bank customers, tracking satisfaction/WOM/repurchase intent
across two failure-and-recovery episodes. It does explicitly measure WOM
recommendation likelihood over time.

**Genuine dead end on exact wording.** Consensus could not recover the WOM
item stems from what it accessed — the only verbatim items it could pull
from this paper are for a *different* construct (service-recovery
satisfaction: "In my opinion, [firm] provided a satisfactory resolution to
my banking problem on this particular occasion," etc. — not usable as WOM
items). A related 2003 Maxham & Netemeyer paper confirms the *format* this
research stream typically used — three-item, 7-point Likert scales for
both purchase intent and positive WOM — which is consistent with this
project's existing reconstruction's structure, but does not confirm the
existing item text is correct.

**Status: no better than the 2026-08-04 note left it.** This is the one
scale where today's Consensus pass added no real ground — confirming the
format (3-item, 7-pt Likert) but not the wording. Needs your library pull;
no usable proxy found this pass the way Dodds/Fennell provided one.
