## Recent Real-World Developments

Corporate tariff-driven price increases are actively happening right now, not a settled 2025 story — this is live raw material for Study 1's corporate-messaging corpus:

- **Nike**: executives confirmed "surgical price increases" to offset tariff costs, warning tariffs could add ~$1B in costs for FY2026. [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/5-companies-predicting-major-price-110305478.html)
- **Mattel**: announced U.S. price increases on some products explicitly to offset tariff costs.
- **BMW**: price increases of $400–$1,500 on certain 2026 models effective Jan 1, 2026, explicitly citing tariffs on imported components.
- **Lennox (HVAC)**: announced up to 10% price increases on residential equipment effective Feb 16, 2026, with the CFO confirming the tariff/pricing linkage on the Q4 2025 earnings call — a clean example of an *earnings-call* tariff-messaging artifact, exactly the corpus type Study 1 wants.
- **Dormakaba, GMS**: mixed signals — Dormakaba raising list prices ~4% (March 2026) while GMS actually *cut* its tariff surcharge (18%→12%, Feb 2026) — a useful contrast case (not all firms are raising prices, some are absorbing/adjusting surcharges downward), good for typology variation.
- **Williams-Sonoma**: guidance explicitly flags that Section 122 tariffs expiring **July 2026** are assumed to be replaced with similar-rate tariffs — meaning corporate messaging is actively responding to policy uncertainty right now, in the same month as this scan.
- **KPMG's 2026 Tariff Survey**: 55% of executives plan further price increases in the next six months, and firms passing on more than half of tariff costs to consumers has risen from 13% to 34% year-over-year — useful aggregate-level framing/citation for the intro, showing this is a widespread, escalating phenomenon, not a handful of isolated cases.

## Relevance to IRB Filing Timing

This scan argues **for filing now, not waiting**:

1. **The phenomenon is escalating, not stabilizing.** KPMG's own survey shows the share of firms passing >50% of tariff costs to consumers more than doubled year-over-year (13%→34%), and 55% plan further increases in the next 6 months. Corporate tariff-messaging behavior is still actively evolving — the longer Study 1's corpus-collection window is delayed, the more of this evolving discourse gets missed or has to be reconstructed retroactively.
2. **A concrete near-term deadline exists inside the data itself**: Section 122 tariffs are set to expire in July 2026 (this month) unless Congress extends them — meaning corporate messaging is likely to shift again in the next few weeks regardless of what Congress does. That's a natural, time-bounded corpus window (before/after the expiration) that only exists if data collection starts soon.
3. **Concrete, ready-to-use corpus examples already exist** (Nike, Mattel, BMW, Lennox earnings call, Dormakaba/GMS contrast pair) — Study 1 doesn't need to wait for "enough" messaging to accumulate; there's already a workable initial set to start coding while the IRB is in review.

Since Study 1 (corporate document analysis) is IRB-exempt (45 CFR 46.102(e), public documents, no human-subjects contact) per [[project_tariff_paper]] notes, it could start now independent of the IRB timeline for Studies 2/3 — filing IRB paperwork for the Prolific studies doesn't block starting Study 1 immediately.
