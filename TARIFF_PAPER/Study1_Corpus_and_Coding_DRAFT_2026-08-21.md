**Artifacts 8–15 (revised codes reflect verification-pass findings, superseding the original
one-line draft entries in `2026-08-04-study1-corpus-expansion.md`):**

8. **IKEA** — Ingka Group's Tolga Öncü on record: passed on "part of the cost increase" because
   the company "can't stay immune to absorb all the costs" itself. Confirmed prices: Uppland
   sofa $849→$899, oak bedroom set $959→$1,049.
   → `causation-explicit`, `numeric-transparency`, `partial-pass-through-explicit` (sharpened
   from the original `causation-explicit`/`numeric-transparency`-only coding — this is now a
   clean partial-pass-through case, useful for Cost-Response vignette cross-reference).

9. **La-Z-Boy** — 2025 increases real but modest ("very low end of what we're hearing"); company
   also said it did **not** expect to raise prices again if the planned Jan 2026 furniture-tariff
   hike (25%→30%) took effect, citing near-total domestic manufacturing — then that hike itself
   was delayed a year by the White House.
   → `causation-explicit` (2025 increases only), `no-further-increase-anticipated` (new candidate
   code), domestic-manufacturing-insulation narrative. **`industry-norm-appeal` from the original
   draft entry does not hold up** on closer reading — drop or flag for Phase 3 as a miscode.
   **Open question for Phase 3, not resolved here:** does this artifact still function as a clean
   pass-through exemplar, or is it better used as a counter-example (limited exposure due to
   domestic sourcing)?

10. **Lovesac** — Official IR release: four-pronged strategy (renegotiated supplier terms,
    China-sourcing reduction, price increases, vendor concessions) plus a "Made in America"
    initiative. Quantified: $22M+ in tariff costs absorbed into gross profit (still 56.4% gross
    margin); net income fell from $11.6M to $4.1M.
    → `causation-explicit`, `mitigation-narrative`, `restraint-language`, `quantified-cost-absorption`
    (new candidate code — the richest quantified mitigation-narrative artifact in the corpus,
    candidate lead illustrative example for that theme).

11. **Chipotle** — Quote confirmed accurate ("It is our intent as we sit here today to absorb
    those costs") but is from an **NBC interview dated March 2, 2025** — a full year before the
    rest of the corpus (2026). Q1 2026 earnings call, checked directly, does not mention tariffs
    at all.
    → `causation-explicit`, `full-absorption-promise` (candidate Theme 6), `restraint-language`,
    `hedged-commitment`. **Open scope question for Phase 3, not resolved here:** the corpus's
    only clean full-absorption case is also its only artifact from the *first* tariff wave
    (pre-dates the Feb 2026 SCOTUS/IEEPA ruling entirely — see `notes/2026-08-14-scotus-ieepa-
    legal-sequence-confirmed.md`). Whether to keep it and treat the corpus's two-wave span as a
    finding in itself, or handle Chipotle as a distinct case, is unresolved.

12. **Birkenstock** — CFO Ivica Krolo: ~100bp gross-margin/EBITDA hit expected; prices adjusted
    **style by style each season**, not uniformly; explicit quantified partial-absorption logic
    ("the price increase would have to be 2.5x the tariffs" to hold margin — not done "to our
    customers, being a democratic brand").
    → `causation-explicit` (sharpened from original `causation-vague`), `mitigation-narrative`,
    `selective/style-by-style-framing`, `partial-absorption-quantified` (new candidate code).

13. **Walmart** — Official IR-hosted transcripts. CFO John David Rainey: "tariff-related costs
    lifted prices across many categories." CEO Doug McMillon: inventory replenished "at
    post-tariff price levels." General-merchandise inflation >3%, up from 1.7% prior quarter.
    → `causation-explicit`, `category-broad`, `official-transcript-quotable` (upgraded confidence
    tag — largest-scale, most citable artifact in the corpus for a lead illustrative quote).

14. **Insteel Industries** — CEO Woltz, Q3 2026: "when a [freight] cost $1,500... now $3,000
    somebody's gotta pay the bill." Explicitly tied to **Section 232 steel tariffs** specifically
    (not generic "tariffs"). Price increase effective July 13, 2026.
    → `causation-explicit`, `vivid-quotable-framing`, `section-232-specific` (new metadata tag —
    the corpus's one artifact naming a specific statutory basis other than the IEEPA→122→301
    sequence; see the legal-sequence note for why that distinction matters).

15. **Home Depot** — Three-beat sequence, not a single reversal: (1) May 2025, publicly said it
    would **not** raise prices broadly (>50% U.S.-sourced, no single country >10% of purchases);
    (2) Aug 2025, EVP Merchandising Billy Bastek (not CFO Richard McPhail — see corrected 2026-09-01)
    reversed course, on Home Depot's own Q2 FY2025 earnings call — verbatim: "there'll be some
    modest price movement in some categories, but it won't be broad based"; (3) May 2026, further
    update citing fuel/commodity pressure plus new tariffs, "the environment is changing almost
    every day."
    → `causation-explicit` (from Aug 2025 on), `reversal-narrative` (new candidate code — a
    stated position changing under continued cost pressure), `hedged-commitment`. **This artifact
    was originally flagged as the weakest lead in the corpus (placeholder-only entry); the
    verification pass found it to be arguably the richest narrative-arc artifact instead** —
    recommend against dropping it, though the final call on including/promoting it is Phase 3's.
