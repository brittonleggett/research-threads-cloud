# Writing a revise-and-resubmit / response-to-reviewers letter (2026-08-11)

Pulled because Britton has a first-authored paper in second round at JMTP right
now — see [[project_tariff_paper_pipeline]]. This is the template/checklist to
use when he's ready to draft that response letter (or the next one).

Sources:
- Carnegie Mellon University Student Academic Success Center, "Writing the
  Revise and Resubmit Letter" (official handout PDF, with sentence templates)
- "How to Respond to Peer Review Comments Like a Pro!" — real transcript pulled
  via yt-dlp, presenter identifies as a handling/journal editor
  (youtube.com/watch?v=MqNtLc4Ehi0)

## The 5-move structure (CMU handout — this is a recognized genre with real conventions)

1. **Express gratitude** — open by thanking the reviewers for their time/effort.
2. **Signal attention to comments** — explicitly state you've addressed every
   comment, early in the letter.
3. **Claim positive results** — say plainly that the revisions improved the
   paper. This isn't flattery, it's a genre convention editors expect.
4. **Preview content** — give the editor a roadmap before the point-by-point
   response (e.g. "Reviewer A's comments are below in italics, followed by our
   response to each").
5. **Respond to specific comments** — the bulk of the letter. For each comment:
   restate it verbatim → evaluate it → describe the specific revision made →
   justify it (with literature citations where relevant). Include page/paragraph/
   line numbers for every change so the editor can verify quickly.

Template sentence stems (from the handout, useful as starting language):
- Gratitude: "We thank Reviewer A for the constructive comments on our manuscript."
- Attention: "We have made every attempt to fully address these comments in the
  revised manuscript."
- Positive framing: "We believe these revisions have resulted in a significantly
  improved manuscript."
- Disagreement (still polite): "This is a good point; however, [reasoned
  explanation with citation]..."

## Editor's-eye tips (the things a template won't tell you)

- **"It's okay to disagree, but not okay to disrespect."** Reviewers aren't
  always right and sometimes ask for things that are genuinely impossible
  (different dataset, different sample). Push back — but only ever with a
  reasoned, evidence-backed counter-argument, never a bare assertion.
- **Make it trivially easy to verify.** Reference exact manuscript line numbers,
  and consider pasting the actual revised text into the response letter itself
  so the editor/reviewer doesn't have to flip between two documents.
- Editors are optimizing for turnaround time (it's a metric journals compete
  and advertise on) — a clean, well-organized, easy-to-audit response letter is
  itself a point in the paper's favor, independent of the revisions' content.
- Most manuscripts go through at least one revision round; only rejecting a
  comment outright (vs. explaining why) is what damages an author's case, not
  disagreement itself.

## How to apply

When Britton is ready to draft the JMTP response letter (or any future R&R):
1. Pull his actual reviewer comments and pair each with the specific revision
   made in the manuscript.
2. Use the 5-move structure as the letter's skeleton.
3. For any comment he wants to push back on, help build the reasoned/cited
   counter-argument rather than just a restatement of disagreement — that's
   the part reviewers/editors actually scrutinize.
4. Cross-reference exact page/line numbers from the revised manuscript.
