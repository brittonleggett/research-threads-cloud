## Idea 1a — Ratepayer cost-shifting fairness (Louisiana data centers)

**Real finding: Journal of Consumer Affairs (JCA) is a better first choice than the
originally-guessed JPP&M.** JCA's own stated remit — "government policies for consumers'
well-being," explicitly welcomes public-policy/public-administration contributions, origin
in the consumer protection movement (American Council on Consumer Interests) — is a tighter
match for a "who bears the cost, is it fair to ratepayers" question than JPP&M's more
marketing-strategy-adjacent scope (FTC regulation, advertising, deregulation broadly). No
on-topic JCA precedent article surfaced in this pass, but the fit is scope-level strong, not
just plausible. JPP&M is not ruled out (pricing/regulation confirmed in-scope there too) —
treat as a strong second choice, not wrong.

**Real academic literature exists on the underlying construct** (distributive energy justice,
fairness in electricity cost allocation) in *Energy Research & Social Science* and *Energy
Policy* — mostly quantitative/simulation-based (fairness-indicator modeling) or policy-legal
(Harvard Electricity Law Initiative), not survey-based consumer-perception research. That's a
real opening: a consumer-perception survey on ratepayer-cost fairness looks like it would be
genuinely novel method-wise even though the topic itself is well-covered from a policy-design
angle. **Method fit:** the proposed Study1(TA)→Study2(PLS-SEM) template is not what JCA or
ERSS typically publish (JCA leans toward survey/econometric consumer research, not qualitative
TA-to-experiment; ERSS is often policy-analytic) — flag this as a real methods-fit risk to
resolve before designing Study 1, more than a topic-fit risk.

**Overlap/cannibalization risk with Data Center Paper: real and worth resolving explicitly.**
Both use the same real-world material (Meta Richland Parish deal, LPSC dockets, the same
Louisiana data-center fights) and both could plausibly land at JPP&M. The distinction — Data
Center Paper is about opposition to facilities *existing*; this idea is about *who pays* for
the ones that get built — is real but subtle, and a shared reviewer pool would likely ask why
these aren't one paper with two DVs rather than two papers. **Recommendation: don't build this
as a fully separate thread by default** — closer to a natural Study 3 extension or companion
paper *explicitly positioned against* Data Center Paper (same corpus era, different dependent
variable), not an independent pipeline item, unless Britton wants the pattern-repetition
argument he already made for Data Center vs. CCS to extend here too (he was explicit that
method-pattern repetition across his own papers doesn't bother him — this could still apply,
but the *material* overlap here is tighter than the Data-Center-vs-CCS case, which used
different states/documents).

**Ranked recommendation:** JCA first, JPP&M second (or as a two-DV companion inside Data
Center Paper rather than standalone). Real venue, real gap, but resolve the Data Center
overlap question before doing any real design work.

## Idea 2a — Tariff-driven brand/private-label switching

**Critical finding, not previously flagged: the proposed venue (Journal of Consumer
Marketing) is the exact same special issue TARIFF_PAPER is already targeting.** Confirmed via
direct search of the JCM special-issue call: "Crafting Shape in a Fluid World," guest editors
Kevin W. James, Janna M. Parker, Hyunju Shin, submissions open **August 15, 2026**, close
**October 15, 2026** — the identical special issue, identical editors, identical deadline
already driving Tariff Paper's urgency (see [[project_tariff_paper_pipeline]]). This is a real
problem, not a minor overlap:
1. **Feasibility:** the submission window is already open and closes in two months. This idea
   has zero corpus, zero instrument, zero data — an experimental vignette study (not even a
   TA-based one, which could partially reuse Tariff's existing infrastructure) would need to be
   fully designed, fielded, and analyzed from scratch in that window. Not realistic as a
   same-cycle submission regardless of venue merit.
2. **Same-author, same-issue risk:** submitting two papers from the same author to the same
   guest-edited special issue, both about tariff-driven consumer response, is a real editorial
   and perception risk even if the designs are genuinely distinct (Tariff Paper: causation-
   attribution framing → trust/fairness chain, TA-based; this idea: causation-attribution
   framing → brand-switching *behavior*, experimental-vignette-based). A shared guest-editor
   pool is likely to read these as overlapping rather than complementary.

**Real market/survey evidence for the underlying phenomenon is genuinely strong** (Statista:
60% of consumers say they'd stop buying a favorite brand over tariff price hikes, ~48-53%
would switch to private label/generic; Inmar's 2025 shopper survey; Circana/Simon-Kucher
industry reporting on private-label "stickiness") — this supports the idea's real-world hook,
just not this venue/timeline. No formal academic (peer-reviewed) experimental study on
tariff-attribution-framing-driving-switching surfaced in this pass — the gap itself looks
real and open.

**Recommendation: keep the idea, drop the venue/timeline as currently proposed.** Either (a)
hold this for the *next* JCM-adjacent cycle or a different journal (Journal of Retailing is a
plausible standalone alternate — real private-label/brand-switching literature exists there,
though nothing on tariff-attribution specifically), explicitly *after* Tariff Paper's own
submission is out the door, or (b) reconsider whether this is better absorbed as a Study 3
extension of Tariff Paper itself (same author, same theoretical territory, avoids the
same-issue collision entirely, and Tariff Paper's own Study 2/3 infrastructure — see
`TARIFF_PAPER/notes/2026-08-04-full-instrument-assembly.md` — could plausibly be extended
rather than rebuilt). Flag this one to Britton directly — it's the idea most likely to cause a
real problem if pursued as currently scoped.

## Idea 3a — LNG-terminal community opposition (Cameron Parish)

**Real, directly on-topic precedent found: "Critical energy justice in US natural gas
infrastructuring"** (*Energy Research & Social Science*, ScienceDirect) — natural-gas
infrastructure siting analyzed through a justice lens, published in exactly the outlet
already flagged as a Data Center Paper candidate. This is a stronger, more specific match
than the originally-guessed JPP&M/generic-energy-policy-outlet framing. Broader adjacent
literature is real and active: procedural-justice-in-energy-siting scoring across states,
justice-framed opposition to gas pipelines/LNG terminals (recognition and procedural
"frictions" is the field's own vocabulary) — this is a live, receptive sub-literature, not a
reach.

**On the standalone-vs-comparative-extension question (the real structural decision here):**
the venue check makes this clearer, not murkier. Idea 3a and Data Center Paper would
plausibly target the *same* venues (ERSS, JPP&M, JBE, B&S) using the *same* theoretical
toolkit (procedural/distributive justice, secrecy/exclusion). Built as two fully separate
papers, that's a harder sell than Data Center Paper's own LA-vs-national generalizability
question was — this isn't cross-state generalization of one infrastructure type, it's a
second, different infrastructure type running the same theoretical playbook. **Recommendation:
frame this as a comparative extension, not a fifth standalone paper** — "does the same
opposition grammar (secrecy/procedural exclusion, distributive injustice, ambivalent
economic-necessity framing) generalize across fundamentally different infrastructure types
(AI/data infrastructure vs. fossil-fuel export infrastructure), or does it diverge" is a
genuinely stronger, more novel single contribution than two similar papers competing for the
same reviewer pool — and it directly parallels the region-as-moderator structure Data Center
Paper is already being pushed toward after today's national-scope pivot. This would make
infrastructure-type itself a second moderator/comparison dimension alongside region.

**Ranked recommendation:** don't spin up as an independent 5th thread. Revisit once Data
Center Paper's own national-scope restructuring is settled (Britton's open call, per
[[project_data_center_paper]]) — the LNG material is strong and ready whenever he wants to
extend that paper's comparative scope, not on its own separate clock.

## Idea 4a — AI-assisted-research-workflows methods paper (quick sanity check only, per
Britton's instruction not to over-invest here)

**IJQM confirmed as the right venue, with a bonus finding: a near-direct interlocutor
already exists.** Friese, Nguyen-Trung, Powell, & Morgan, "Beyond Binary Positions: Making
Space for Critical and Reflexive GenAI Integration in Qualitative Research" (*IJQM*, 2026) —
explicitly argues *against* treating GenAI as an autonomous agent, insisting on close
researcher control to preserve reflexive rigor. This is close enough to be a direct
counterpoint this repo's own case study could respond to (an unattended, scheduled agent
*with structurally bounded* interpretive authority — Phase 3 coded off-limits in the repo's
own README — as a middle position between "full researcher control" and "autonomous
analysis machine"). Also confirmed IJQM is actively publishing this exact genre right now
(Xu 2026 on GenAI thematic-analysis reflexivity; Costa et al. 2025 on AI-as-co-researcher) —
a hot, receptive venue, not a stale one. Organizational Research Methods / AI & Society
remain plausible alternates if the framing shifts toward governance/infrastructure rather
than qualitative-methods-specifically, unchanged from the original guess.
