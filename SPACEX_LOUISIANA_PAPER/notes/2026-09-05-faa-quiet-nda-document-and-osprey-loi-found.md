### 2b. FWS Biological and Conference Opinion (2022) — the federal review Boca Chica actually got

Looked for a primary federal environmental-review document to compare against the Louisiana
FAA-waiver fight (the core of this paper's regulatory-venue-shifting frame candidate — what
happens when this review *isn't* waived). Found and fetched the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's
final Biological and Conference Opinion directly:
https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/5-12-2022%20SpaceX%20Final%20BCO_signed%20with%20appendix%20A-D.pdf
(HTTP 200, 5.9MB, direct `curl`).

**Confirmed directly from the document:**
- Consultation No. 02ETCC00-2012-F-0186-R001, dated **May 12, 2022**, addressed to the FAA's
  Office of Commercial Space Transportation.
- Subject: effects of FAA's proposed experimental permit/vehicle operator license for the
  Starship/Super Heavy program at Boca Chica on the northern aplomado falcon, Gulf Coast
  jaguarundi, ocelot, Kemp's ridley/hawksbill/leatherback/loggerhead/green sea turtles, piping
  plover (and its critical habitat), and red knot (and its proposed critical habitat).
- **Conclusion, quoted directly**: "the action as proposed, is not likely to jeopardize the
  continued existence of the species nor adversely modify piping plover critical habitat or modify
  red knot proposed critical habitat."
- Basis given: the action area is a small fraction of each species' rangewide habitat/population;
  a 2013 BCO had already cleared the site's original Falcon 9/Heavy operations for the ocelot/
  jaguarundi; and a package of protective measures (noise/lighting mitigation, monitoring,
  debris-removal, recovery-plan partnership) accompanies the permit.
- An Incidental Take Statement follows, authorizing specific, quantified take of individual
  animals (e.g., a fixed number of ocelot/jaguarundi harassment incidents) rather than prohibiting
  all impact outright.

**Not fetched tonight, flagged as a lead**: search results mention a 2023 addendum and a 2025
"Final Amended BCO Addendum #2" to this same consultation — i.e., the review was revisited more
than once as launch cadence increased. Following that chain (did later addenda relax or tighten
the "not likely to jeopardize" finding as SpaceX scaled up?) would directly inform whether Boca
Chica's environmental review process held up over time or eroded — genuinely useful for the
paper's Louisiana-waiver comparison, but not pursued tonight for time.

### 2c. A second, distinct TCEQ docket — SpaceX's own filing attacking the coalition's standing

The 09-04 note pulled the **$3,750 penalty agreed order** (Docket 2024-1282-IWD-E). Tonight,
searching for further coverage of the "penalty inadequate" public comment (this note's specific
ask #2), turned up a **different, still-open TCEQ matter**: Docket No. 2024-1821-IWD, SpaceX's new
TPDES permit application (No. WQ0005462000) to authorize ongoing deluge-water/washdown-water/
stormwater discharge — the very permit the $3,750 penalty was a stopgap for pending. Fetched
SpaceX's own filing directly:
https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/agency/decisions/agendas/backup/2024/2024-1821-iwd-appr.pdf
(HTTP 200, direct `curl`, 1724 lines extracted).

**Confirmed directly from the document** — "Space Exploration Technology Corporation's Response
to Requests for Contested Case Hearing and Requests for Reconsideration," filed **January 17,
2025**:
- STEJN, the Carrizo/Comecrudo Nation of Texas, Save RGV, and 12 individuals filed hearing
  requests on SpaceX's application; two associations and two individuals also filed requests for
  reconsideration.
- **SpaceX's own argument, quoted directly**: the associations "fail to identify any individual
  member who would have standing," "fail to provide addresses for the members they identify,"
  and their "members' recreational interests at or beyond the mouth of the Rio Grande River are
  shared with the general public" — therefore none of them are "affected associations" or
  "affected persons," and the Commissioners "should deny the requests."
- SpaceX opens its own filing with a company self-narrative ("Since its founding in 2002, SpaceX
  has substantially reduced the cost of access to space... now handles about two-thirds of NASA's
  launches...") before getting to the standing argument — directly quotable corporate framing
  language, in SpaceX's own voice, responding to the exact coalition tracked across three prior
  nights.
- A follow-up WebSearch found the TCEQ Executive Director then **recommended denying all hearing
  requests**, with the Commissioners' meeting on this scheduled for Feb. 13, 2025 — but no
  document found tonight confirms the Commissioners' actual final vote/outcome. **Status: SpaceX's
  position and the ED's recommendation are primary-confirmed; the Commission's final ruling is
  not** — flag before citing an outcome.

This is a genuinely new, distinct primary document (not a re-read of the 09-04 items), and it's
the first time this project has SpaceX's *own* voice on the record specifically contesting this
opposition coalition's legitimacy to participate at all — likely useful regardless of which theory
chain Britton eventually picks, since "does the company frame its opponents as illegitimate
stakeholders" is a recognizable move in both the venue-shifting and greenwashing literatures.

## 4. General news sweep — the single most important find of the night, and it predates tonight

Swept for anything new since 09-04. Nothing new was posted dated after 09-04 about the core
project (permitting, construction, parish council action, litigation filed against the project

**Louisiana Illuminator, "SpaceX sweeteners: How did Louisiana lure Elon Musk's rocket launch site
to its coast?"** (Wes Muller, published 2026-09-01T16:09:18Z, fetched directly via `r.jina.ai`
proxy) reports that LED's incentive package for SpaceX, per documents obtained through a public
records request, totals an estimated **$27.5 billion** — and links directly to the underlying LED
document. That document was fetched directly tonight:
**https://lailluminator.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/LOI-Project-Osprey.pdf** (HTTP 200, 1.78MB,
`curl` + `pdftotext`, no proxy needed — hosted on Illuminator's own WordPress uploads).

**This is LED's actual "Letter of Intent for Project Osprey," dated July 10, 2026** (i.e., written
six weeks before the public Aug. 25 announcement, while the deal was still under NDA — "Project
Osprey" was the negotiation codename, addressed to "Ben Lancaster, Senior Manager, Governmental
Affairs" without naming SpaceX). **Read directly from the document:**
- Section (a): total capital investment "of at least $100.0 billion by December 31, 2044."
- Section (b): total charitable contribution to the Community Foundation of Acadiana "of at least
  $25.0 million by December 31, 2030" — matches the figure already in the corpus from LED's public
  page.
- A year-by-year jobs/payroll schedule from 2026 (18 jobs) through 2035 (3,000 jobs, $320.4M
  payroll) — the *contractual* trajectory behind the "3,000 direct jobs" figure already in the
  corpus, now shown with its full ramp-up schedule rather than just the endpoint.
- **Incentive program table, read directly, with LED's own dollar estimates**:
  1. PILOT (25-yr property-tax abatement, $25M/yr + 2%/yr escalator + $20M one-time community
     payment) — **LED's own estimated value: up to $23.852 billion.**
  2. Aerospace Facilities and Activities Rebate (state/local sales & use tax rebate on equipment/
     materials, 20-yr term, extendable 10 more years) — **$3.663 billion.**
  3. "Project Osprey VIP Permitting Initiative" (a funding mechanism to accelerate state-agency
     permitting/regulatory review across DOTD, LDEQ, Wildlife and Fisheries, CPRA, etc.) — value
     "TBD."
  4. High Impact Jobs Program cash grants — up to $25.5 million.
  5. LED FastStart recruitment/training support — up to $2.8 million.
  - **Total Potential Value, stated by LED itself: $27.544 billion.**
- **No line item for a "$100M coastal master plan" appears anywhere in this document.** This
  directly corroborates — with a stronger primary source than was available on 08-29 — that note's
  finding that the $100M coastal figure is a separate, verbal, press-conference-only commitment,
  not part of LED's written incentive package. Not re-litigating that resolution here, just noting
  the new document is fully consistent with it.

**Why this matters more than anything else found tonight**: this is a real, LED-authored,
publicly-released-via-records-request document that puts a number ($27.544B) on the incentive
package that is roughly **35x larger** than the "$25M/year + $20M upfront" figures emphasized in
LED's own public-facing press release and most news coverage, and it exists as a written
commitment from six weeks before the public announcement — i.e., negotiated and finalized entirely
under NDA. For this paper's economic-benefit-claim-specificity frame, this is about as clean a
primary-sourced "claimed/emphasized figure vs. actual full commitment" gap as this project has
found on either side of the Texas/Louisiana comparison. Flagging it prominently rather than
burying it under the news-sweep heading it happened to surface from.

Also from the same WWNO article (Drew Hawkins, Gulf States Newsroom, published 2026-09-02T18:35
