## 1. Attribution-specificity framing (inherited from TARIFF_PAPER) — solidly grounded

Extracted `TARIFF_PAPER/tariff-messaging-consumer-behavior-lit-review_perplexity.docx` (zipfile/XML
method, Read tool can't open .docx directly). Confirmed real, substantial theoretical grounding:
dual entitlement theory (Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1986) — external attribution of cost increases
("we're raising prices because of tariffs") generates higher fairness perceptions than internal
attribution; corporate crisis communication/attribution theory; Yıldırım, Koç & Güzel (2024) on
attribution and equity theory in price-gouging perceptions (analyzed 4,001 real consumer complaint
records); Freudenreich & Penz (2024, JCM) on reactance theory. **This pillar transfers to SpaceX
cleanly** — Musk's round "10,000 exciting jobs" vs. LED's specific, sourced 3,000+3,100 figures is
structurally the same explicit-vs-vague attribution-specificity contrast Tariff's own theory
predicts should affect perceived fairness/trust.

## 2. Regulatory-venue-shifting (inherited from DATA_CENTER_PAPER) — real construct, but its own
source paper had NO citation anchor until tonight

Checked `DATA_CENTER_PAPER/Study1_Corpus_and_Coding_DRAFT_2026-08-17_national-restructure.md`
directly. The construct is real and well-evidenced empirically (AZ's Project Blue, LA's Amazon/
Caddo case, LA's Meta/PSC case — three independent states, same mechanism), but the note itself
flags: "ties directly to regulatory-capture theory **if you want that citation anchor**" — an
open TODO, never closed. **SpaceX was about to inherit an ungrounded construct from its own
sibling paper.**

**Found and verified tonight**: **Coen, D., Guidi, M., Héritier, A. & Yordanova, N. (2020/2021),
"The Logic of Regulatory Venue Shopping: A Firm's Perspective," *Public Policy and
Administration*, 36(3), 323-342.** Full text reached (open access via UCL's institutional
repository). Real theoretical fit: firms strategically select among regulatory venues when they
can identify more favorable treatment elsewhere, especially in infrastructure/utility sectors
characterized by monopolistic access and continuous regulatory dependence — a close match for both
Data Center's and SpaceX's pattern (a firm blocked by one body routing around it via a friendlier
one — Tucson Council → Corporation Commission; FAA environmental review → an executive-order-driven
waiver push). **Recommend this become the citation anchor for regulatory-venue-shifting across
both Data Center and SpaceX** — flagging for Data Center's own literature-building pass too, since
this fixes a real gap in that paper's own foundation, not just SpaceX's borrowed version of it.

## 3. Real-time relevance: the FAA waiver fight just got sharper

Found live, directly on-point context not yet in the project's notes: the FAA has formally proposed
waiving requirements under **13 federal environmental/natural-resource laws** for commercial space
licenses (NEPA, Endangered Species Act, Clean Water Act, Clean Air Act, National Historic
Preservation Act, Coastal Zone Management Act, and others) — implementing **Executive Order 14335,
"Enabling Competition in the Commercial Space Industry."** The FAA's legal basis leans on the
Supreme Court's 2025 ruling in *Seven County Infrastructure Coalition v. Eagle County*, which gave
agencies broad deference on environmental-impact-statement scope. This is a much more sweeping,
more citable regulatory-venue-shifting/deregulation story than the Vermilion Parish-specific fight
alone — worth folding into the project's real-world grounding notes as the national policy
backdrop the Louisiana fight sits inside. Sources: [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/09/faa-and-fcc-actions-signal-narrower-reviews-for-commercial-space-activities),
[Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/cleared-for-launch-faa-proposes-sweeping-environmental-waivers).

## 4. Deepened novelty check — confirms and strengthens the existing claim

The 2026-08-27 novelty check found "no marketing or PLS-SEM treatment of the framing-claim-vs-
outcome gap specifically" for spaceport development. Tonight's deeper pass found real, closely
adjacent academic work — but none of it closes the gap, which if anything makes the novelty claim
stronger, not weaker:

- **Olson, Cinar, Li, Jia-Richards & Johnson (2024), "A Review of Documented Community Impacts Due
  to Spaceport Development," AIAA ASCEND Conference Proceedings, AIAA 2024-4928.** Confirmed real
  (DOI 10.2514/6.2024-4928). A synthesis of existing spaceport-community-impact literature, from
  an **aerospace engineering** venue (AIAA), not marketing/social science. Its own framing
  (per search-level detail) explicitly notes limited existing literature on spaceport social
  impact, with what exists coming mostly from anthropology/sociology/investigative journalism —
  independently corroborating the project's own novelty claim from a different angle.
- **A 2026 *Tourism Geographies* paper, "Resident perceptions of spaceport destination
  development"** (DOI 10.1080/09669582.2026.2678478, authors not yet recovered — WebFetch blocked
  on Taylor & Francis). The closest existing precedent found: examines resident perceptions of
  spaceport development through a sustainability/ESG lens. **Still not marketing/consumer-behavior/
  PLS-SEM framed** — it's tourism-geography, addressing destination-development perceptions rather
  than the specific claim-attribution/trust mechanism this paper's design would test. Worth citing
  as the closest adjacent precedent and explicitly differentiating against, the same move Flock had
  to make against Nhan & Helfers and CCS against Keane (2018) — not a reason to abandon the novelty
  claim, but it does need direct engagement in the Introduction, not silence.
- **Flyvbjerg, B. (2009), "Survival of the Unfittest: Why the Worst Infrastructure Gets Built, and
  What We Can Do about It," *Oxford Review of Economic Policy*, 25(3), 344-367** — a well-
  established, highly-cited academic tradition on megaproject cost-overrun/benefit-shortfall
  patterns (the "iron law of megaprojects": ~90% of megaprojects run over budget/schedule).
  Directly relevant background for the economic-benefit-claim-inflation pattern this paper
  documents (Musk's escalating Boca Chica figures, $800M→$13B). Also relevant: Flyvbjerg's "Design
  by Deception: The Politics of Megaproject Approval" (2005, *Harvard Design Magazine*), whose
  title alone describes almost exactly the claim-inflation dynamic this paper's Boca Chica
  comparison documents. Worth citing as the broader megaproject-literature anchor around the
  attribution-specificity mechanism, distinct from and complementary to the Tariff-inherited
  consumer-marketing framing.
