## 1. Kudłak (2025) — now fully verified via Crossref, tier upgraded to bibliographically confirmed

The 09-09 note flagged this citation as single-AI-search-sourced and unverified; 09-10 upgraded it
to "independently cross-verified" via three secondary bibliographic listings (Springer DOI page,
ResearchGate, German National Library catalog). Tonight it was queried directly against
**Crossref's own API** (`api.crossref.org/works`), which is a primary bibliographic registry, not
a secondary listing:

**Kudłak, R. (2025). Greenwashing or Striving to Persist: An Alternative Explanation of a Loose
Coupling Between Corporate Environmental Commitments and Outcomes. *Journal of Business Ethics*.**
DOI: 10.1007/s10551-024-05778-w. Crossref confirms: single author (Robert Kudłak), journal =
*Journal of Business Ethics*, published-print March 2025 (the DOI's own creation timestamp is
2024, i.e. published online ahead of print — consistent with the "2024-05778" DOI segment).
Crossref's own abstract field (a direct primary-source read, not a paraphrase) confirms this is a
**conceptual paper**, not an empirical study: it uses organizational-theory "loose coupling" (firms
as loosely-integrated subassemblies, not fully rational unified bureaucracies) to offer an
alternative to the assumption that a commitment-outcome gap is always deliberate deception —
"greenwashing might result from loose coupling caused by causal indeterminacy as well as a
fragmented external and internal environment." **Citation is now fully resolved as real and
correctly attributed — safe to cite the bibliographic details directly; the content
characterization above is now Crossref-abstract-sourced (a genuine primary bibliographic field),
one step better than 09-10's secondary-listing tier but still short of a full read of the article
body itself (still paywalled).**

## 2. Two new 2024-2025 papers extending the claim-specificity anchor (Janssen, Swaen & Du 2022)

Both found via WebSearch, both independently confirmed real via a direct Crossref API query
(title, authors, DOI, journal, and publication date all pulled from Crossref's own record, not
from the search snippet):

**Choubey, A., et al. (2025). Signals vs. Reality: Consumer Responses to Green Claims in Quick
Commerce. *Business Strategy and the Environment*, 34(3), 3656–3671.** DOI: 10.1002/bse.4165.
Crossref-confirmed: Wiley, published-print March 2025, open access (CC BY 4.0), is-referenced-by
26 citations already (per Crossref's own count as of tonight). Two-phase longitudinal survey (512
respondents Phase 1, 478 Phase 2) building a model from signalling theory, the elaboration
likelihood model, agenda-setting theory, and cognitive load theory. Core finding (from Crossref's
own abstract field): signals of "greenness" positively influence perceived credibility, visibility,
and complexity of a claim, which in turn positively shape consumer response — but a company's
actual environmental actions only moderated that relationship in the study's first phase, not the
second; by Phase 2, "consumers were aware and were not affected by some actions claiming companies'
green practices," i.e. claim-exposure effects can decay/get discounted over repeated exposure even
when the underlying practice claims stay constant. **Why this matters here:** a genuinely new,
verified 2025 empirical anchor directly on claim-signal-vs-reality dynamics, with a longitudinal
design (rare in this literature) — relevant to any framing where SpaceX's own claims get
re-evaluated by the same audience over time as Vermilion Parish construction actually proceeds.

**Wang, J., Zhou, Y., Zhang, J., & Wang, X. (2024). Pseudo-environmentalist or true defender? How
claim specificity and message framing influence consumers' brand evaluation in green demarketing
ads. *Current Psychology*.** DOI: 10.1007/s12144-024-07117-8. Crossref-confirmed: Springer, four
named authors, published online 2024-12-14 (technically outside the task's 2025-2026 window by
two weeks — flagging that precisely rather than rounding it into "2025" for convenience). Full text
not read tonight (abstract/content characterization not independently pulled — flagging this as a
citation-only lead, weaker tier than the Choubey item above, which had a Crossref abstract to
verify content against). Title alone indicates direct relevance (claim specificity x message
framing, interacting to shape perceived authenticity of environmental positioning) — a lead for a
future session to read in full before citing its substantive claims.
