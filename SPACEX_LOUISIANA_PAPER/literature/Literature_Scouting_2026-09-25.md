## 1. Bartik (2020) and the Upjohn Institute's economic-development-incentive-credibility
## literature — a new, directly relevant anchor for the "economic-benefit claim specificity" thread

**Bartik, T. J. (2020). Introduction to Special Issue: Learning More About Incentives. *Economic
Development Quarterly*, 34(2), 95–100.** — **BIBLIOGRAPHIC TIER**, verified directly against
Crossref's own metadata API (`api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0891242420916033`; title, single
author, journal, volume/issue/pages, and 2020-05 publication date all independently confirmed from
Crossref's record, not from an AI-search summary or the SAGE page itself, which 403'd/CAPTCHA'd on
every fetch attempt tonight). Content characterization below is therefore still search-snippet
tier (WebSearch summary + the article's own abstract-level framing as a special-issue intro), not a
full-text read — flag that distinction before citing its arguments in detail, though the citation
itself (title/author/venue/year/pages) is now solidly verified.

Per WebSearch-summarized content (Bartik's own broader Upjohn Institute body of work, cross-checked
across multiple independent secondary sources tonight — Center for American Progress, the Upjohn
Institute's own technical-report listings): economic-development incentive programs' job-creation
claims are frequently **not independently verified against actual net new local employment**, and
survey-based "but for" methodologies used to justify incentives are prone to a positive bias (firms
asked whether an incentive was "needed" have an incentive to say yes regardless). A companion,
independently real working paper — **"'But For' Percentages for Economic Development Incentives:
What Percentage Estimates are Plausible Based on the Research Literature?"** (Upjohn Institute
working paper #289, `research.upjohn.org/up_workingpapers/289/`, URL resolves, HTTP 200) —
estimates only 2-25% of incentivized firms would not have located/expanded there anyway, i.e. most
claimed "created jobs" would likely have existed regardless of the incentive.

**Why this is a stronger fit for this paper's core framing than either existing greenwashing
anchor:** Delmas & Burbano (2011) and Janssen/Swaen/Du (2022) are about *environmental* claims.
This project's other central claim type — Musk's round "10,000 exciting jobs" vs. LED's specific
"3,000 direct + 8,100 indirect," and Cameron County/SpaceX's self-reported "$13B economic output/
24,000 jobs" figures already in the Boca Chica corpus (row 7b) — is an *economic-benefit* claim,
governed by a distinct, decades-old public-finance/economic-geography literature on whether
incentive-linked job/investment promises are independently verifiable at all, separate from whether
they're specific-sounding. Bartik's broader research program is the standard citable anchor for "a
government or company's own claimed job-creation numbers from an incentive/investment deal are
usually unaudited and often inflated" — directly on-point for both the LED/Vermilion Parish side and
the Cameron County/Starbase-impact-report side of this corpus, and notably *not* a paper about
communication/framing at all, which is a useful methodological complement (an economics literature
converging on the same skepticism as the marketing/CSR-communication literature, from a completely
different discipline and method).

directly fetchable — `research.upjohn.org` resolved HTTP 200 tonight — just not attempted this
