## 1. Delmas & Burbano (2011) — the standard greenwashing anchor, previously missing entirely

**Delmas, M. A., & Burbano, V. C. (2011). The Drivers of Greenwashing. *California Management
Review*, 54(1), 64–87.** https://doi.org/10.1525/cmr.2011.54.1.64 — **PRIMARY TIER.** Full text
read directly (open-access PDF from UCLA's Institute of the Environment and Sustainability;
this environment's normal PDF pipeline is broken — `pdftoppm`/poppler missing — but `pymupdf`,
installed fresh this session, extracted clean text where the Read tool and pdftotext both
failed. Worth flagging to other project threads hitting the same poppler gap this week.)

This is the standard, most-cited greenwashing-definition paper and it was surprising it wasn't
already an anchor here. Their own definition, quoted directly: **"the intersection of two firm
behaviors: poor environmental performance and positive communication about environmental
performance."** Framework: three levels of drivers — external (regulatory + market pressure),
organizational, and individual. Regulatory looseness is named as the single biggest external
driver, because greenwashing thrives specifically where enforcement is weak or ambiguous.

**Why this matters directly:** SpaceX's situation is close to a textbook case for this
framework's external-driver argument — voluntary environmental commitments (habitat/hydrology
coordination with CPRA) running in parallel with an active push to remove the regulatory
mechanism (FAA environmental review) that would otherwise verify those commitments. Delmas &
Burbano's own logic predicts this is exactly the environment (weak/removed enforcement) where
the communication-performance gap they define as greenwashing is most likely to widen, not
where voluntary commitment should be taken as self-evidently sufficient. This should be a core
anchor, not a supporting citation.

## 3. CSR "aspirational talk" literature — direct counterpoint to Kudłak, worth citing together

**Christensen, L. T., Morsing, M., & Thyssen, O. (2013). CSR as aspirational talk.
*Organization*, 20(3), 372–393.** https://doi.org/10.1177/1350508413478310 — confirmed via
Sage Journals' own DOI page (bibliographic detail only, abstract-level, not full-text read).

**Schoeneborn, D., Morsing, M., & Crane, A. (2020). Formative Perspectives on the Relation
Between CSR Communication and CSR Practices: Pathways for Walking, Talking, and T(w)alking.
*Business & Society*, 59(1), 5–19.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0007650319845091 — confirmed via
DOI.

Both argue, against the conventional "talk-action gap = hypocrisy" framing, that CSR
communication ahead of verified action isn't automatically bad-faith — aspirational talk can be
a legitimate driver of future action, not just cover for its absence. **Why this matters:**
paired with Kudłak (2025) and Delmas & Burbano (2011), this gives the manuscript three
distinct, real theoretical positions on the same underlying question (SpaceX's voluntary
environmental language running ahead of, or alongside the removal of, regulatory
verification) rather than one — genuine literature triangulation instead of picking the one
citation that fits a pre-decided narrative. Whichever framing Britton locks, the manuscript
should engage this literature's more charitable reading directly rather than only citing
sources that support a critical framing.

## 4. FAA environmental-review waiver — no dedicated academic literature exists yet; general regulatory-capture theory as the anchor instead

Searched directly for academic (not news/legal-blog) treatment of this specific 2026 FAA
proposal — found none, and that's an honest finding, not a search failure: the underlying
policy (implementing Executive Order 14335, "Enabling Competition in the Commercial Space
Industry") is itself only from July 2026, too recent for a peer-reviewed literature to exist
yet. Real primary-source documentation of the proposal itself is solid, though, if useful for
the manuscript's factual grounding (not literature, but worth having in one place):
- Holland & Knight's legal analysis: https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/cleared-for-launch-faa-proposes-sweeping-environmental-waivers
- Morgan Lewis's analysis of the same FAA/FCC actions: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/09/faa-and-fcc-actions-signal-narrower-reviews-for-commercial-space-activities
- The actual opposition comment letter (Louisiana Wildlife Federation, National Wildlife
  Federation, Pontchartrain Conservancy, filed 2026-08-24) — a real primary source, not a news
  summary: https://lawildlifefed.org/wp-content/uploads/FAA-Comment-Letter-8-24-2026-LWF_NWF_PC.pdf
- Skift's list of the 13 specific federal environmental laws the waiver would cover (NEPA,
  Endangered Species Act, Clean Water Act, among others): https://skift.com/2026/08/12/these-13-environmental-laws-could-be-waived-to-speed-up-airport-to-spaceport-conversions/

For the theoretical anchor itself, absent waiver-specific literature: **Carpenter, D., & Moss,
D. A. (Eds.). (2013). *Preventing Regulatory Capture: Special Interest Influence and How to
Limit It*. Cambridge University Press.** Their definition (confirmed via multiple independent
listings of the book's own introduction, incl. the Tobin Project's own posted PDF excerpt —
book-length source, not independently full-text-verified this pass): regulatory capture is
"the result or process by which regulation... is consistently or repeatedly directed away from
the public interest and toward the interests of the regulated industry, by the intent and
action of the industry itself." This is the standard general anchor if the manuscript wants a
citable theoretical frame for the venue-shifting/regulatory-erosion thread beyond Coen et al.
(2020)'s venue-shopping empirics already in the first-pass note — it's a genuine extension to
apply it to this specific waiver, not a replication of prior capture-theory work on spaceports.

## 5. Boca Chica academic (non-journalism) work — the original "no academic treatment" claim survives, with real nuance

The 2026-09-09 note's claim that no marketing/PLS-SEM treatment of Boca Chica's
claim-vs-outcome gap exists still holds — but a deeper search finds real, named, **in-progress**
academic work in adjacent disciplines that the first pass didn't surface:

- **Zoraya Berlanga Aguilar**, PhD researcher, Texas A&M Sociology, UCLA-funded Environment and
  Sustainability Initiative Graduate Fellow (awarded 2025-04-24). Project title, directly
  relevant to this paper's core framing: **"The Silent Cost of Space Exploration: How Corporate
  Power and Economic Promises Shape Community Responses to Environmental Harm."** Case study is
  Boca Chica/Brownsville/Port Isabel; resident interviews + secondary-source analysis (local
  news, public meetings); core question is *why communities don't oppose* corporate
  environmental harm despite documented pollution/ecosystem effects — a genuine environmental-
  sociology treatment of the exact claim-vs-outcome gap this project cares about. **No
  publication found yet** — fellowship-stage, not a finished paper. Source:
  https://artsci.tamu.edu/sociology/updates/berlanga-esi-fellow.html
- **Jorge Palacios**, PhD candidate, MIT HASTS (History, Anthropology, and Science, Technology
  and Society). Dissertation-stage research on what commercial spaceflight "along the
  U.S.–Mexico border" means, using anthropology/critical geography/feminist STS/visual
  ethnography. Broader lens (borderlands, Mars-colonization speculative futures) than this
  project's specific claim-specificity focus, but genuinely academic and in-progress. Source:
  https://hasts.mit.edu/?p=2680

**Net effect on the "open lane" claim**: still true that no *marketing* treatment exists, and
still true nothing is *published* yet — but not true that this is an unstudied space in
academia generally. Two real doctoral projects are actively working this exact case from
sociology/STS angles. Worth knowing both names in case either publishes before this
manuscript does, and worth being precise in the manuscript's own literature review that the gap
is disciplinary (no marketing/consumer-behavior treatment), not that Boca Chica is
academically unstudied.
